## Summary

The Third Council of Toledo (589), at which the Visigothic kingdom under King Reccared renounced Arianism, is the earliest council at which the words 'and the Son' (Filioque) are found introduced into the Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed: the newly converted Goths were required to sign the creed with the addition, so that the Spirit is confessed as proceeding from the Father 'and the Son'. (D) This is a later Western formulation about the Spirit's procession, not a statement of any biblical text; the council itself appears to have believed it was reciting the unaltered creed of Constantinople. The addition later became a central point of East-West dispute.

## Researcher notes

CAVEAT: the transmitted acts are disputed — some scholarship holds the Filioque in the Toledo III acts may be a later interpolation. Schaff argues the council itself had no suspicion it was using anything but the unaltered Constantinopolitan creed, and that 589 is the earliest date at which the words are found in the Creed. The milestone is therefore recorded with its verification record marked partially-verified.
