## Summary

The Council of Florence (1439), in the decree of union Laetentur Caeli, defined that 'the holy Spirit is eternally from the Father and the Son, and has his essence and his subsistent being from the Father together with the Son, and proceeds from both eternally as from one principle and a single spiration', and that the addition of 'and from the Son' (Filioque) to the creed was licitly and reasonably added. It also explained the Greek phrase 'through the Son' as meaning the Son is, like the Father, cause/principle of the Spirit's subsistence. (D) This is a fifteenth-century conciliar formulation of the filioque, not a statement of any biblical text; the Eastern churches rejected the union, and the disagreement is recorded, not harmonised.

## Researcher notes

The decree states that 'texts were produced from divine scriptures' in the debate, but the checked text names no specific biblical passage by reference, so no passage is listed in passageIds; do not pad. The union was rejected in the East and did not hold; Mark of Ephesus refused to sign. The decree's definition is recorded here as the Latin West's position, with the Eastern disagreement noted in the summary.
