## Summary

The Fourth Lateran Council (1215), in its first constitution Firmiter credimus, gave the classic medieval Latin profession of the Trinity: 'one true God, eternal and immense, omnipotent, unchangeable, incomprehensible, and ineffable, Father, Son, and Holy Ghost; three Persons indeed but one essence, substance, or nature absolutely simple; the Father proceeding from no one, but the Son from the Father only, and the Holy Ghost equally from both, always without beginning and end.' (D) This is a thirteenth-century conciliar formulation — including the Latin 'equally from both' (filioque) — not a statement of any biblical text. Canon 2, against Joachim of Fiore, defended Peter Lombard's account of the one essence against the charge of quaternity.

## Researcher notes

Canon 2 also condemns Abbot Joachim's tract against Peter Lombard and confesses with Lombard that there is one supreme entity which is truly Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, 'three persons and each one of them singly', so that in God there is only Trinity, not quaternity. Canon 2 quotes 1 John 5:7-8 (including the Johannine Comma) and John 10:29 and John 17:22 in the course of its argument; only 1john-5-7 is listed in passageIds, because it is the only one of the three that is in the 60-passage inventory (John 10:29 and John 17:22 are not).
