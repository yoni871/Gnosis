## Summary

(D) The Arian controversy (c. 318-381) was the dispute over whether the Son is eternal God of the Father's essence or a creature made out of nothing who 'once was not'. Arius taught the latter in his Thalia; Athanasius defended the former; the Council of Nicaea (325) condemned Arius, but the dispute continued for over fifty years until the Council of Constantinople (381) reaffirmed the Nicene faith. John 1:1 was the central text: both sides appealed to 'In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was God', Arius reading 'beginning' as the Word's creation, Athanasius reading it as his eternity. (A) The verse itself states neither the Arian nor the Nicene formulation.

## Notes

The range 318-381 is the conventional span for the controversy as a whole, not the duration of any single dispute or council. Intermediate phases (e.g. the councils of the 350s, the exile of Athanasius and Hilary) are not separately recorded here.
