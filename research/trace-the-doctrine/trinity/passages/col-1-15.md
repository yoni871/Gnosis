# Colossians 1:15-20 (col-1-15)

**Reference:** Colossians 1:15-20 — canonical position 51 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: Colossians 1:15-20 is classified indirect because it describes the Son as the image of God with the fullness dwelling in him without stating his deity in so many words, so it supports the doctrine by implication rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: Christ is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation; by him all things were created, in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible; he is before all things, and in him all things hold together; he is the head of the body, the church, the firstborn from the dead, that in everything he might be preeminent; and in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from the imaging of God, the creation of all things through him, and the fullness dwelling in him that Christ shares the divine being, and may read the passage as consistent with the later doctrine. But the passage states the Son's imaging of God and the fullness dwelling in him by description; it does not state in so many words that Christ is God, and “firstborn” is contested, so deity is inferred from the description rather than stated as a formula. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage supports the doctrine by implication.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does “firstborn of all creation” mean pre-eminence over creation, or that Christ is himself a created being?
- Does “the image of the invisible God” state Christ's deity, or only that Christ reveals the unseen God?
- What does “all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell” imply: that deity dwells in Christ, or that God's fullness is present in him for reconciliation?

## Cross-references

- phil-2-5: Both passages describe the Son's divine status, Philippians by form and equality, Colossians by image and fullness.
- col-2-9: Colossians 2:9 states explicitly what 1:15-20 describes: the fullness of deity dwelling in Christ.
- john-1-1: Both passages bear on the Word's relation to God in creation.

## Textual-variant and uncertainty flags

Uncertainty flag: “firstborn of all creation” is contested — pre-eminence over creation versus created status; recorded as an open question, not resolved.
