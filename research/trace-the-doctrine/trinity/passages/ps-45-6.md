# Psalm 45:6-7 — Research Note

- **Reference:** Psalm 45:6-7 (canonical position 19, Protestant canon)
- **Doctrine:** trinity
- **Classification:** historically-interpreted
- **Justification:** The king is addressed as "O God", but the referent of the elohim vocative is debated, and verse 7 distinguishes the addressee from "God, your God". The trinitarian weight comes from later application (Hebrews 1:8), not from the psalm's own statement. The dispute is recorded, not smoothed over.
- **Status:** verification-pending

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

- **(A) What the text states:** Addressing the king, "Your throne, O God, is forever and ever," and continues, "Therefore God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness beyond your companions." The psalm addresses a human king with the word "God" while also distinguishing him from "God, your God".
- **(B) Inference (labelled as inference):** A reader may reason that addressing the king as God is consistent with the later doctrine's claim that the Son is divine, but the psalm's own referent is disputed and that is an inference, not a statement of the text.
- **(C) Historical reading:** Later writers applied these verses to Christ, reading the "O God" address as divine; that application is reception history, to be attributed in Phase 3, and the dispute over the referent must be recorded, not smoothed over. No specific writer or quotation is recorded here.
- **(D) Later formulation:** The formulated doctrine of the Son's deity was articulated in later centuries and must not be projected back onto this wedding song.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Who is addressed as "O God" in verse 6 — the king himself, God as the source of the throne, or a figure beyond the king?
- Alternative: "elohim" here may be a royal honorific or an address to God about the king, rather than a divine title for the king; how is that alternative weighed?
- How does verse 7's "God, your God, has anointed you" affect the reading of verse 6 — does it distinguish the addressee from God?
- What would the psalm need to say for the "O God" address to state the king's deity directly?
