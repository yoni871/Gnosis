# Colossians 2:9 (col-2-9)

**Reference:** Colossians 2:9 — canonical position 51 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: direct

Justification: Colossians 2:9 is classified direct because it explicitly states that the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily in Christ, so it states an element of the doctrine.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: in Christ the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily. The verse explicitly states that deity, in its whole fullness, dwells in Christ in bodily form. That is a direct statement of the Son's deity, an element of the later doctrine, and it meets the standard's test for direct evidence of that element. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from the fullness of deity dwelling bodily in Christ that Christ shares the divine being, and may read the verse as direct evidence for the Son's deity. But the verse does not state a doctrine of three persons in one God, and it does not name the Father or the Spirit; the step from this statement to the formulated Trinity remains an inference, not the text's statement. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The verse is direct evidence for the Son's deity only.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does “the whole fullness of deity” mean the complete divine nature, or the totality of God's attributes and powers present in Christ?
- Does “dwells bodily” locate deity in Christ's incarnate body, or in Christ as the embodied fullness of God?
- How does the warning against philosophy and empty deceit in verse 8 shape the force of verse 9?

## Cross-references

- col-1-15: Colossians 1:15-20 describes the fullness dwelling in Christ; 2:9 states it explicitly.
- phil-2-5: Both passages state Christ's divine status, Philippians by equality with God and Colossians by the fullness of deity.
- rom-9-5: Both passages are cited for deity ascribed to Christ; Colossians 2:9 states it, Romans 9:5 only on a disputed reading.
