# Deuteronomy 6:4 — Research Note

- **Reference:** Deuteronomy 6:4 (canonical position 5, Protestant canon)
- **Doctrine:** trinity
- **Classification:** indirect
- **Justification:** The Shema explicitly states God's oneness — the foundational premise of the doctrine — but says nothing about threeness, distinct persons, or Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. It supports the doctrine by supplying its "one God" half; the "three persons" half must come from elsewhere.
- **Status:** verification-pending

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

- **(A) What the text states:** "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one." It explicitly affirms the oneness of the LORD, Israel's God.
- **(B) Inference (labelled as inference):** This oneness is the foundational premise on which the later doctrine of the Trinity is built: the doctrine claims one God in three persons, so the Shema supplies the "one God" half of that claim and is consistent with — indeed required by — the doctrine, while the "three persons" half must come from elsewhere. That supporting role is a matter of reasoning from the text, not a statement of the text.
- **(C) Historical reading:** The verse has no trinitarian reading to record as reception history; its relevance is as the doctrine's monotheistic ground.
- **(D) Later formulation:** The formulated doctrine, which holds divine oneness together with three persons, was articulated in later centuries and must not be projected back onto this verse.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- What does "one" affirm here — numerical oneness, uniqueness, or exclusive loyalty — and does the verse rule out internal plurality?
- Alternative: the verse is sometimes read as a polemic against polytheism only, not as a statement about God's inner being; how is that alternative weighed?
- How does the ESV footnote's alternative rendering ("the LORD is our God, the LORD alone") affect the verse's meaning?
- Does the Shema's oneness support, constrain, or stand neutral toward the later doctrine of three persons in one God?

## Cross-references

- deut-32-6 — both Deuteronomy passages ground the doctrine: the Shema states God's oneness and Deuteronomy 32:6 calls God father.
- 1cor-8-6 — 1 Corinthians 8:6 reworks the Shema's oneness around one God, the Father, and one Lord, Jesus Christ.
- eph-4-4 — Ephesians 4:4-6 affirms one Spirit, one Lord, and one God and Father, echoing the Shema's oneness.

## Textual-variant / uncertainty flags
