## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states: 'This Jesus God raised up' (v. 32), and 'Being therefore exalted at the right hand of God, and having received from the Father the promise of the Holy Spirit, he has poured out this that you yourselves are seeing and hearing' (v. 33). Father, exalted Son, and Spirit appear in one act of outpouring. (B) Inference: that this joint action reveals the three as distinct divine persons in one Godhead is an inference; the passage states the outpouring and exaltation, not a trinitarian formula. (C) Later writers read this Pentecost outpouring in a trinitarian light; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine came later and is not stated here. The passage supports the doctrine by implication: it is consistent with the three acting together in salvation and points toward their shared divine work, without stating the doctrine.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does the joint action of Father, exalted Son, and Spirit in one outpouring state a trinitarian pattern, or only three actors in one event?
- Does 'received from the Father the promise of the Holy Spirit' state the Spirit's distinct personhood, or only a promised gift?
- Is 'right hand of God' a statement of the Son's divine status, or of exalted honour?
