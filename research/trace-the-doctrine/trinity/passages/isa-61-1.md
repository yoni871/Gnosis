## Classification and justification

Classified `indirect`. The text does not explicitly state the doctrinal content, but it bears on the doctrine by a pattern it establishes, without stating it (RESEARCH-STANDARDS §1.2).

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: 'The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me, because the LORD has anointed me,' and the speaker is sent to bring good news, bind up the brokenhearted, and proclaim liberty. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: the verse distinguishes the Lord, his Spirit, and the anointed speaker, a pattern consistent with, and supportive of, the doctrine; but it does not name the speaker, does not identify him with the Son, and does not state that the Spirit is a distinct divine person, so the trinitarian connection is an inference from the pattern. (C) Later readers applied the verse to Jesus, who applies it to himself in the Gospels; that application is reception, not the oracle's own statement, and Phase 3 will attribute specific readings. (D) The later trinitarian formulation must not be projected back onto the oracle. The passage is therefore indirect.

No historical writer is named and no quotation is given here: attributed readings are Phase 3 work. The later trinitarian formulation (D) is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Who is the speaker ('me') in verse 1 — the prophet, the Servant, or another figure — and does the verse itself say?
- Does 'the Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me' state the Spirit's distinct personhood, or only the Spirit's empowering presence on the speaker?
- Alternative: the verse may be read as the prophet's own commission, with the Spirit as God's power rather than a distinct person — what in the wording supports or resists that reading?
- Is the Lord–Spirit–speaker pattern enough to count as pointing toward the doctrine, or is it only consistent with it?

## Cross-references

- `isa-48-16` — Isaiah 48:16 shows the same pattern — the Lord, his Spirit, and a sent speaker — in a neighbouring oracle.
- `matt-3-16` — At Jesus' baptism the Spirit descends and remains on him, echoing the anointing described in Isaiah 61:1.
- `john-14-16` — John 14:16 speaks of another Helper, the Spirit, sent by the Father; compare the Spirit-upon-the-anointed pattern here.

## Textual-variant and uncertainty flags

Uncertainty: the speaker is not identified in the verse itself. Textual flag: ESV footnote records that the Septuagint reads 'recovery of sight to the blind' where the ESV has 'the opening of the prison to those who are bound.' The verse does not name the Holy Spirit as a distinct person.
