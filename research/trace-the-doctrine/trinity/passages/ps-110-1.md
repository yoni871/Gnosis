# Psalm 110:1 — Research Note

- **Reference:** Psalm 110:1 (canonical position 19, Protestant canon)
- **Doctrine:** trinity
- **Classification:** historically-interpreted
- **Justification:** "The LORD says to my Lord" presents two lords, but the psalm does not identify the second lord, does not call him divine in so many words, and does not name the Holy Spirit. The divine reading of the second lord is later interpretation, not the psalm's own statement.
- **Status:** verification-pending

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

- **(A) What the text states:** "The LORD says to my Lord: 'Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies your footstool.'" One LORD speaks to a second "Lord" who is enthroned at his right hand.
- **(B) Inference (labelled as inference):** A reader may reason that two figures sharing the divine throne is consistent with a distinction of persons within God, but the psalm's own frame is a royal oracle to a Davidic king, and that is an inference, not a statement of the text.
- **(C) Historical reading:** Later writers read the second lord as the divine Son; that reading is reception history, to be attributed in Phase 3, and must not be presented as the psalm's plain meaning. No specific writer or quotation is recorded here.
- **(D) Later formulation:** The doctrine of the Trinity, including the Son's session at the Father's right hand, was formulated in later centuries and must not be read back into this psalm.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Who is "my Lord" — the Davidic king, a future messianic figure, or someone else — and does the psalm identify him?
- Alternative: the second lord may be a human king enthroned by the LORD, with no divine status implied; how is that alternative weighed?
- What does sitting "at my right hand" signify in the psalm's royal context — shared rule, honour, or shared divinity?
- Does the psalm itself state the second lord's deity, or does that reading depend on later interpretation?

## Cross-references
