# Psalm 2:7 — Research Note

- **Reference:** Psalm 2:7 (canonical position 19, Protestant canon)
- **Doctrine:** trinity
- **Classification:** historically-interpreted
- **Justification:** A royal psalm: the LORD decrees "You are my Son" to the Davidic king. The verse does not name Jesus, does not state that the Son is divine, and does not name the Holy Spirit. Application to Christ's divine sonship is later (NT) reception, not the psalm's own statement.
- **Status:** verification-pending

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

- **(A) What the text states:** As a royal decree, "The LORD said to me, 'You are my Son; today I have begotten you.'" The speaker is the king, addressed by the LORD as his son, in a psalm about the LORD's anointed king.
- **(B) Inference (labelled as inference):** A reader may reason that the father-son language is consistent with the later doctrine's claim of an eternal Son, but the psalm's own frame is the Davidic king's adoption or installation, and that is an inference, not a statement of the text.
- **(C) Historical reading:** Later writers applied this verse to Christ's divine sonship; that application is reception history, to be attributed in Phase 3, and must not be presented as the psalm's plain meaning. No specific writer or quotation is recorded here.
- **(D) Later formulation:** The doctrine of the eternal Son and the Trinity was formulated in later centuries and must not be read back into this royal psalm.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Who is the "me" addressed as Son — the Davidic king at his enthronement, Israel, or a figure beyond the king?
- Alternative: "today I have begotten you" may mark the king's installation rather than an eternal begetting; how is that alternative weighed?
- Does the psalm's own context give any indication that the Son is divine, or does that reading depend entirely on later application?
- How do the later applications of this verse to Christ relate to the psalm's original royal setting?
