# Titus 2:13 (titus-2-13)

**Reference:** Titus 2:13 — canonical position 56 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: direct

Justification: Titus 2:13 is classified direct because it explicitly ascribes the title God to Jesus Christ as our great God and Savior, so it states an element of the doctrine.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: believers wait for their blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ. On the best-supported grammatical reading, the single article governing “God and Savior” names one person, so the verse explicitly ascribes the title God to Jesus Christ. That ascription is a direct statement of the Son's deity, an element of the later doctrine. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from that ascription that Christ shares the divine being, and may read the verse as direct evidence for the Son's deity. But the two-person reading has also been proposed, and the verse does not state a doctrine of three persons in one God, so the step from this ascription to the formulated Trinity remains an inference, not the text's statement. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The verse is direct evidence for the Son's deity only, on the single-person reading.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Do “God and Savior” under one article name one person, Jesus Christ, or two persons, God the Father and Jesus Christ?
- How does the parallel construction in 2 Peter 1:1 bear on the reading of Titus 2:13?
- Does the phrase “the appearing of the glory” refer to Christ's appearing, or to the appearing of God's glory in Christ?

## Cross-references

- rom-9-5: Both passages ascribe the title God to Jesus Christ, and both readings are contested.
- col-2-9: Both passages state deity in or of Christ explicitly.
- 2pet-1-1: Both passages use the same grammatical pattern joining God and Savior under one article.

## Textual-variant and uncertainty flags

Variant flag: a two-person reading (God the Father and Savior Jesus Christ) has been proposed; the single-person reading is the best-supported grammatical reading; see verification record ver-titus-2-13-variant-001.
