## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states: 'the Helper, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, he will teach you all things' (v. 26). All three are named: the Father sends, the Son names the mission, the Spirit teaches. (B) Inference: that this sending pattern reveals an eternal trinitarian relation is an inference; the verse states a mission - sending and teaching - not an eternal relation or a formula. (C) Later writers read the sending pattern in a trinitarian light; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine came later. This differs from Matthew 28:19, which gives a single triadic formula ('in the name of...'), whereas here the three appear across a statement about the Spirit's work; that is why this passage is indirect, not direct. It points toward the doctrine by pattern, without stating it.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Why is this passage indirect while Matthew 28:19 is a candidate for direct - is the distinction (formula vs. statement about sending) sound?
- Does the Father sending the Spirit 'in my name' state the Spirit's relation to the Son, or only his mission?
- Does 'he will teach you all things' state the Spirit's personhood?
