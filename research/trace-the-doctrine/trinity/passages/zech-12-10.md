## Classification and justification

Classified `historically-interpreted`. The text does not state the doctrinal content; only later writers read it in a trinitarian light. This classification records reception history, not a fact about what the text says (RESEARCH-STANDARDS §1.3).

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: YHWH says he will pour out a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy, 'so that, when they look on me, on him whom they have pierced, they shall mourn for him, as one mourns for an only child.' (B) Inference, labelled as inference: the shift from 'me' (YHWH) to 'him whom they have pierced' is consistent with, and supports, identifying the pierced one with the divine speaker, which points toward the deity of the pierced Son; but the verse never names Jesus or the Son, and never states a trinitarian claim, so the identification is an inference. (C) Later readers applied the verse to the pierced Jesus; that is reception history, and Phase 3 will attribute specific readings. (D) The later trinitarian formulation must not be projected onto the oracle. The passage is therefore historically-interpreted.

No historical writer is named and no quotation is given here: attributed readings are Phase 3 work. The later trinitarian formulation (D) is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Who is the pierced one — is he the same as the speaking YHWH ('me'), or a distinct human figure whom YHWH identifies with himself?
- Does the pronoun shift from 'me' to 'him' indicate a distinction of persons within God, or is it a feature of the oracle's rhetoric?
- Alternative: the verse may be read as mourning for a pierced human leader (or for YHWH's representative) without any claim about the pierced one's deity — what in the wording supports or resists that reading?
- Does 'a spirit of grace' in this verse refer to the Holy Spirit, or to a disposition God gives — and does the verse itself say?

## Cross-references

- `dan-7-13` — Daniel 7:13-14 likewise presents a heavenly figure who is approached and given universal dominion; both visions are later read of the Son.
- `john-20-28` — John 20:28 has Thomas address Jesus as 'My Lord and my God'; contrast with Zechariah 12:10, which never names the pierced one.
- `john-1-1` — John 1:1 states the Word's deity explicitly; contrast with Zechariah 12:10, where the pierced one's identity is inferred from the pronoun shift.

## Textual-variant and uncertainty flags

Uncertainty: the pronoun shift from 'me' (YHWH) to 'him whom they have pierced' is the crux of the passage; alternative readings take the pierced one as a distinct human figure. ESV renders 'a spirit of grace' in lowercase; the verse does not name the Holy Spirit. Phase 3 to attribute specific later readings; none are asserted here.
