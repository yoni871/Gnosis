# Galatians 4:4-7 (gal-4-4)

**Reference:** Galatians 4:4-7 — canonical position 48 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: Galatians 4:4-7 is classified indirect because it describes God sending the Son and then the Spirit of the Son in one sending narrative, so it supports the doctrine by pattern rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: when the fullness of time had come, God sent forth his Son, born of woman, born under the law, to redeem those under the law so that they might receive adoption as sons; and because believers are sons, God has sent the Spirit of his Son into their hearts, crying Abba, Father; so the believer is no longer a slave but a son, and if a son, then an heir through God. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer a trinitarian pattern from the two sendings — God sends the Son, then sends the Spirit of the Son — and may read the passage as consistent with the later doctrine. But the text states the sendings and the resulting adoption; it does not state that Father, Son and Spirit are three persons in one God, so the trinitarian synthesis is a pattern-based inference, not the text's statement. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage supports the doctrine by implication.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does “the Spirit of his Son” mean the Spirit who belongs to the Son, the Spirit sent by the Son, or the Spirit who is the Son's own spirit?
- Does the double sending imply a distinction of persons between the Son and the Spirit, or only two stages of one divine mission?
- Is the cry Abba, Father the Spirit's cry, or the believers' cry enabled by the Spirit?

## Cross-references

- rom-8-14: Both passages describe adoption through the Spirit, crying Abba, Father, and heirship with Christ.
- rom-1-3: Both passages describe God's Son in relation to the Spirit.
- titus-3-4: Both passages describe salvation as God's act involving the Son and the Holy Spirit.
