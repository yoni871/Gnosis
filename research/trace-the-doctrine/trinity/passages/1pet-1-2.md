# 1 Peter 1:2

- **Reference:** 1 Peter 1:2 (canonical position 60)
- **Classification:** indirect
- **Justification:** All three are named together in one act of election, but the passage's statement is about election, and it says nothing about the three persons' relation to one another. The triadic pattern supports the doctrine by implication, not by formula.
- **Relationship (A–D):** (A) The text states believers are elect "according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, in the sanctification of the Spirit, for obedience to Jesus Christ and for sprinkling with his blood". (B) Inference, labelled as inference: the joint naming of the three in a single act of election is consistent with and supports the later doctrine that Father, Son, and Spirit are distinct persons acting together in salvation. (C) How later writers read the passage is a matter for Phase 3; no historical reading is asserted here. (D) The later formulation of the Trinity is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto the verse as its explicit teaching.
- **Interpretive questions and alternatives:**
  - Does the verse present three distinct agents in election, or is the pattern a greeting formula without doctrinal intent about their relation?
  - Alternative: the verse assigns roles (foreknowledge to the Father, sanctification to the Spirit, obedience to Christ) without asserting anything about their relation to one another.
  - Does "God the Father" here name the Father specifically as distinct from the Son, and does that distinction bear on the doctrine?
