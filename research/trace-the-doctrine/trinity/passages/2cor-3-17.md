# 2 Corinthians 3:17-18 (2cor-3-17)

**Reference:** 2 Corinthians 3:17-18 — canonical position 47 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: 2 Corinthians 3:17-18 is classified indirect because its identification language, the Lord is the Spirit, is heavily debated and does not state the Trinity, so it supports the doctrine by implication rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: the Lord is the Spirit, and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom; believers with unveiled face behold the glory of the Lord and are transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another, which comes from the Lord who is the Spirit. The identification language is explicit, but its sense is heavily debated. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader might infer from “the Lord is the Spirit” that Christ and the Spirit are identified, or instead that the Spirit is the mode of the Lord's presence; both are inferences, and the record does not harmonise them or choose between them. The text does not state a doctrine of three persons in one God, so whatever its sense, it supports the doctrine only by implication. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage is classified indirect, and the alternatives must be kept open.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does “the Lord is the Spirit” identify Christ with the Holy Spirit, or speak of the Spirit as the Lord's presence and agency?
- Who is “the Lord” in this context: Christ, God, or the Spirit of the Lord of the preceding verses?
- Does the transformation “from the Lord who is the Spirit” imply the Spirit's deity, or only the Spirit's transforming work?

## Cross-references

- rom-8-9: Both passages describe the Spirit as the Spirit of the Lord or of Christ dwelling and working in believers.
- john-14-16: Both passages bear on the Spirit's relation to the Lord who sends or is present through the Spirit.
- 2cor-13-14: Both passages name the Lord and the Holy Spirit together, in a blessing and in an identification statement.

## Textual-variant and uncertainty flags

Uncertainty flag: the sense of “the Lord is the Spirit” is heavily debated; alternatives are recorded and not harmonised.
