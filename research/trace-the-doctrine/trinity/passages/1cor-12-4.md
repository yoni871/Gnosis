# 1 Corinthians 12:4-6 (1cor-12-4)

**Reference:** 1 Corinthians 12:4-6 — canonical position 46 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: 1 Corinthians 12:4-6 is classified indirect because its triadic distribution of gifts names Spirit, Lord, and God in a pattern, so it supports the doctrine by implication rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: there are varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit; varieties of service, but the same Lord; varieties of activities, but the same God who empowers them all in everyone. The passage's statement is about the distribution of gifts, services and activities in the church, attributed in turn to Spirit, Lord and God. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from the triadic pattern — the same Spirit, the same Lord, the same God — a coordinated divine agency behind the church's gifts, and may read the passage as consistent with the later doctrine. But the text does not state that Spirit, Lord and God are three persons in one God, nor does it identify the Lord with Jesus explicitly here; the triadic pattern supports the doctrine by implication, not by stating it. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage supports the doctrine by implication.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does the triadic pattern reflect a deliberate theological statement, or is it a rhetorical distribution of the one divine work?
- Is “the same Lord” here Jesus Christ, and how far does the immediate context support that identification?
- Does the passage distinguish three agents, or describe one God acting in three ways?

## Cross-references

- eph-4-4: Both passages name Spirit, Lord, and God in a threefold unity pattern.
- 2cor-13-14: Both passages name all three together; 2 Corinthians states the triad as a blessing, 1 Corinthians as a distribution of gifts.
- eph-2-18: Both passages describe one divine agency reaching believers through Christ and the Spirit.
