## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states: 'when the Helper comes, whom I will send to you from the Father, the Spirit of truth, who proceeds from the Father, he will bear witness about me' (v. 26). It states the Spirit's procession from the Father, his sending by the Son, and his witness to the Son. (B) Inference: the doctrinal weight - an eternal relation of procession within the Godhead - is drawn by implication from a statement about the Spirit's mission; the verse states the mission, not the eternal relation. (C) Later writers read 'proceeds from the Father' as the classic procession text; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine, including later debate over procession, came later and is not stated here. The passage supports the doctrine by implication: it is consistent with the Spirit's distinct personhood and relation to Father and Son, and points toward it.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does 'proceeds from the Father' state an eternal procession, or only the Spirit's mission in time?
- Does the Son sending the Spirit 'from the Father' state a relation between Son and Spirit, or only a chain of mission?
- Does the passage state the Spirit's personhood, or could the Spirit be read as an impersonal power?
