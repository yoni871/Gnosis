## Classification and justification

Classified `historically-interpreted`. The text does not state the doctrinal content; only later writers read it in a trinitarian light. This classification records reception history, not a fact about what the text says (RESEARCH-STANDARDS §1.3).

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: from Bethlehem Ephrathah shall come forth one who is to be ruler in Israel, 'whose coming forth is from of old, from ancient days.' (B) Inference, labelled as inference: the ruler's origins 'from of old' are consistent with, and are commonly read as pointing toward, eternal pre-existence, which would support identifying him with the Son; but the wording says only antiquity, not eternity, and may refer to ancient Davidic lineage, so pre-existence does not follow from the text itself. (C) Later readers identified the ruler with Jesus and read 'from of old' as eternal pre-existence; that is reception history, and Phase 3 will attribute specific readings. (D) The later doctrine of the Son's eternal generation must not be projected onto the oracle. The passage is therefore historically-interpreted.

No historical writer is named and no quotation is given here: attributed readings are Phase 3 work. The later trinitarian formulation (D) is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does 'from of old, from ancient days' mean eternal pre-existence, or only great antiquity — for example ancient Davidic lineage?
- How much turns on the translation: ESV 'from of old, from ancient days' against KJV 'from of old, from everlasting'?
- Alternative: the verse may promise a ruler whose lineage is ancient (Davidic) without any claim about his pre-existence — what evidence decides between these readings?
- What exactly did later readers claim about this verse, and where — to be established with attributed quotations in Phase 3?

## Cross-references

- `isa-9-6` — Isaiah 9:6 likewise promises a child who will rule; both oracles are later read of the same promised figure.
- `matt-1-18` — Matthew 1:18 begins the account of Jesus' birth; the Bethlehem ruler of Micah 5:2 is later identified with him.
- `john-1-1` — John 1:1 states the Word's existence 'in the beginning' explicitly; contrast with Micah 5:2, which speaks only of ancient origins.

## Textual-variant and uncertainty flags

Verse-numbering flag: this verse is Micah 5:1 in the Hebrew text and in some editions; the ESV numbers it 5:2. Translation flag: ESV 'from of old, from ancient days'; KJV renders 'from of old, from everlasting.' Whether the verse claims eternity or only antiquity turns on this wording and is unresolved here.
