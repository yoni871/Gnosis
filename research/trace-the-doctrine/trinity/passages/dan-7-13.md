## Classification and justification

Classified `historically-interpreted`. The text does not state the doctrinal content; only later writers read it in a trinitarian light. This classification records reception history, not a fact about what the text says (RESEARCH-STANDARDS §1.3).

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: in a night vision, 'one like a son of man' comes with the clouds of heaven to the Ancient of Days, is presented before him, and is given dominion, glory, and a kingdom, with all peoples serving him in an everlasting dominion. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: the figure's heavenly coming, universal service, and everlasting dominion are consistent with a divine Son distinct from yet equal with the Ancient of Days, and support that reading; but the text calls him only 'one like a son of man,' never names him the Son, and never states his deity, so the divine reading is an inference. (C) Later readers, above all Jesus' own self-application of the title, read the figure as the Son; that is reception history, and Phase 3 will attribute specific readings. (D) The later trinitarian formulation must not be projected onto the vision. The passage is therefore historically-interpreted.

No historical writer is named and no quotation is given here: attributed readings are Phase 3 work. The later trinitarian formulation (D) is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Is the 'one like a son of man' an individual heavenly figure, or a collective figure representing the saints of the Most High?
- Does the universal service given to the figure ('all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him') imply his deity, or only his delegated kingship?
- Alternative: the vision may be read as a court scene in which a human-like figure receives delegated dominion, with no claim about his divine nature — what in the wording supports or resists that reading?
- What exactly did later readers claim about this vision, and where — to be established with attributed quotations in Phase 3?

## Cross-references

- `ps-110-1` — Psalm 110:1 likewise places a figure enthroned beside the Lord; both passages are later read of the exalted Son.
- `john-1-1` — John 1:1 states the Word's deity explicitly; contrast with Daniel 7, which gives the figure everlasting dominion without stating his deity.
- `phil-2-5` — Philippians 2:5-11 speaks of the one who was in the form of God and was exalted; compare the exaltation of the Son of Man here.

## Textual-variant and uncertainty flags

Uncertainty: 'one like a son of man' is a simile; the figure's identity is disputed (an individual heavenly figure vs. a collective figure for the saints). The verse does not name the figure the Son and does not state his deity. Phase 3 to attribute specific later readings; none are asserted here.
