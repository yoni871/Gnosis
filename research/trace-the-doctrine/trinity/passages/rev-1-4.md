# Revelation 1:4-5

- **Reference:** Revelation 1:4-5 (canonical position 66)
- **Classification:** indirect
- **Justification:** The greeting is triadic in form, but "the seven spirits" is interpreted as the Holy Spirit only by inference; the alternative reading (seven angels/spirits as a symbol) is recorded, so the verse cannot be classed as direct.
- **Relationship (A–D):** (A) The text states: "Grace to you and peace from him who is and who was and who is to come, and from the seven spirits who are before his throne, and from Jesus Christ the faithful witness, the firstborn of the dead, and the ruler of kings on earth". The greeting names three sources of grace and peace. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: if "the seven spirits" denotes the Holy Spirit in sevenfold fullness, the greeting is a triadic pattern consistent with and supporting the later doctrine; but that identification is itself an inference, and the verse does not state it. (C) How later writers read the passage is a matter for Phase 3; no historical reading is asserted here. (D) The later formulation of the Trinity is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto these verses as their explicit teaching.
- **Interpretive questions and alternatives:**
  - Does "the seven spirits who are before his throne" denote the Holy Spirit (sevenfold fullness), or seven angels or spirits as a symbol?
  - Alternative: if the seven spirits are seven angels, the greeting is not triadic in a trinitarian sense.
  - Does Revelation 4:5, where the seven torches of fire "are the seven spirits of God", support identifying the seven spirits with the Holy Spirit?
