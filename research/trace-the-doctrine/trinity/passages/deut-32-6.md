# Deuteronomy 32:6 — Research Note

- **Reference:** Deuteronomy 32:6 (canonical position 5, Protestant canon)
- **Doctrine:** trinity
- **Classification:** indirect
- **Justification:** God is called "your father, who created you" — father language for God as creator and founder of the nation. The verse does not name the Father as a distinct divine person and does not name Son or Spirit. Identifying that father with the first person of the Trinity is an inference, not a statement of the text.
- **Status:** verification-pending

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

- **(A) What the text states:** In the Song of Moses, "Is not he your father, who created you, who made you and established you?" God is called Israel's father in the sense of creator and founder of the nation.
- **(B) Inference (labelled as inference):** A reader may connect this father language with the later doctrine's naming of the first person as "the Father"; the connection is an inference, because the verse's "father" describes God's relation to Israel as creator, not a relation within God.
- **(C) Historical reading:** There is no trinitarian reading of this verse to record as reception history; its bearing on the doctrine is by implication only.
- **(D) Later formulation:** The formulated doctrine, in which "Father" names one person of the Trinity in eternal relation to the Son, was articulated in later centuries and must not be projected back onto this song.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- In what sense is God called "father" here — creator, redeemer, founder of the nation — and does the verse imply a personal relation within God?
- Alternative: the father language may be purely covenantal and national, with no bearing on the doctrine's "Father"; how is that alternative weighed?
- How does the KJV rendering "thy father that hath bought thee" compare with the ESV "your father, who created you", and does the difference matter?
- What step of reasoning connects this verse's father language to the first person of the Trinity, and is that step an inference or a statement?
