## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states Jesus' words: 'before Abraham was, I am' (v. 58). It explicitly states his pre-existence before Abraham. (B) Inference: identifying 'I am' with the divine name of Exodus 3:14 is an inference, and it is contested - the phrase could be read as a claim to pre-existence alone rather than a divine-name claim, so the deity inference must not be presented as the text's statement. (C) Later writers read the verse as a divine-name claim; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine of the Trinity came later and is not stated here. The passage supports the doctrine by implication: it is consistent with the Son's pre-existence and points toward his deity, but the step from pre-existence to deity is an inference, and the Exodus link is a contested inference.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does 'I am' invoke the divine name of Exodus 3:14, or is it only a claim to pre-existence?
- Is the hearers' attempt to stone Jesus (v. 59, outside this range) evidence of how they understood the claim?
- Could 'before Abraham was, I am' be read as pre-existence without a deity claim?
