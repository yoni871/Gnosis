# Titus 3:4-7 (titus-3-4)

**Reference:** Titus 3:4-7 — canonical position 56 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: Titus 3:4-7 is classified indirect because it names God our Savior, the Holy Spirit, and Jesus Christ our Savior in one saving act without stating the Trinity, so it supports the doctrine by implication rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared, he saved believers, not because of works done in righteousness, but according to his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit, whom he poured out richly through Jesus Christ our Savior, so that, being justified by his grace, believers might become heirs according to the hope of eternal life. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from the coordinated act — God our Savior saving, the Holy Spirit renewing, and Jesus Christ our Savior as the one through whom the Spirit is poured out — a trinitarian shape to salvation, and may read the passage as consistent with the later doctrine. But the passage's statement is about salvation; it does not state that Father, Son and Spirit are three persons in one God, so the trinitarian reading is an inference from the pattern, not the text's statement. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage supports the doctrine by implication.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does “God our Savior” in verse 4 refer to the Father, or to God generally, and how does that affect the threefold pattern?
- Does the Spirit's renewal imply the Spirit's deity, or only the Spirit's role in regeneration?
- Is the Spirit poured out by God the Father through Jesus Christ, or by Jesus Christ himself, and what follows from each reading?
