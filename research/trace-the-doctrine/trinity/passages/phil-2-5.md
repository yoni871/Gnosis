# Philippians 2:5-11 (phil-2-5)

**Reference:** Philippians 2:5-11 — canonical position 50 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: direct

Justification: Philippians 2:5-11 is classified direct because it explicitly states Christ's pre-existent divine status and equality with God, so it states an element of the doctrine.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: Christ Jesus, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men, and humbled himself to death on a cross; therefore God highly exalted him and gave him the name above every name, so that every knee should bow and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father. The passage explicitly states Christ's pre-existent divine status and equality with God. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from that stated equality that Christ shares the divine being, and may read the passage as direct evidence for the Son's deity, an element of the later doctrine. But the passage does not state a doctrine of three persons in one God, so the step from Christ's equality with God to the formulated Trinity remains an inference. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage is direct evidence for the Son's deity only.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does “the form of God” mean Christ's divine nature, or his divine status and glory?
- Does “did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped” mean Christ refused to exploit equality, or refused to seize equality he did not have?
- Does the exaltation in verses 9 to 11 imply Christ receives a status he did not previously hold, or a public vindication of a status already held?

## Cross-references

- col-1-15: Both passages describe the Son's divine status and pre-eminence, Philippians by form and equality, Colossians by image and fullness.
- col-2-9: Both passages state deity dwelling in or belonging to Christ.
- john-1-1: Both passages state the Word's or Christ's divine status before the incarnation.

## Textual-variant and uncertainty flags

Interpretive question: “form of God” is read as divine nature by some and as divine status or glory by others; recorded as an open question, not resolved.
