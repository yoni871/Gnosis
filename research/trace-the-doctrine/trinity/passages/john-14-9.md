## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states: 'Whoever has seen me has seen the Father' (v. 9), and 'I am in the Father and the Father is in me' (vv. 10-11). It states mutual indwelling and a revelatory identity between seeing Jesus and seeing the Father. (B) Inference: oneness of essence is an inference from mutual indwelling, not what the words state; the text states indwelling and revelation, and the essence claim goes beyond it. (C) Later writers read this mutual indwelling in a trinitarian light; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine came later and must not be read back into the wording. The passage supports the doctrine by implication: it is consistent with the Father-Son unity and points toward it, but the doctrinal weight rests on the inference from indwelling to shared essence.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does 'seen me has seen the Father' state identity of essence, or revelatory representation?
- Does mutual indwelling ('I am in the Father and the Father is in me') state oneness of essence, or a relational indwelling?
- Could the passage be read as functional unity without essential unity?
