# 2 Corinthians 13:14 (2cor-13-14)

**Reference:** 2 Corinthians 13:14 — canonical position 47 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: direct

Justification: 2 Corinthians 13:14 is classified direct because it names the Lord Jesus Christ, God, and the Holy Spirit together in a single blessing, so it states a triadic element of the doctrine.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with believers. The verse names all three — Lord Jesus Christ, God, and the Holy Spirit — together in a single blessing, each with a distinct gift or relation to believers. That triadic naming in one act meets the standard's test for direct evidence of an element of the doctrine. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from the coordinated naming that the three are presented as jointly active in blessing believers, and may read the verse as consistent with the later doctrine. But the verse does not state that the three are one God, nor does it use later technical language; the move from the triadic blessing to the formulated doctrine is an inference, not the text's statement. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity, with its language of persons and essence, is not stated here and must not be projected back onto the blessing. The verse is direct evidence for the triadic element only.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does the coordinated blessing imply equality among the three named, or only their joint activity toward believers?
- Does “the fellowship of the Holy Spirit” mean fellowship produced by the Spirit, or fellowship with the Spirit?
- Why does the blessing name the Lord Jesus Christ first rather than God the Father?

## Cross-references

- matt-28-19: Both passages name Father, Son, and Holy Spirit together in a single act, a baptismal commission and a blessing.
- 1cor-12-4: Both passages distribute divine activity in a threefold pattern, here as a blessing and there as gifts.
- eph-2-18: Both passages describe believers' relation to all three together, through Christ, in the Spirit, to the Father.
