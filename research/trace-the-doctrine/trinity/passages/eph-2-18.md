# Ephesians 2:18 (eph-2-18)

**Reference:** Ephesians 2:18 — canonical position 49 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: Ephesians 2:18 is classified indirect because it describes access to the Father through Christ in one Spirit without stating the Trinity, so it supports the doctrine by implication rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: through Christ, both Jews and Gentiles have access in one Spirit to the Father. The verse names all three in a single movement of access: through Christ, in the Spirit, to the Father. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from this threefold access a trinitarian shape to the believer's approach to God, and may read the verse as consistent with the later doctrine. But the text states the access itself; it does not state that Father, Son and Spirit are three persons in one God, nor that the Spirit is divine, so the trinitarian reading is an inference from the pattern, not the text's statement. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage supports the doctrine by implication.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does “access in one Spirit to the Father” describe the Spirit as the medium of access, or as a distinct person through whom access comes?
- Does the verse imply the Spirit's deity, or only the Spirit's role in uniting Jews and Gentiles?
- How does the immediate context of reconciliation through the cross shape the verse's trinitarian pattern?

## Cross-references

- eph-4-4: Both passages confess one Spirit, one Lord, and one God and Father in a unified pattern.
- 2cor-13-14: Both passages name all three together, here as access and there as blessing.
- rom-8-14: Both passages describe believers coming to the Father as children through the Spirit.
