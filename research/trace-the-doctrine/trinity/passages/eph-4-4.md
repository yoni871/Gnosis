# Ephesians 4:4-6 (eph-4-4)

**Reference:** Ephesians 4:4-6 — canonical position 49 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: Ephesians 4:4-6 is classified indirect because its triadic unity confession names one Spirit, one Lord, and one God and Father without stating the Trinity, so it supports the doctrine by pattern rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: there is one body and one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all, who is over all and through all and in all. The passage is a unity confession: it states oneness — one body, one Spirit, one Lord, one God and Father — as the ground of the church's unity. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from the threefold naming of Spirit, Lord, and God and Father a trinitarian pattern underlying the church's oneness, and may read the passage as consistent with the later doctrine. But the text states oneness, not three persons in one God; it does not name Jesus explicitly, and it does not state the Spirit's deity, so the threefold naming supports the doctrine by pattern, not by formula. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage supports the doctrine by implication.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Is “one Lord” here Jesus Christ, and how far does the surrounding context support that identification?
- Does the sevenfold “one” pattern aim at unity of the church, or at a statement about God's own being?
- Does naming Spirit, Lord, and God in one confession imply three distinct persons, or three descriptions of one God?

## Cross-references

- 1cor-12-4: Both passages name Spirit, Lord, and God in a threefold pattern, here for unity and there for gifts.
- 1cor-8-6: Both passages confess one God the Father and one Lord in a single confession.
- matt-28-19: Both passages name the three together; Matthew as a baptismal formula and Ephesians as a unity confession.
