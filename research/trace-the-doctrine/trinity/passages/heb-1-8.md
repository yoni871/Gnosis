# Hebrews 1:8

- **Reference:** Hebrews 1:8 (canonical position 58)
- **Classification:** direct
- **Justification:** The Son is explicitly addressed as "O God" ("Your throne, O God, is forever and ever"), which is an explicit statement of the deity of the Son in so many words — the element the classification rule requires for "direct". The verse does not state the formulated doctrine as a whole and does not name the Holy Spirit.
- **Relationship (A–D):** (A) The text states: "But of the Son he says, 'Your throne, O God, is forever and ever'". The passage quotes Psalm 45:6-7 (see cross-reference ps-45-6). (B) Inference, labelled as inference: because the Son is addressed as God, a researcher may infer the Son is divine in the sense the later doctrine affirms. (C) How later writers read the passage is a matter for Phase 3; no historical reading is asserted here. (D) The later formulation of the Trinity is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto the verse as its explicit teaching.
- **Interpretive questions and alternatives:**
  - Is "O God" a vocative addressing the Son as God, or should the line be read as "Your throne is God forever and ever" (an alternative noted in the NET footnote on BibleGateway)?
  - How does verse 9, "therefore God, your God, has anointed you", distinguish the Son addressed as God from God the Father who anoints him?
  - Alternative: since the words quote Psalm 45:6-7, originally addressed to a human king, does the quotation itself ascribe deity to the Son, or does the ascription depend on how Hebrews applies the psalm?
- **Cross-references:** ps-45-6, john-20-28, titus-2-13
