# 2 Peter 1:1

- **Reference:** 2 Peter 1:1 (canonical position 61)
- **Classification:** direct
- **Justification:** On the reading adopted here, "our God and Savior Jesus Christ" explicitly ascribes "God" to Jesus in so many words — the element the classification rule requires for "direct". The verse does not state the formulated doctrine as a whole and does not name the Holy Spirit. The alternative two-person reading is recorded as an interpretive question.
- **Relationship (A–D):** (A) The text states believers have obtained faith "by the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ". On the reading adopted here, "God and Savior" both describe Jesus Christ, so the verse explicitly ascribes "God" to Jesus. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: from the explicit ascription of "God" to Jesus a researcher may infer the Son is divine in the sense the later doctrine affirms. (C) How later writers read the passage is a matter for Phase 3; no historical reading is asserted here. (D) The later formulation of the Trinity is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto the verse as its explicit teaching.
- **Interpretive questions and alternatives:**
  - Does "our God and Savior Jesus Christ" ascribe "God" to Jesus, or does it name two persons ("God and our Savior Jesus Christ"), as the KJV wording can be read?
  - Alternative (two-person reading): the phrase could refer to God the Father and to Jesus Christ as Savior separately; what in the wording decides between the one-person and two-person readings?
  - A small number of witnesses read "Lord" for "God" in verse 1 (NET textual note as shown on BibleGateway); does that variant affect the ascription?

- **Textual-variant / uncertainty flags:** Alternative-reading flag: the KJV wording can be read as naming two persons. Variant note: a small number of witnesses read "Lord" for "God" (NET textual note as shown on BibleGateway); the ESV text checked reads "God".
