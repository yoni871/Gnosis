## Classification and justification

Classified `historically-interpreted`. The text does not state the doctrinal content; only later writers read it in a trinitarian light. This classification records reception history, not a fact about what the text says (RESEARCH-STANDARDS §1.3).

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: Isaiah sees the Lord enthroned in the temple while seraphim call, 'Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts,' and the Lord then asks, 'Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?' (B) Inference, labelled as inference: the threefold 'Holy' and the plural 'us' are consistent with a plurality within the one God, and are commonly read as pointing toward the Trinity; but the wording alone does not name three persons, and the plural may instead be a plural of majesty or a reference to the heavenly court, so no trinitarian claim follows from the text itself. (C) Later writers read this vision in a trinitarian light, including reading it as a vision of Christ's glory; that is reception history, not the oracle's own statement, and Phase 3 will attribute specific readings. (D) The later formulation of one God in three persons was reached centuries after Isaiah and must not be projected back onto the passage. It is therefore historically-interpreted, not direct.

No historical writer is named and no quotation is given here: attributed readings are Phase 3 work. The later trinitarian formulation (D) is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does the threefold 'Holy, holy, holy' in verse 3 indicate a plurality within God, or is it liturgical emphasis (repetition for intensity)?
- Does the plural 'us' in verse 8 ('who will go for us?') indicate a plurality within God, a plural of majesty, or the heavenly court?
- Alternative: the passage may be read as a vision of the one Lord's holiness and commissioning of Isaiah, with no trinitarian content at all — how is that reading weighed against the later trinitarian reception?
- What exactly did later readers claim about this passage, and where — to be established with attributed quotations in Phase 3?

## Textual-variant and uncertainty flags

Uncertainty: the plural 'us' in v.8 may be a plural of majesty or a reference to the heavenly court, and the threefold 'Holy' may be liturgical emphasis; neither states a trinitarian claim. Phase 3 to attribute specific later readings; none are asserted here.
