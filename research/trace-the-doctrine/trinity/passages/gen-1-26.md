# Genesis 1:26 — Research Note

- **Reference:** Genesis 1:26 (canonical position 1, Protestant canon)
- **Doctrine:** trinity
- **Classification:** historically-interpreted
- **Justification:** The worked example in RESEARCH-STANDARDS §1.4. The text uses the plural "let us make" but does not name Father, Son, and Holy Spirit and does not state that God is three persons in one essence. It is NEVER direct. The trinitarian reading is later reception, not textual fact.
- **Status:** verification-pending

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

- **(A) What the text states:** God said, "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness," and then God created man in his own image, male and female. The plural verbs and pronouns ("us", "our") are the text's own wording.
- **(B) Inference (labelled as inference):** A reader may note that the plural is unexplained within the verse and is consistent with a plurality within God, but that consistency does not amount to a statement of threeness.
- **(C) Historical reading:** Later writers read this plural in a trinitarian light; that reading is reception history, to be attributed to named writers with verified quotations in Phase 3, and must not be presented as the text's plain meaning. No specific writer or quotation is recorded here.
- **(D) Later formulation:** The doctrine of the Trinity as one essence in three persons was formulated in later centuries and must not be read back into this verse.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Who is addressed by the plural "let us" — a heavenly court, a plural of majesty, God addressing himself, or something else?
- Alternative: the plural may be a divine council or a majestic plural rather than a hint of plurality within God; how is that alternative weighed?
- Why does verse 27 switch back to the singular ("in his own image") immediately after the plural of verse 26?
- What would the text need to say for the plural to state the Trinity directly, and does it say that?

## Cross-references

- gen-1-1 — the plural of Genesis 1:26 occurs in the same creation account in which the Spirit of God is active.
- gen-18-1 — both passages present a threefold or plural divine appearance that later writers read in a trinitarian light.
- matt-28-19 — Matthew 28:19 names Father, Son, and Holy Spirit together, unlike Genesis 1:26, which names no persons.

## Textual-variant / uncertainty flags

- Uncertainty flagged: the addressee of the plural "let us" is not identified in the text.
