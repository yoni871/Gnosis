## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states Thomas's answer to the risen Jesus: 'My Lord and my God!' (v. 28). It explicitly ascribes the title 'God' to Jesus, addressed to him directly. (B) Inference: that this ascription states the Son's full deity in the later doctrinal sense is a small step, but the words themselves do ascribe deity to the Son; the inference is only in connecting this ascription to the formulated doctrine. (C) Later writers read Thomas's confession as a classic ascription of deity to the Son; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The passage does not state the fully formulated doctrine; it names no third person and uses no later trinitarian vocabulary. It is direct evidence for one element only - the deity of the Son as confessed - and the verifier must confirm that limited scope.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does Jesus' response (v. 29, outside this range) accept the ascription?
