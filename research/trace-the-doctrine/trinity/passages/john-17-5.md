## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states Jesus' prayer: 'glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had with you before the world existed' (v. 5). It explicitly states pre-existent shared glory with the Father. (B) Inference: that pre-existent shared glory entails an eternal relation within the Godhead is an inference; the verse states glory shared before creation, not the nature of that relation. (C) Later writers read this as evidence for the Son's eternal pre-existence with the Father; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine came later and is not stated here. The passage supports the doctrine by implication: it is consistent with the Son's eternal pre-existence alongside the Father and points toward it, but the step from shared glory to trinitarian relation is an inference.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does 'the glory that I had with you before the world existed' state eternal pre-existence, or glory foreseen before creation?
- Does shared glory state shared deity, or only a shared honour?
- Does the prayer distinguish Father and Son as persons, or could it be read as one person addressing himself?
