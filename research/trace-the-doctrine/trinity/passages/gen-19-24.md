# Genesis 19:24 — Research Note

- **Reference:** Genesis 19:24 (canonical position 1, Protestant canon)
- **Doctrine:** trinity
- **Classification:** historically-interpreted
- **Justification:** "The LORD rained... from the LORD" names the LORD twice, but the verse does not distinguish two divine persons, does not name Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and does not state that God is three persons. The trinitarian reading is patristic reception, not the text's own statement.
- **Status:** verification-pending

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

- **(A) What the text states:** The LORD rained on Sodom and Gomorrah sulfur and fire from the LORD out of heaven. The verse names the LORD twice — once as the agent raining down judgment and once as its source in heaven.
- **(B) Inference (labelled as inference):** A reader may reason that the twofold naming is consistent with a distinction of persons within the one God, but that is an inference; the doubling may also be a stylistic repetition, and the text does not resolve the question.
- **(C) Historical reading:** Later writers read the twofold LORD in a trinitarian light; that reading is reception history, to be attributed in Phase 3, and must not be presented as the text's plain meaning. No specific writer or quotation is recorded here.
- **(D) Later formulation:** The doctrine of the Trinity was formulated in later centuries and must not be read back into this verse.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does the doubling of "the LORD" distinguish two agents, or is it a stylistic repetition of a single agent?
- Alternative: the verse may be read as emphatic repetition with no distinction of persons; how is that alternative weighed?
- Who is the LORD "out of heaven" as distinct from the LORD who rains — and does the text say?
- What would the verse need to state for the doubling to count as direct evidence of distinct divine persons?

## Textual-variant / uncertainty flags

- Uncertainty flagged: the twofold "LORD" may be stylistic repetition rather than a distinction of persons.
