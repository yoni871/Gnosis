# Hebrews 9:14

- **Reference:** Hebrews 9:14 (canonical position 58)
- **Classification:** indirect
- **Justification:** Christ, the eternal Spirit, and God appear together in one verse, but the passage's statement is about Christ's offering and its efficacy, not about the doctrine. The triadic pattern supports the doctrine by implication.
- **Relationship (A–D):** (A) The text states that the blood of Christ, "who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without blemish to God", purifies the conscience. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: the grouping of Christ, the eternal Spirit, and God in a single act of offering is consistent with and supports the later doctrine that the three are distinct persons sharing one divine work. (C) How later writers read the passage is a matter for Phase 3; no historical reading is asserted here. (D) The later formulation of the Trinity is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto the verse as its explicit teaching.
- **Interpretive questions and alternatives:**
  - Does "through the eternal Spirit" refer to the Holy Spirit, or to Christ's own eternal spirit by which he offered himself?
  - Is the grouping of Christ, the Spirit, and God a deliberate theological pattern, or incidental to the argument about Christ's offering?
  - Alternative: if "the eternal Spirit" is Christ's own spirit rather than the Holy Spirit, the verse names only two persons.
