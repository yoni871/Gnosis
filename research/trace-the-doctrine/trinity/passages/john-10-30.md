## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states Jesus' words: 'I and the Father are one' (v. 30). It explicitly states a oneness between Jesus ('I') and the Father. (B) Inference: whether that oneness is oneness of essence or oneness of purpose and will is an interpretive question, not settled by the bare wording; reading it as oneness of essence is an inference. (C) Later writers read the verse as a statement of essential unity; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The passage does not state the fully formulated doctrine; it names no third person and uses no later trinitarian vocabulary. It is direct evidence for one element only - the unity of Father and Son as stated - and the alternative reading (purpose/will) must be recorded alongside it. The verifier must confirm this limited direct scope.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.
