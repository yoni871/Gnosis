# Hebrews 1:3

- **Reference:** Hebrews 1:3 (canonical position 58)
- **Classification:** indirect
- **Justification:** The verse describes the Son as "the radiance of the glory of God and the exact imprint of his nature" but does not name the Holy Spirit, does not state the doctrine, and does not use later trinitarian vocabulary. The inference to shared essence is labelled as inference in the relationship text.
- **Relationship (A–D):** (A) The text states the Son is the radiance of God's glory and the exact imprint of his nature, and upholds the universe by the word of his power. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: from "exact imprint of his nature" a researcher may infer the Son shares God's very nature, which is consistent with and supports the later doctrine of one essence. (C) How later writers read the passage is a matter for Phase 3; no historical reading is asserted here. (D) The later formulation of one essence in three persons is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto the verse.
- **Interpretive questions and alternatives:**
  - Does "the exact imprint of his nature" assert identity of nature with God, or only a perfect likeness or representation?
  - Does "radiance of the glory of God" imply derivation from God, equality with God, or both?
  - Alternative: the language could describe the Son as God's perfect representation without stating a shared essence.
