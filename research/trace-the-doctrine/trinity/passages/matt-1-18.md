## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states that Mary was found with child 'from the Holy Spirit' (v. 18, repeated in v. 20), that the child is Jesus, who will save his people, and that he will be called Immanuel, 'which means, God with us' (v. 23). (B) Inference: the Spirit's agency in the conception together with the name Immanuel points toward the child's divine identity and the Spirit's personal agency, but the passage does not state that the Spirit, the Father, and the Son are three persons in one essence. (C) Later writers read this conception narrative in a trinitarian light; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine of the Trinity came later and must not be read back into Matthew's wording. The passage therefore supports the doctrine by implication: it is consistent with, and points toward, the Son's deity and the Spirit's role, without stating the doctrine.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does 'from the Holy Spirit' state the Spirit's personal agency, or only a divine mode of conception?
- Does 'Immanuel' ('God with us') ascribe deity to the child, or is it a titular description of God's presence?
- How far does the passage support the Son's deity without stating the Trinity?
