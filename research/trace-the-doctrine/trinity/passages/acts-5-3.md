## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states Peter's words: Ananias lied 'to the Holy Spirit' (v. 3), and 'You have not lied to man but to God' (v. 4). It explicitly equates the two acts of lying: lying to the Spirit and lying to God. (B) Inference: the Spirit's deity is an inference from that equation - that the one lied to in both descriptions is the same divine person - and must be labelled as inference, not presented as the text's statement; the text states the equation of the acts, not 'the Holy Spirit is God'. (C) Later writers read this equation as evidence for the Spirit's deity; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine came later and is not stated here. The passage supports the doctrine by implication: it is consistent with the Spirit's deity and points toward it, but only through the labelled inference.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does equating lying to the Holy Spirit with lying to God state the Spirit's deity, or only the gravity of the lie?
- Is the inference from the equation to the Spirit's deity sound, or could the same act have two descriptions without identity of persons?
- Does the passage state the Spirit's personhood, or could 'Holy Spirit' here be a metonym for God?
