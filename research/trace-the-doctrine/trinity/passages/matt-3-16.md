## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states one event: Jesus is baptized, the heavens open, 'the Spirit of God' descends like a dove and rests on him, and 'a voice from heaven' says, 'This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased' (vv. 16-17). All three - the Son being baptized, the Spirit descending, and the Father speaking - are present together in a single act. (B) Inference: that their joint presence in one act points toward their distinct personhood and shared divine action is a step beyond what the words state. (C) Later writers read this scene as a trinitarian manifestation; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The passage does not use later trinitarian vocabulary and does not state the fully formulated doctrine. Classification as direct is therefore a candidate only, per standards section 1.1: it rests on the three being named together in one act, and the independent verifier must confirm it.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.
