# Romans 9:5 (rom-9-5)

**Reference:** Romans 9:5 — canonical position 45 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: Romans 9:5 is classified indirect because the reading that applies the words God over all to Christ is disputed on punctuation grounds, so the verse supports the doctrine only on a contested reading and does not state it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states, on the ESV's punctuation: from the Israelites, according to the flesh, is the Christ, who is God over all, blessed forever. On that reading the verse applies the words God over all to Christ. But this reading is DISPUTED, and the dispute is part of the record: the earliest manuscripts lack systematic punctuation, so an alternative reading places a break after the Christ and takes the following words as a separate doxology to God. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: IF the words are read as applying to Christ, a reader may infer that Paul ascribes deity to Christ; but because the punctuation is disputed, that inference is conditional on a contested reading and must not be presented as certain. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage is therefore classified indirect: it is consistent with the doctrine on one reading, and its bearing on the doctrine depends on a disputed punctuation choice.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Which punctuation of the Greek best reflects Paul's intent: a relative clause applying “God over all” to Christ, or a separate doxology to God?
- How much weight should the earliest manuscripts' lack of systematic punctuation carry against the near-unanimous later reading?
- Does Paul elsewhere apply the title God to Christ, and how does that bear on this verse?
- If the doxology reading is adopted, does the verse still bear on the doctrine of Christ's deity at all?
