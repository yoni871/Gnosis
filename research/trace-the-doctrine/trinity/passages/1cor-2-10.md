# 1 Corinthians 2:10-11 (1cor-2-10)

**Reference:** 1 Corinthians 2:10-11 — canonical position 46 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: 1 Corinthians 2:10-11 is classified indirect because it states the Spirit's knowledge of the depths of God without stating the Spirit's deity, so it supports the doctrine by implication rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: God has revealed hidden things to believers through the Spirit; the Spirit searches everything, even the depths of God; and just as no one knows a person's thoughts except that person's own spirit, so no one comprehends the thoughts of God except the Spirit of God. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: from the Spirit's exhaustive knowledge of the depths of God, a reader may infer that the Spirit shares in God's own knowledge and life, and may read the passage as consistent with the Spirit's deity. But the text states the Spirit's knowledge of God; it does not state that the Spirit is God, nor that the Spirit is a distinct divine person, so the Spirit's deity is an inference from that knowledge, not the text's statement. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage supports the doctrine by implication.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does the analogy with a person's own spirit imply the Spirit is God's own inner life rather than a distinct person?
- Does knowing the depths of God require the Spirit to be divine, or could a created spirit know God by revelation?
- How does verse 12, on receiving the Spirit who is from God, affect the reading of the Spirit's relation to God?

## Cross-references

- john-14-26: Both passages describe the Spirit revealing and teaching what belongs to God.
- john-16-13: Both passages describe the Spirit communicating God's truth to believers.
