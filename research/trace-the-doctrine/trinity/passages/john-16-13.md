## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states the Spirit of truth 'will guide you into all the truth,' 'will take what is mine and declare it to you,' and 'All that the Father has is mine' (vv. 13-15). It states shared possession between Father and Son and the Spirit's mediating declaration. (B) Inference: that shared possession and the Spirit's taking-and-declaring reveal an eternal trinitarian relation is an inference; the verses state a revelatory economy, not an eternal relation. (C) Later writers read this shared possession in a trinitarian light; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine came later and is not stated here. The passage supports the doctrine by implication: it is consistent with the unity of Father and Son and the Spirit's personal agency, and points toward their shared divine life, without stating the doctrine.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does 'All that the Father has is mine' state shared divine possession, or shared authority in mission?
- Does the Spirit taking what is Christ's state an eternal relation, or only a revelatory role?
- Does 'he will not speak on his own authority' imply subordination, or a pattern of divine self-giving?
