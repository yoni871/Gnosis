## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states the angel's words: 'The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you; therefore the child to be born will be called holy - the Son of God' (v. 35). Spirit, Most High, and Son of God are named in one announcement. (B) Inference: identifying 'the Most High' with 'the Father' is itself an inference, and reading the three together as the Trinity is a further inference from the pattern, not something the verse states. (C) Later writers read this annunciation in a trinitarian light; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine came later and must not be projected back onto Luke's wording. The passage therefore supports the doctrine by implication: it is consistent with the Spirit's agency and the Son's divine sonship, and points toward a trinitarian pattern, without stating the doctrine.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Is 'the Most High' the Father, or a distinct title whose identification with the Father is an inference?
- Does 'Son of God' here state deity, or sonship in a messianic sense?
- Does the verse state a trinitarian pattern, or only the Spirit's role in the conception?
