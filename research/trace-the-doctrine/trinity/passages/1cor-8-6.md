# 1 Corinthians 8:6 (1cor-8-6)

**Reference:** 1 Corinthians 8:6 — canonical position 46 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: 1 Corinthians 8:6 is classified indirect because it confesses one God the Father and one Lord Jesus Christ together without stating the Trinity, so it supports the doctrine by implication rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: for believers there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things and for whom we exist, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things and through whom we exist. The verse is a single confession naming the Father and the Lord Jesus together, echoing the Shema, and it assigns both of them a role in the existence of all things. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from the shared confession and the shared creative and sustaining roles that Christ stands within the identity of the one God rather than outside it, and may read the verse as consistent with the later doctrine. But the text does not state that Christ is God, does not name the Holy Spirit, and does not state a doctrine of three persons in one God; the full trinitarian reading is an inference, not the text's statement. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage supports the doctrine by implication.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does placing Jesus Christ inside the one-God confession imply Christ shares the divine identity, or only that Christ is the one Lord alongside the one God?
- How does the echo of the Shema shape the reading: does it include Christ in God's oneness, or contrast Christ with God?
- Does the absence of the Holy Spirit from this confession limit what the verse can contribute to a trinitarian reading?
