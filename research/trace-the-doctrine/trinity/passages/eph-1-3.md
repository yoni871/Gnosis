# Ephesians 1:3-14 (eph-1-3)

**Reference:** Ephesians 1:3-14 — canonical position 49 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: Ephesians 1:3-14 is classified indirect because its long blessing is structured triadically around the Father, the Son, and the Spirit without stating the Trinity, so it supports the doctrine by pattern rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: one long blessing of the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who chose believers in Christ before the foundation of the world, predestined them for adoption through Jesus Christ, redeemed them through Christ's blood, and sealed believers who heard and believed with the promised Holy Spirit, the guarantee of their inheritance. The blessing is structured triadically across Father, Son and Spirit. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from that triadic structure a coordinated divine agency in salvation, and may read the passage as consistent with the later doctrine. But the passage's statement is the blessing itself and the history of salvation it recounts; it does not state that Father, Son and Spirit are three persons in one God, so the trinitarian reading is a pattern-based inference, not the text's statement. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto it. The passage supports the doctrine by implication.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Is the triadic structure of the blessing a deliberate theological pattern, or the natural shape of recounting salvation history?
- Does the sealing by the Holy Spirit imply the Spirit's deity, or only the Spirit's role as God's seal and guarantee?
- Does the Father's choosing “in Christ” imply Christ's pre-existence, or only God's purpose in Christ?

## Cross-references

- eph-2-18: Both passages describe believers' access to the Father through Christ in the Spirit.
- eph-4-4: Both passages present the one God, one Lord, and one Spirit in a unified confession.
- 2cor-13-14: Both passages name all three together in blessing believers.
