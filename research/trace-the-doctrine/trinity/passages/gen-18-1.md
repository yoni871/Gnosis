# Genesis 18:1-15 — Research Note

- **Reference:** Genesis 18:1-15 (canonical position 1, Protestant canon)
- **Doctrine:** trinity
- **Classification:** historically-interpreted
- **Justification:** Three visitors and the LORD appear, but the text never names Father, Son, and Holy Spirit and never states that God is three persons. Any trinitarian reading is later reception. The reading is contested, and that uncertainty is flagged honestly rather than smoothed over.
- **Status:** verification-pending

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

- **(A) What the text states:** The LORD appeared to Abraham by the oaks of Mamre; Abraham looked up and saw three men standing in front of him; he bowed and addressed them as "O Lord"; the LORD then promised Sarah a son. The narrative moves between "the LORD" speaking in the singular and "three men" acting together.
- **(B) Inference (labelled as inference):** A reader may observe that the threefold appearance alongside the singular LORD is consistent with a plurality within the one God, but the text itself does not draw that conclusion.
- **(C) Historical reading:** Later writers read the three visitors in a trinitarian light; that reading is contested reception history, to be attributed in Phase 3, and must not be presented as the text's plain meaning. No specific writer or quotation is recorded here.
- **(D) Later formulation:** The formulated doctrine of the Trinity dates from later centuries and must not be projected back onto this narrative.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- How are the three men and the LORD related in the narrative — is the LORD one of the three, distinct from them, or speaking through them?
- Alternative: the visitors may be read as angels or as a theophany of the LORD alone, with no trinitarian significance; how is that alternative weighed?
- Does Abraham address one figure or all three when he says "O Lord", and does the singular/plural shifting in the passage resolve the question?
- What evidence within the passage itself, as opposed to later reading, could support a trinitarian interpretation?

## Cross-references

- gen-1-26 — both passages present plurality language or a threefold appearance later read in a trinitarian light.
- gen-19-24 — the Genesis 18 visitation leads directly into the Sodom narrative, where the LORD is again named twofold.
- matt-28-19 — Matthew 28:19 names three persons explicitly, unlike Genesis 18, which names no persons.

## Textual-variant / uncertainty flags

- Uncertainty flagged: the trinitarian reading of the three visitors is contested; the text identifies the visitors as men and the LORD, and the reading is later reception, not textual fact.
