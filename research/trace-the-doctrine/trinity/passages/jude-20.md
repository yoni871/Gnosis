# Jude 20-21

- **Reference:** Jude 20-21 (canonical position 65)
- **Classification:** indirect
- **Justification:** All three are named together in one exhortation, but the passage's statement is an exhortation to persevere, and it says nothing about the three persons' relation to one another. The triadic pattern supports the doctrine by implication.
- **Relationship (A–D):** (A) The text states: "But you, beloved, building yourselves up in your most holy faith and praying in the Holy Spirit, keep yourselves in the love of God, waiting for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ that leads to eternal life". (B) Inference, labelled as inference: the joint naming of the three in a single exhortation is consistent with and supports the later doctrine that Father, Son, and Spirit are distinct persons in the one divine life. (C) How later writers read the passage is a matter for Phase 3; no historical reading is asserted here. (D) The later formulation of the Trinity is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto these verses as their explicit teaching.
- **Interpretive questions and alternatives:**
  - Is the threefold pattern (Holy Spirit, God, Jesus Christ) a deliberate triadic structure, or an exhortation that happens to name all three?
  - Does "the love of God" refer to God the Father specifically, and does that affect how the three are related in the verse?
  - Alternative: the verse may simply stack three distinct devotional acts without asserting anything about the persons' relation.
