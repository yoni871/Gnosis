## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states Jesus will ask the Father, who 'will give you another Helper, to be with you forever, even the Spirit of truth' (vv. 16-17). The Father gives, Jesus asks, and the Spirit comes as Helper. (B) Inference: 'another' (of the same kind) implies the Spirit is like Jesus, and the Spirit's divine personhood is inferred from being given in Jesus' place forever - neither is stated in so many words. (C) Later writers read 'another Helper' as evidence for the Spirit's personhood and deity; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine came later and is not stated here. The passage supports the doctrine by implication: it is consistent with a personal Spirit distinct from Father and Son, and points toward the Spirit's divine status, without stating it.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does 'another Helper' mean another of the same kind as Jesus, or only another helper in sequence?
- Does the passage state the Spirit's personhood, or could 'Spirit of truth' be read impersonally?
- Does 'to be with you forever' state the Spirit's divine status, or only the permanence of his presence?
