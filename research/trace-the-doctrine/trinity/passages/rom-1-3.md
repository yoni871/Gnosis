# Romans 1:3-4 (rom-1-3)

**Reference:** Romans 1:3-4 — canonical position 45 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: Romans 1:3-4 is classified indirect because it places the Son and the Spirit together in the Son's designation without stating the Trinity, so it supports the doctrine by implication rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: God's Son was descended from David according to the flesh and was declared to be the Son of God in power according to the Spirit of holiness by his resurrection from the dead. The verse names the Son and the Spirit together in the one designation of the Son. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: a reader may infer from this joint naming a close relation between the Son and the Spirit within God's single action, and may read the passage as consistent with the later doctrine. But the text does not state that the Spirit is a distinct divine person, nor that Father, Son and Spirit share one divine being; that synthesis is the researcher's inference, not the text's statement. (C) Later writers read this verse in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity, expressed in technical language of persons and essence, is not stated in this passage and must not be projected back onto it. The passage therefore supports the doctrine by implication and points toward it, without stating it.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does “according to the Spirit of holiness” refer to the Holy Spirit, or to Christ's own holy spirit or inner life?
- Does “declared to be the Son of God in power” imply a change in the Son's status at the resurrection, or a public declaration of a status already held?
- What exactly does the joint naming of Son and Spirit license as an inference about their relation, and what does it not license?

## Cross-references

- gal-4-4: Both passages describe God sending his Son, with the Spirit named alongside the Son.
- phil-2-5: Both passages bear on the Son's divine status, Philippians by direct statement and Romans by implication.
