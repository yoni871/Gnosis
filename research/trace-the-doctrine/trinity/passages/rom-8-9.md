# Romans 8:9-11 (rom-8-9)

**Reference:** Romans 8:9-11 — canonical position 45 in the Protestant canon.
**Status:** verification-pending

## Classification: indirect

Justification: Romans 8:9-11 is classified indirect because it calls the one indwelling Spirit both the Spirit of God and the Spirit of Christ without stating the Spirit's deity, so it supports the doctrine by implication rather than stating it.

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: the Spirit of God dwells in believers, anyone without the Spirit of Christ does not belong to Christ, and the Spirit of him who raised Jesus from the dead will give life to believers' mortal bodies. The same indwelling Spirit is called both the Spirit of God and the Spirit of Christ. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: from the Spirit being named as the Spirit of both God and Christ, and from the Spirit's life-giving work, a reader may infer that the Spirit stands in a close, shared relation to Father and Son, and may read the passage as consistent with the Spirit's divine personhood. The text does not state that the Spirit is God, nor that the Spirit is a distinct person of the Trinity; that is an inference, not a statement. (C) Later writers read this passage in a doctrinal light; Phase 3 will supply the attributed readings, and no specific writer or quotation is asserted here. (D) The later formulated doctrine of the Trinity is not stated here and must not be projected back onto the passage. The passage supports the doctrine by implication.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Does calling the one Spirit both the Spirit of God and the Spirit of Christ identify the Spirit with both, or describe the Spirit's relation to each?
- Does the Spirit's indwelling and life-giving work imply the Spirit's deity, or only the Spirit's divine agency?
- How does the passage's contrast between flesh and Spirit bear on reading the Spirit as a person rather than an impersonal power?

## Cross-references

- rom-8-14: The two passages form one continuous argument about life in the Spirit and adoption as God's children.
- gal-4-4: Both passages name the Spirit of the Son dwelling in believers.
- 1cor-2-10: Both passages describe the Spirit's knowledge of and relation to God.
