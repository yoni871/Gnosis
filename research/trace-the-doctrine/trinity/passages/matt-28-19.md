## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states the risen Jesus' command to baptize 'in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit' (v. 19). The three are named together in a single baptismal formula, under one singular 'name'. (B) Inference: the singular name shared by the three points toward their unity of essence, but the verse does not state 'one essence, three persons' in so many words. (C) Later writers read this formula as the classic trinitarian baptismal text; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The formulated doctrine came later and is not stated here; this passage is direct evidence for the triadic element only, not for the fully formulated doctrine. Classification as direct is a candidate per standards section 1.1, resting on the three named together in one formula, and the independent verifier must confirm it.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.
