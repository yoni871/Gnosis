## Classification and justification

Classified `indirect`. The text does not explicitly state the doctrinal content, but it bears on the doctrine by a pattern it establishes, without stating it (RESEARCH-STANDARDS §1.2).

## Relationship to the doctrine (A–D)

(A) What the text states: the speaker declares, 'And now the Lord GOD has sent me, and his Spirit,' naming the Lord, a sent speaker, and his Spirit together in one sentence. (B) Inference, labelled as inference: this grouping is consistent with, and supports, a trinitarian pattern of sender, sent one, and Spirit, and it points toward the doctrine; but the verse does not name the sent speaker, does not identify him with the Son, and does not state that the three are one God, so the trinitarian reading is an inference from the pattern, not an explicit statement. (C) Later readers identified the sent speaker with the Son; that is reception history, and Phase 3 will attribute specific readings. (D) The later doctrine of the Trinity was formulated centuries after Isaiah and must not be projected onto the verse. The passage is therefore indirect: the text itself supplies the threefold pattern, while the doctrinal identification of its figures is inferred.

No historical writer is named and no quotation is given here: attributed readings are Phase 3 work. The later trinitarian formulation (D) is dated generally as later and is never projected back onto the text.

## Interpretive questions and alternatives

- Who is the speaker ('me') in verse 16 — the prophet, the Servant, Cyrus, or another figure — and does the verse itself say?
- Does the verse present the Spirit as a co-sender with the Lord GOD, or as accompanying the sent speaker, and does that distinction matter for the trinitarian reading?
- Alternative: the verse may be read as the prophet speaking of his own commission with no trinitarian content — what in the wording supports or resists that reading?
- Is the grouping of Lord, speaker, and Spirit enough to count as pointing toward the doctrine, or is it only consistent with it?

## Textual-variant and uncertainty flags

Textual/translation flag: the identity of the speaker ('me') is not stated in the verse; some readings identify the speaker with the prophet, the Servant, or Cyrus. ESV renders 'the Lord GOD has sent me, and his Spirit'; other translations render the Spirit as co-sender ('the Lord GOD and his Spirit have sent me'). The trinitarian identification of the speaker is an inference, not stated.
