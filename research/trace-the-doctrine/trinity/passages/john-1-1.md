## Relationship to the doctrine (A-D)

(A) The text states: 'In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God' (v. 1), and 'All things were made through him' (v. 3). The Word is both distinct from God ('with God') and identified as God. (B) Inference: identifying the Word with the Son, Jesus Christ, rests on the wider Gospel (notably v. 14, outside this range) and is an inference for this passage's range, though the prologue's own logic points that way. (C) Later writers read this as the classic statement of the Son's deity; those readings belong to Phase 3 and are not asserted here. (D) The passage does not state the fully formulated doctrine of the Trinity: it names no third person here and uses no later trinitarian vocabulary. It is direct evidence for one element only - the deity of the Son (the Word) - and the verifier must confirm that limited scope.

Labels used above: (A) what the text explicitly states; (B) what may reasonably be inferred, labelled as inference; (C) how later writers read it — no specific writers, works, or quotations are asserted here (Phase 3); (D) how the doctrine was later formulated, dated only generally as later and never projected back onto the text.
