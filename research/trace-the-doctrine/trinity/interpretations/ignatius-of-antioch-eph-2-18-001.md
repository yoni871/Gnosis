## Quotation

> as being stones of the temple of the Father, prepared for the building of God the Father, and drawn up on high by the instrument of Jesus Christ, which is the cross, making use of the Holy Spirit as a rope, while your faith was the means by which you ascended, and your love the way which led up to God.

## Explanation

In Ephesians 9 Ignatius praises the Ephesians for stopping their ears to false doctrine: they are temple stones of the Father, raised by the instrument of Jesus Christ, the cross, using the Holy Spirit as a rope, with faith as the means of ascent and love as the way to God. Father, Christ, and Spirit each have a role in the one ascent to God.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): the threefold pattern - the Father's temple, Christ the instrument, the Spirit the rope - parallels Ephesians 2:18, though Ignatius does not quote the verse; the link is a doctrinal connection drawn by the researcher (row B). The Ephesians text (row A) speaks of access to the Father through Christ in one Spirit; the building-site metaphor is Ignatius's own.

## Historical context

Epistle to the Ephesians, written c. 105-115 (range) while Ignatius travelled under guard to Rome for martyrdom, exhorts the Ephesians to unity and warns against false teachers. Chapters 7 and 9 confess Christ and picture the believers' ascent to God.

## Alternatives and disagreements

No second-century dissenting reading of Ephesians 2:18 was found in the sources checked.
