## Quotation

> "Word," according as we use the term strictly of God, signifies something proceeding from another; which belongs to the nature of personal terms in God, inasmuch as the divine persons are distinguished by origin.

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 34, a. 1, respondeo
- **Language:** English
- **Translation:** Fathers of the English Dominican Province
- **Edition:** English translation by the Fathers of the English Dominican Province (1920), New Advent online edition
- **Reproducibility:** public-domain

## Explanation

Aquinas asks whether 'Word' in God is a personal name. He answers that 'Word', taken strictly, signifies the concept of the intellect proceeding from the knower, and therefore signifies something proceeding from another, which is the mark of a personal term in God, since the divine persons are distinguished by origin. The Word of John 1:1 is thus, for Aquinas, the proper name of the Son: the Son alone is properly called Word in God (developed in q. 34, a. 2).

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads John 1:1 ('In the beginning was the Word') as naming the Son: the Word is the divine person's proper name, proceeding from the Father by an intelligible emanation. (A) The text of John 1:1 itself states that the Word was with God and was God; it does not use the later scholastic vocabulary of 'personal term' or 'procession by way of intellect'. (D) The formulation of Word as a personal term distinct by relation of origin is Aquinas's thirteenth-century scholastic formulation and must not be projected back onto John's wording.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). Aquinas is working in the scholastic Trinitarian treatise (qq. 27-43), building on Augustine's De Trinitate and Anselm's Monologion, and answering the Arian claim that the Son differs in substance from the Father.
