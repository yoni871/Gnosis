## Quotation
> and if, finally, the Apostle Thomas, instructed in all the proofs and conditions of Christ’s divinity, says in reply to Christ, “My Lord and my God;” […]

- Location: Treatise Concerning the Trinity, ch. 13
- Translation: Robert Ernest Wallis
- Edition: Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 5 (1886)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Novatian treats Thomas's address to the risen Christ, 'My Lord and my God', as the instructed apostle's confession of Christ's divinity, standing alongside 'I and the Father are one' and Romans 9:5 in his cumulative proof that Christ is God. The ellipsis marks the continuation of the sentence into the Romans 9:5 citation, quoted in novatian-rom-9-5-001.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Novatian reads John 20:28 as Thomas confessing Christ as Lord and God. (A) The verse records Thomas's address to Jesus; that it states Christ's deity is Novatian's reading of it, shared by later Nicene writers. (D) The reading predates Nicaea and is not itself the Nicene formulation.

## Historical context

Treatise Concerning the Trinity, c. 250 (approximate), ch. 13, in the cumulative scriptural proof that Christ is God.
