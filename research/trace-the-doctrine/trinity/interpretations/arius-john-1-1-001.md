## Quotation
> ‘The Son was not always;’ for, whereas all things were made out of nothing, and all existing creatures and works were made, so the Word of God Himself was ‘made out of nothing,’ and ‘once He was not,’ and ‘He was not before His origination,’ but He as others ‘had an origin of creation.’

- Location: Athanasius, Four Discourses Against the Arians, Discourse I, ch. 2, §5, quoting Arius, Thalia
- Translation: John Henry Newman and Archibald Robertson (translators of the Athanasius text preserving the fragments)
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 4 (1892)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Athanasius quotes the Thalia of Arius to exhibit the Arian doctrine: God was not always Father but became Father; the Son was not always, but was made out of nothing, 'once was not', and had an origin of creation like other creatures; God made him Word and Wisdom and Son in order to form us by means of him. In §6 Athanasius adds that Arius held the Word to be 'not the very God' but God only in name, by participation of grace, 'alien and unlike in all things to the Father's essence'.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Arius read John 1:1 against the Nicene reading: the Word 'was in the beginning' means he was made before other things, not that he is eternal and of the Father's essence. His position is the dissenting reading of John 1:1 in this era and is recorded fairly, as RESEARCH-STANDARDS.md §6 requires. (D) The Council of Nicaea (325) anathematised this teaching ('there was a time when the Son of God was not'); the anathema is a later formulation responding to Arius, not a statement of John 1:1 itself. ATTRIBUTION WARNING: the Thalia survives only in fragments quoted by Athanasius, a hostile witness; the wording is Athanasius's quotation of Arius, not a manuscript of Arius himself.

## Historical context

Thalia, c. 318-321 (range), quoted in Athanasius, Four Discourses Against the Arians, Discourse I, ch. 2, §§5-6 (c. 356-360). Arius, presbyter of Alexandria, was condemned at Nicaea in 325; the controversy continued until 381.

## Alternative readings

- athanasius (athanasius-against-the-arians): Athanasius read John 1:1 as proof that the Word is God from God, eternal, of the Father's own essence, and inseparable from the Father. See athanasius-john-1-1-001.
