## Quotation

> For it is He of whom the apostle says, But because you are sons, God has sent forth the Spirit of His Son into your hearts: and it is He of whom the same Son says, For it is not ye who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you.

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series I, Vol. 3, ed. Philip Schaff (1887)
- Translation: Arthur West Haddan
- Location: Book XV, chapter 26, section 45
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

Augustine is arguing that the Holy Spirit is the Spirit of the Father and of the Son. He cites the apostle's words that God sent forth the Spirit of His Son into our hearts (Galatians 4:6), together with the Son's words that it is the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you (Matthew 10:20), to show that the same Spirit is called both the Spirit of the Son and the Spirit of the Father.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads Galatians 4:6 as showing that the Spirit is the Spirit of the Son as well as of the Father. (A) The text states that God sent the Spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying 'Abba! Father'. It does not state a doctrine of the Spirit's procession. (B) The step from the Spirit being called the Spirit of the Son to the Spirit's eternal procession from the Son is Augustine's inference. (D) The double-procession formulation is Augustine's own theological development, later disputed by the East (see john-of-damascus-john-15-26-001).

## Historical context

De Trinitate was written 399-422 (range; see works.json). Books I-IV defend the unity and equality of the Trinity from Scripture against those who argued, from texts about the sending and subordination of the Son and the Spirit, that the Son is less than the Father. This passage comes from Book XV, chapter 26, where Augustine treats the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father and from the Son.
