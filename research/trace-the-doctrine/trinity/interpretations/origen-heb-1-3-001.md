## Quotation
> And when writing to the Hebrews, he says of Him that He is “the brightness of His glory, and the express image of His person.”

- Location: De Principiis, Book I, ch. 2, §5
- Translation: Frederick Crombie
- Edition: Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 4 (1885)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Origen cites Hebrews 1:3 alongside Colossians 1:15 to support his account of the Son: the Son is the brightness of God's glory and the express image of his person, just as he is the image of the invisible God and the first-born of every creature. The image language lets Origen argue, in §6, that the image contains 'the unity of nature and substance belonging to Father and Son'.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Origen reads Hebrews 1:3 as Paul's description of the Son as the brightness of God's glory and the express image of his person, and draws from the image analogy a unity of nature and substance between Father and Son. (B) The step from 'image' to 'unity of nature and substance' is Origen's inference. (D) Origen's 'unity of nature and substance' is his own pre-Nicene formulation, not the Nicene homoousios, and it sits alongside his subordination of the Son to the Father.
