## Quotation
> This the Lord said, viz. ‘I and the Father are One;’ for neither is the Word separated from the Father, nor was or is the Father ever Wordless; […]

- Location: Four Discourses Against the Arians, Discourse IV, §2
- Translation: John Henry Newman and Archibald Robertson
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 4 (1892)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Athanasius argues that Father and Son are two, not one person as Sabellius said, because the Father is Father and the Son is Son, 'and they are one, because He is Son of the Essence of the Father by nature, existing as His own Word.' The Lord's words 'I and the Father are One' show the Word is not separated from the Father, and the Father was never Wordless. The ellipsis marks the continuation ('on this account He says, I in the Father and the Father in Me').

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Athanasius reads John 10:30 as the Son's oneness with the Father by nature, as Son of the Father's essence, while remaining distinct from the Father. (D) This is the post-Nicene defence of Nicaea against both Sabellianism (which collapsed Father and Son into one person) and Arianism (which separated them in essence).

## Historical context

Four Discourses Against the Arians, Discourse IV, §2, c. 356-360 (range; Discourse IV possibly later). The section is headed in the edition 'Illustration of John 10:30, drawn from Deuteronomy 4:4'.
