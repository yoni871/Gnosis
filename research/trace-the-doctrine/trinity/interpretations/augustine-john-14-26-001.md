## Quotation

> And it is proved by many other testimonies of the Divine Word, that the Spirit, who is specially called in the Trinity the Holy Spirit, is of the Father and of the Son: of whom likewise the Son Himself says, Whom I will send unto you from the Father; and in another place, Whom the Father will send in my name.

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series I, Vol. 3, ed. Philip Schaff (1887)
- Translation: Arthur West Haddan
- Location: Book XV, chapter 26, section 45
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

In the closing book of De Trinitate, Augustine argues that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and from the Son, apart from time. He supports this by joining the two sendings: the Son sends the Spirit from the Father (John 15:26), and the Father sends the Spirit in the Son's name (John 14:26). The two statements are, for Augustine, one sending, showing the Spirit to be of both.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads John 14:26 together with John 15:26 as showing that the Spirit is of the Father and of the Son. (A) John 14:26 states that the Father will send the Comforter, the Holy Spirit, in Christ's name; it does not state eternal procession from the Son. (B) The step from the two sendings to eternal procession from both is Augustine's inference. (D) The double-procession (filioque) formulation is Augustine's own theological development; the East later rejected it, and the difference is recorded, not harmonised (see john-of-damascus-john-15-26-001).

## Historical context

De Trinitate was written 399-422 (range; see works.json). Books I-IV defend the unity and equality of the Trinity from Scripture against those who argued, from texts about the sending and subordination of the Son and the Spirit, that the Son is less than the Father. This passage comes from Book XV, chapter 26, the summary and conclusion of the whole work, where Augustine treats the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father and from the Son.

## Alternative readings

- Author: `john-of-damascus`; work: `john-of-damascus-exposition-of-the-orthodox-faith`.
  John of Damascus holds that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father alone, not from the Son. The East/West difference on the Spirit's procession is recorded here, not harmonised.
  Citation: John of Damascus, An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith, Book I, ch. 8 (NPNF Series II, Vol. 9).
