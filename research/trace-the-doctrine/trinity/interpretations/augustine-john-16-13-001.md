## Quotation

> For He shall not speak of Himself, but whatsoever He shall hear, that shall He speak; and, For He shall receive of mine, and shall show it unto you. But because He has rendered the reason why He said, He shall receive of mine (for He says, All things that the Father has are mine; therefore said I, that He shall take of mine); it remains that the Holy Spirit be understood to have of that which is the Father's, as the Son also has.

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series I, Vol. 3, ed. Philip Schaff (1887)
- Translation: Arthur West Haddan
- Location: Book II, chapter 3, section 5
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

Augustine is answering the claim that John 16:13 ('He shall not speak of Himself') proves the Spirit is less, as if born of Christ as Christ is of the Father. He replies that Christ immediately gives the reason: 'All things that the Father has are mine; therefore said I, that He shall take of mine.' The Spirit is therefore to be understood to have of that which is the Father's, as the Son also has.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads John 16:13-15 as showing that the Spirit has of that which is the Father's, as the Son also has. (A) The text states that the Spirit of truth will guide into all truth, will not speak of Himself, will receive of Christ's and show it, because all things the Father has are Christ's. (B) The conclusion that the Spirit shares the Father's substance equally with the Son is Augustine's inference from the 'all things' clause. (D) The equality-of-substance formulation is later theological language applied to the text.

## Historical context

De Trinitate was written 399-422 (range; see works.json). Books I-IV defend the unity and equality of the Trinity from Scripture against those who argued, from texts about the sending and subordination of the Son and the Spirit, that the Son is less than the Father. This passage comes from Book II, chapter 3, the same section as augustine-john-15-26-001.
