## Explanation

In Martyrdom of Polycarp 14, bound at the stake, Polycarp thanks God the Father of Jesus Christ for counting him worthy of the martyrs' cup and resurrection through the incorruption of the Holy Spirit, and closes by giving glory to the Father, with the Son and the Holy Spirit. The prayer is reported by the letter's authors, the church of Smyrna, not written by Polycarp himself.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): the letter records a threefold doxology - Father, Son, and Holy Spirit receiving glory together - consistent with the threefold pattern of 2 Corinthians 13:14, which the letter does not quote; the link is a doctrinal connection drawn by the researcher (row B). The verse (row A) is Paul's benediction; the prayer is the Smyrnaeans' report of Polycarp's words.

## Historical context

Martyrdom of Polycarp, written c. 155-160 (range), is a letter from the church of Smyrna to the church of Philomelium reporting Polycarp's arrest and burning. Chapter 14 gives Polycarp's prayer at the stake, as reported by the letter's authors; Polycarp is the subject of the letter, not its author.
