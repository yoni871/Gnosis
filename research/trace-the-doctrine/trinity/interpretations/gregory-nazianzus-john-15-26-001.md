## Quotation
> The Holy Ghost, which proceedeth from the Father; Who, inasmuch as He proceedeth from That Source, is no Creature; and inasmuch as He is not Begotten is no Son; and inasmuch as He is between the Unbegotten and the Begotten is God.

- Location: Fifth Theological Oration (Oration 31), §8
- Translation: Charles Gordon Browne and James Edward Swallow
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 7 (1894)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Answering the dilemma that the Spirit must be either Unbegotten or Begotten, Gregory appeals to the Saviour's own word: 'The Holy Ghost, which proceedeth from the Father' (John 15:26). Procession is the Spirit's distinctive mode: because he proceeds from the Father he is no creature; because he is not begotten he is no Son; and because he stands between the Unbegotten and the Begotten, he is God. 'What then is Procession?' Gregory asks, and answers that it is as unexplainable as the Father's unbegottenness and the Son's generation.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Gregory reads John 15:26 as giving the Spirit's distinctive property, procession from the Father, which marks him as God, neither creature nor Son. (D) This procession language is the direct source of the Constantinopolitan Creed's 'who proceedeth from the Father' (381); Gregory's oration (380) precedes the council and is not itself the creed.

## Historical context

Fifth Theological Oration (Oration 31), c. 380 (approximate), §8, delivered at Constantinople against those who denied the Spirit's deity (the Pneumatomachians/Macedonians).
