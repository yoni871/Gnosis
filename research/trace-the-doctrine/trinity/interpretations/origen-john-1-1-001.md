## Quotation
> John, however, with more sublimity and propriety, says in the beginning of his Gospel, when defining God by a special definition to be the Word, “And God was the Word, and this was in the beginning with God.”

- Location: De Principiis, Book I, ch. 2, §3
- Translation: Frederick Crombie
- Edition: Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 4 (1885)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Origen argues that Wisdom, understood as the beginning of the ways of God, is also to be understood as the Word of God, because she discloses the mysteries of divine wisdom to creation. John, he says, defines God as the Word with more sublimity: 'And God was the Word, and this was in the beginning with God.' Whoever assigns a beginning to the Word or Wisdom, Origen warns, is guilty of impiety against the unbegotten Father, since that would deny the Father had always been Father.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Origen reads John 1:1 as John's definition of God as the Word, eternal with God, and uses it to deny that the Word ever began to be. (D) This eternal generation is Origen's own doctrine; it is pre-Nicene, and Origen elsewhere subordinates the Son to the Father (the Father alone is unbegotten and the source). Origen must not be presented as teaching Nicaea's homoousios.

## Historical context

De Principiis, c. 220-230 (range), Book I, ch. 2, §§2-3. Origen is arguing against the idea that God was ever without his Wisdom or Word.

## Alternative readings

- arius (arius-thalia): Arius read the Word as a creature made out of nothing, who 'once was not' and 'was not before His origination'; the Word is God only in name, by participation of grace, and is 'alien and unlike in all things to the Father's essence'. See arius-john-1-1-001.
