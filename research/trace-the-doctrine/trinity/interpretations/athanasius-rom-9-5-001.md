## Quotation
> And since Christ is God from God, and God’s Word, Wisdom, Son, and Power, therefore but One God is declared in the divine Scriptures. For the Word, being Son of the One God, is referred to Him of whom also He is; so that Father and Son are two, yet the Monad of the Godhead is indivisible and inseparable.

- Location: Four Discourses Against the Arians, Discourse IV, §1
- Translation: John Henry Newman and Archibald Robertson
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 4 (1892)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

In the same opening section, Athanasius draws the conclusion from John 1:1 and Romans 9:5 together: because Christ is God from God, the Scriptures declare one God, while Father and Son are two and the Monad of the Godhead remains indivisible. Romans 9:5 ('God over all') is one of the two texts on which the argument is built.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Athanasius reads Romans 9:5 as Paul calling Christ 'God over all', and uses it with John 1:1 to argue for one God in Father and Son. (A) As with Novatian's use, the verse's reference is disputed in modern scholarship; Athanasius's reading takes it as referring to Christ, and that attribution is what is recorded here. (D) The argument defends the Nicene settlement after 325.
