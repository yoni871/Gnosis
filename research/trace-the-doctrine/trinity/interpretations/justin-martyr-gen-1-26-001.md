## Explanation

In Dialogue 62 Justin answers Trypho's teachers, who said God spoke to himself, or to the elements from which man was formed, when he said 'Let us make man.' Justin replies that Moses shows God conversing with someone numerically distinct and rational, and he identifies that someone with the Offspring truly brought forth from the Father, who was with the Father before all creatures and is the Wisdom of Proverbs 8.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (standards section 2, row C): Justin reads the plural of Genesis 1:26 as God the Father speaking to the Son/Word. The Genesis text itself (row A) does not name Father, Son, or Holy Spirit and does not state the later doctrine of the Trinity; Justin's inference (row B) that the addressee is the pre-existent Word is his own, and the later Nicene formulation (row D, 325) must not be projected back onto him.

## Historical context

Dialogue with Trypho, written c. 155-160 (range), is Justin's defence of Christianity in dialogue with the Jew Trypho, arguing from the Old Testament that Jesus is the Christ and is divine. The chapters cited here (56, 62) belong to the section in which Justin argues that the Scriptures speak of another God and Lord, distinct from the Maker yet subject to him.

## Alternatives and disagreements

Justin himself records the opposing readings in Dialogue 62: Trypho's teachers said either that God spoke to himself, as one says 'Let us make' to oneself, or that God spoke to the elements, e.g. the earth, from which man was formed. Justin rejects both. No other second-century dissenting reading of Genesis 1:26 was found in the sources checked.
