## Explanation

Against the claim that angels made man, Irenaeus argues in IV.20 that God needed no angels: the Word and Wisdom, Son and Spirit, were always with him, and to them he spoke 'Let us make man.' Man was formed by God's hands, that is, by the Son and Holy Spirit. The preface to Book IV states the same point: man was 'moulded by His hands, that is, by the Son and Holy Spirit, to whom also He said, Let Us make man.'

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Irenaeus identifies the addressees of Genesis 1:26 as Son and Spirit. The text (row A) gives only the plural; the identification is Irenaeus's reading, and his 'two hands' imagery is his own, not later Nicene vocabulary (row D).

## Historical context

Against Heresies, written c. 175-185 (range), is Irenaeus's refutation of Valentinian Gnosticism. Book III argues from Scripture that the Creator is the one true God and Father of Jesus Christ; Book IV argues that the one God formed all things by means of the Word and the Holy Spirit.

## Alternatives and disagreements

Irenaeus records the opposing position he is refuting: that angels, or some power remotely distant from the Father, made and formed man (IV.20.1; Preface to Book IV). No other second-century dissenting reading of Genesis 1:26 was found in the sources checked.
