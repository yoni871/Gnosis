## Quotation
> who sent also from heaven from the Father, according to His own promise, the Holy Ghost, the Paraclete, the sanctifier of the faith of those who believe in the Father, and in the Son, and in the Holy Ghost.

- Location: Against Praxeas, ch. 2
- Translation: Peter Holmes
- Edition: Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 3 (1885)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

In stating the rule of faith, Tertullian names the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost together as the objects of Christian belief, and describes the Spirit as sent from heaven from the Father to sanctify the faith of believers. He then states the formula that the dispensation 'distributes the Unity into a Trinity, placing in their order the three Persons—the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost', yet of one substance, one condition, and one power.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Tertullian reads the baptismal naming of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit (Matt 28:19) as the shape of the rule of faith: one God in three Persons. (A) The verse itself names the three together in one name; it does not state one substance in three persons. (D) Tertullian's 'one substance, three Persons' is the Latin formula that later fed into Nicene theology, but Tertullian himself predates Nicaea by over a century and does not teach homoousios.

## Historical context

Against Praxeas, c. 207-213 (range), ch. 2, 'The Catholic Doctrine of the Trinity and Unity'. Written against Praxeas, who held Father, Son, and Holy Ghost to be the very selfsame Person.
