## Quotation
> We read The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Ghost, and again I beseech you for the Lord Jesus Christ’s sake, and for the love of the Spirit.

- Location: On the Holy Spirit, ch. 25, §59
- Translation: Blomfield Jackson
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 8
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Defending the doxology 'with the Son together with the Holy Ghost' against those who object to the preposition 'with', Basil argues that Scripture joins the names with 'and' in the same way: 'The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Ghost' (2 Corinthians 13:14, cited as 13:13 in the edition's numbering). The conjunction of the three names shows their conjunction in glory; 'with' sets forth the distinction of the hypostases and the inseparability of the fellowship, and refutes both Sabellius and those who separate the Son from the Father and the Spirit from the Son.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Basil reads 2 Corinthians 13:14 as Scripture joining the names of the Lord Jesus Christ, God, and the Holy Spirit in one benediction, which warrants joining them in the doxology. (A) The verse itself is a benediction naming the three; it does not state that they are one God or one substance. (D) Basil's argument is about liturgical language (c. 375), not a creedal definition; the later creed of 381 would confess the Spirit as worshipped and glorified with Father and Son.

## Historical context

On the Holy Spirit, c. 375 (approximate), ch. 25, §59, on the use of 'with' in the doxology, against the Pneumatomachians and in answer to critics of Basil's own liturgical language.
