## Quotation
> Peter’s words to Sapphira, How is it that you have agreed together to tempt the Spirit of the Lord? You have not lied unto men, but unto God, show that sins against the Holy Spirit and against God are the same; […]

- Location: On the Holy Spirit, ch. 16, §37
- Translation: Blomfield Jackson
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 8
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Basil argues that the Holy Spirit is inseparable from the Father and the Son in every operation. Peter's words to Sapphira (Acts 5:3-4, 9) show that lying to the Holy Spirit is lying to God, so 'sins against the Holy Spirit and against God are the same'. The ellipsis marks the continuation ('and thus you might learn that in every operation the Spirit is closely conjoined with, and inseparable from, the Father and the Son').

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Basil reads Acts 5:3-4 as identifying the Holy Spirit with God: to lie to the Spirit is to lie to God. (B) The step from 'lying to the Spirit is lying to God' to 'the Spirit is God, of the same nature as the Father' is Basil's inference, labelled as such. (D) Basil is writing after Nicaea and before Constantinople (381); he argues for the Spirit's inseparability but does not in this treatise call the Spirit homoousios.

## Historical context

On the Holy Spirit, c. 375 (approximate), ch. 16, §37, addressed to Amphilochius of Iconium against the Pneumatomachians ('Spirit-fighters'), who held the Spirit to be a creature.
