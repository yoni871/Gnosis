## Quotation

> And only the Holy Spirit proceedeth from the Father's essence, not having been generated but simply proceeding. For this is the doctrine of Holy Scripture.

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series II, Vol. 9, ed. Philip Schaff and Henry Wace (1898)
- Translation: S. D. F. Salmond
- Location: Book I, chapter 8
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

John of Damascus states the Eastern doctrine of the Spirit's procession: the Father alone is uncaused; the Son is begotten of the Father; the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father's essence, not by generation but by simple procession. He presents this as the doctrine of Holy Scripture, appealing to John 15:26 ('the Spirit of truth, which proceedeth from the Father'). In the same chapter he says: 'we speak likewise of the Holy Spirit as from the Father, and call Him the Spirit of the Father. And we do not speak of the Spirit as from the Son.'

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) John of Damascus reads John 15:26 as teaching that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father alone, by simple procession, not by generation. (A) The text states that the Spirit of truth proceeds from the Father; it does not state the technical distinction between generation and procession, nor the word 'alone'. (B) The inference that the Spirit proceeds from the Father alone is John's, argued from the verse together with his account of the Father as the sole cause. (D) The generation/procession distinction and the rejection of the filioque are later formulations and are recorded here as John's doctrine, not as the Gospel's own claim.

## Historical context

An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith was written c. 730-743 (range; exact date not established). Book I, ch. 8 ('Concerning the Holy Trinity') is a compendium of earlier Greek Fathers' teaching on the Trinity, and sets out the Eastern doctrine of the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father alone.

## Alternative readings

- Author: `augustine`; work: `augustine-de-trinitate`.
  Augustine holds that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and from the Son (De Trinitate XV.26.46; II.3.5). Leo I likewise speaks of the Spirit as proceeding 'from both' (Letter 15, ch. II). The East/West difference is recorded, not harmonised.
  Citation: Augustine, On the Holy Trinity, Book XV, ch. 26 (NPNF Series I, Vol. 3); Leo the Great, Letter 15, ch. II (NPNF Series II, Vol. 12).
