## Quotation

> It is said (Deuteronomy 6:4): "Hear, O Israel, the Lord thy God is one God."

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 39, a. 3, sed contra

## Explanation

Aquinas asks whether essential names should be predicated of the three persons in the singular or the plural. Against the claim that the three persons are 'three Gods', he cites Deuteronomy 6:4 ('Hear, O Israel, the Lord thy God is one God') as the authority that the one divine essence is predicated in the singular. The Shema is, for Aquinas, the scriptural ground for saying 'one God' rather than 'three Gods'.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads Deuteronomy 6:4 as the scriptural authority for predicating 'God' of the three persons in the singular: one God, not three Gods. (A) The text states that the LORD is one; it says nothing about three persons or about the later doctrine. (D) The scholastic question of singular versus plural predication of essential names is a thirteenth-century formulation; the Shema supplies the 'one God' premise, and the 'three persons' half of the doctrine comes from elsewhere.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article belongs to Aquinas's treatment of the persons in relation to the essence (q. 39), addressing the grammar of divine names.
