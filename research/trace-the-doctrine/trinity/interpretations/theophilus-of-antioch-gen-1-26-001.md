## Quotation

> Moreover, God is found, as if needing help, to say, Let Us make man in Our image, after Our likeness. But to no one else than to His own Word and wisdom did He say, Let Us make.

## Explanation

In II.18 Theophilus says God made all things by his Word, and reckons the creation of man the only work worthy of his own hands; the plural 'Let us make' is addressed to his Word and Wisdom alone, as if God needed help.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Theophilus identifies the addressees of Genesis 1:26 as God's own Word and Wisdom. The text (row A) gives only the plural; the identification is Theophilus's reading, and his Word/Wisdom pair is not later Nicene Father/Son/Spirit language (row D).

## Historical context

To Autolycus, written c. 180 (approximate), is Theophilus's apology addressed to his pagan friend Autolycus. Book II walks through Genesis 1 day by day with allegorising commentary, and explains how the uncontainable Father can be said to walk in Paradise (ch. 22).

## Alternatives and disagreements

No second-century dissenting reading of Genesis 1:26 was found in the sources checked.
