## Quotation

> For it is plain that we are to take the Word of God to be the only Son of God, of whom it is afterwards said, And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, on account of that birth of His incarnation, which was wrought in time of the Virgin. But herein is declared, not only that He is God, but also that He is of the same substance with the Father.

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series I, Vol. 3, ed. Philip Schaff (1887)
- Translation: Arthur West Haddan
- Location: Book I, chapter 6, section 9
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

Augustine cites John 1:1 against those who said Christ is not God, or not very God, or not the one and only God with the Father. He takes 'the Word' to be the only Son of God, and argues from 'all things were made by Him, and without Him was not anything made' that the Son was not made, and therefore is not a creature but is of the same substance with the Father.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads John 1:1 as declaring the Word to be God and of the same substance with the Father. (A) The text states that the Word was with God and was God, and that all things were made through Him; the technical term 'same substance' (homoousios) is Augustine's, not the Gospel's wording. (B) The step from 'was not made' to 'of the same substance' is Augustine's inference. (D) The consubstantiality formula was fixed at Nicaea (325), before Augustine, and is a formulation about the text, not the text's own vocabulary.

## Historical context

De Trinitate was written 399-422 (range; see works.json). Books I-IV defend the unity and equality of the Trinity from Scripture against those who argued, from texts about the sending and subordination of the Son and the Spirit, that the Son is less than the Father. This passage comes from Book I, chapter 6, where Augustine collects scriptural testimonies that the Son is very God, of the same substance with the Father.
