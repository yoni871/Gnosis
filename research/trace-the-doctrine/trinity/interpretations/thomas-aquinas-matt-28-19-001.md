## Quotation

> Although there are three personal names of the three Persons, there is but one essential name. Now the Divine power which works in Baptism, pertains to the Essence; and therefore we say, "in the name," and not, "in the names."

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae III, q. 66, a. 5, reply to Objection 6
- **Language:** English
- **Translation:** Fathers of the English Dominican Province
- **Edition:** English translation by the Fathers of the English Dominican Province (1920), New Advent online edition
- **Reproducibility:** public-domain

## Explanation

Aquinas asks whether 'I baptize thee in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost' is a suitable form of Baptism. The form is Christ's command in Matthew 28:19. In reply to the objection that three personal names should take the plural 'names', Aquinas argues that although there are three personal names of the three persons, there is but one essential name, and the divine power working in Baptism pertains to the essence; therefore the singular 'in the name' is correct. The singular 'name' shared by the three is, for Aquinas, the grammatical sign of the unity of essence.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads the baptismal formula of Matthew 28:19 as naming three persons under one name, and takes the singular 'name' as the sign of one essence. (A) The text commands baptism 'in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit'; it does not state that the three are one essence or use the language of 'essential name'. (B) The step from the singular 'name' to unity of essence is an inference, which Aquinas presents as a grammatical argument about the form. (D) The essence/person distinction in this form is Aquinas's scholastic formulation and must not be presented as the wording of Matthew 28:19.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Tertia Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1272-1273 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article treats the sacramental form of Baptism; Aquinas also argues in q. 66, a. 6 that Christ commanded Baptism to be given with the invocation of the Trinity, and that at Christ's own baptism the Trinity was present in sensible signs (Father in the voice, Son in the human nature, Holy Ghost in the dove).
