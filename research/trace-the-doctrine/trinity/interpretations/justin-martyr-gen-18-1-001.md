## Quotation

> Moses, then, the blessed and faithful servant of God, declares that He who appeared to Abraham under the oak in Mamre is God, sent with the two angels in His company to judge Sodom by Another who remains ever in the supercelestial places, invisible to all men, holding personal intercourse with none, whom we believe to be Maker and Father of all things

## Explanation

Justin argues that the one who appeared to Abraham at Mamre (Gen 18:1) is God, yet distinct from the Father, who remains in the supercelestial places and holds personal intercourse with none. The visible God who appears and speaks is, for Justin, the Son/Word, sent by the Father to judge Sodom.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Justin reads Genesis 18:1 as a theophany of the Son, distinct from the Father. The text itself (row A) says the LORD appeared to Abraham, without distinguishing persons; the identification of the visible God with the Son is Justin's reading, not the text's explicit statement, and it is not the later Nicene doctrine (row D).

## Historical context

Dialogue with Trypho, written c. 155-160 (range), is Justin's defence of Christianity in dialogue with the Jew Trypho, arguing from the Old Testament that Jesus is the Christ and is divine. The chapters cited here (56, 62) belong to the section in which Justin argues that the Scriptures speak of another God and Lord, distinct from the Maker yet subject to him.
