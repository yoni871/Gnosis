## Quotation

> It is said (1 John 5:7): "There are three who bear witness in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost."

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 36, a. 1, sed contra
- **Language:** English

## Explanation

Aquinas asks whether 'Holy Ghost' is the proper name of one divine person. He answers yes, citing 1 John 5:7 ('There are three who bear witness in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost') as the scriptural naming of three, and Augustine's 'Three persons' as the answer to 'Three what?'. For Aquinas, the verse names the Holy Ghost as one of the three persons. TEXTUAL FLAG: 1 John 5:7 in this form is the Johannine Comma, absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts and omitted in modern critical editions (the ESV omits it; the KJV includes it). Aquinas, like the medieval Latin tradition generally, cites the verse as it stood in the Latin Vulgate text he used. The Comma must not be asserted as original to John's epistle; what is recorded here is Aquinas's medieval use of the verse as he received it.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads 1 John 5:7 as naming the Holy Ghost as one of three persons who bear witness in heaven, and takes it as scriptural warrant that 'Holy Ghost' is the proper name of one divine person. (A) The verse as Aquinas cites it names Father, Word, and Holy Ghost as three witnesses; the Comma's originality is disputed (see notes) and must not be asserted. (D) The scholastic account of 'Holy Ghost' as the proper name of the person proceeding by way of love is Aquinas's formulation and must not be presented as the wording of the verse.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article opens Aquinas's treatment of the person of the Holy Ghost (q. 36), in a Latin tradition that read 1 John 5:7 with the Comma as part of the biblical text.

## Researcher notes

TEXTUAL DISPUTE FLAGGED: the Johannine Comma (1 John 5:7) is not original to the epistle on the evidence of the earliest Greek manuscripts; modern critical editions omit it. Aquinas cites it as Scripture because it stood in the Vulgate text he used. The Fourth Lateran Council (1215), Canon 2, likewise quotes 1 John 5:7-8, adding of v. 8 'as it is found in some codices'. Do not present the Comma as original.
