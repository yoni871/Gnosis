## Quotation

> It is said (Galatians 4:4): "When the fullness of the time was come, God sent His Son."

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 43, a. 2, sed contra

## Explanation

Aquinas asks whether mission is eternal or only temporal. Against the claim that mission could be eternal, he cites Galatians 4:4: 'When the fullness of the time was come, God sent His Son.' Mission, for Aquinas, is the eternal procession with the addition of a temporal term: the Son is sent in time by becoming man (visible mission) and by dwelling in man by grace (invisible mission), while generation and spiration are exclusively eternal.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads Galatians 4:4 as showing that the Son's mission is temporal: God sent His Son when the fullness of time came. (A) The text states that God sent His Son, born of a woman, in the fullness of time; it does not distinguish mission from procession or use the scholastic vocabulary of eternal versus temporal terms. (D) The distinction between eternal procession and temporal mission is Aquinas's scholastic formulation and must not be presented as the wording of Galatians 4:4.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article belongs to q. 43 on the mission of the divine persons, distinguishing the eternal processions from the temporal missions.
