## Quotation
> and if, besides, the Apostle Paul says, “Whose are the fathers, and of whom Christ came according to the flesh, who is over all, God blessed for evermore,” writing in his epistles; […]

- Location: Treatise Concerning the Trinity, ch. 13

- Language: English

## Explanation

Novatian cites Paul's words in Romans 9:5, 'who is over all, God blessed for evermore', as apostolic testimony that Christ, who came according to the flesh, is God. It closes his series of New Testament proofs in chapter 13, after which he concludes, 'reasonably Christ is God.' The ellipsis marks the continuation of the sentence.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Novatian reads Romans 9:5 as Paul calling Christ 'God over all'. (A) The verse's punctuation and reference are disputed in modern scholarship (whether 'God blessed for evermore' refers to Christ or is a separate doxology to God the Father); Novatian's reading takes it as referring to Christ, and that attribution to Novatian is what is recorded here. (D) The reading predates Nicaea.

## Historical context

Treatise Concerning the Trinity, c. 250 (approximate), ch. 13, concluding the chapter's proof that Christ is God.
