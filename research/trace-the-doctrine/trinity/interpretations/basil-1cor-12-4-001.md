## Quotation
> For, it is said, there are diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit; and differences of administrations, but the same Lord; and there are diversities of operations, but it is the same God which works all in all.

- Location: On the Holy Spirit, ch. 16, §37
- Translation: Blomfield Jackson
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 8
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

In the same section, Basil quotes 1 Corinthians 12:4-6 (with 12:11) to show the joint operation of Spirit, Lord, and God: God works the differences of operations, the Lord the diversities of administrations, while the Holy Spirit is present too, dispensing the gifts. The apostle names Spirit first, Son second, and God the Father third, which Basil insists does not reverse their rank; the order follows our habits of thought when receiving gifts.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Basil reads 1 Corinthians 12:4-6 as showing the Spirit, the Lord, and God working inseparably together in the distribution of gifts. (B) The inference that inseparable operation implies inseparability of nature is Basil's, labelled as such. (D) Basil is writing between Nicaea (325) and Constantinople (381); the passage supports the Spirit's divine rank without stating the later creedal formula.

## Historical context

On the Holy Spirit, c. 375 (approximate), ch. 16, §37, against the Pneumatomachians.
