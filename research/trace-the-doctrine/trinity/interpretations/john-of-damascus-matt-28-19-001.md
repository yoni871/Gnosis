## Explanation

In the chapter 'Concerning the Holy Trinity', John of Damascus states what Christians believe: in one God, in Father and Son and Holy Spirit. He appeals to Matthew 28:19 as the Lord's command to baptise in the name of the three, and treats the single name as the warrant for confessing one God in three persons.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) John of Damascus reads Matthew 28:19 as the command to baptise into the faith in Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and as the warrant for confessing one God in three persons. (A) The text commands baptism in the name (singular) of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit; it does not use the word Trinity or state a doctrine of one essence. (B) The conclusion that the single name shows one God in three persons is John's inference. (D) The formulated doctrine is later and is not read back into the commission.

## Historical context

An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith was written c. 730-743 (range; exact date not established). Book I, ch. 8 ('Concerning the Holy Trinity') is a compendium of earlier Greek Fathers' teaching on the Trinity, and sets out the Eastern doctrine of the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father alone.
