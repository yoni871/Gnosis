## Explanation

In First Apology 6 Justin denies the charge of atheism: Christians are 'atheists' only toward the pagan gods, while they worship the Father, the Son, and the prophetic Spirit. The sentence also names the host of good angels, a feature of Justin's list that later readers have found puzzling; it is reproduced here verbatim and not smoothed over.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Justin names Father, Son, and Spirit together in worship, paralleling the threefold pattern of 2 Corinthians 13:14 (the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit). He does not quote 2 Corinthians 13:14 here; the link is a doctrinal connection drawn by the researcher, labelled as such (row B). Justin's language - including the angels and 'the Son who came forth from Him' - is not the later Nicene formulation (row D).

## Historical context

First Apology, written c. 150-155 (range) and addressed to Emperor Antoninus Pius, defends Christians against charges of atheism and immorality and describes Christian worship, including baptism (ch. 61) and the refutation of atheism (ch. 6).
