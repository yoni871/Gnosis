## Quotation

> Of some, namely, the rational creature (He is the Father), by reason of the likeness of His image, according to Deuteronomy 32:6: "Is He not thy Father, who possessed, and made, and created thee?"

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 33, a. 3, respondeo

## Explanation

Aquinas argues that 'Father' is applied to God first as a personal name (the Father of the Son from eternity) and only secondarily as an essential name (God as Father of creatures). He illustrates the secondary, creaturely sense with Deuteronomy 32:6: God is called Father of the rational creature 'by reason of the likeness of His image', because He possessed, made, and created them. The verse is used for the creaturely, not the intra-Trinitarian, sense of fatherhood.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads Deuteronomy 32:6 as speaking of God's fatherhood toward the rational creature, by likeness of image, and explicitly distinguishes this from the personal sense in which the Father is Father of the Son. (A) The text calls God Israel's father as creator and founder of the nation; it does not name the Father as a distinct divine person. (D) The distinction between personal and essential uses of 'Father' is Aquinas's scholastic formulation and must not be read back into the Song of Moses.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article treats the person of the Father (q. 33) and distinguishes personal from essential uses of 'Father'.

## Researcher notes

Aquinas cites Deuteronomy 32:6 in the Douay wording ('who possessed, and made, and created thee'); the ESV renders 'your father, who created you'. The wording difference does not affect the point recorded here.
