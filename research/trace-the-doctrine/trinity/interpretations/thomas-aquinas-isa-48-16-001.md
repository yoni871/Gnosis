## Quotation

> The Son is sent by the Holy Ghost, according to Isaiah 48:16, "Now the Lord God hath sent Me and His Spirit."

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 43, a. 8, sed contra
- **Language:** English
- **Translation:** Fathers of the English Dominican Province
- **Edition:** English translation by the Fathers of the English Dominican Province (1920), New Advent online edition
- **Reproducibility:** public-domain

## Explanation

Aquinas asks whether a divine person is sent only by the person whence He proceeds eternally. Against that view he cites Isaiah 48:16: 'Now the Lord God hath sent Me and His Spirit' — the Son is sent by the Holy Ghost, although the Son does not proceed from the Holy Ghost. Aquinas resolves the difficulty by distinguishing two senses of 'sending': as designating the principle of the person sent, a person is sent only by the one whence He proceeds; as designating the principle of the effect implied in the mission, the whole Trinity sends the person sent.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads Isaiah 48:16 as the Son being sent by the Holy Ghost, and uses it to distinguish sending as principle of the person from sending as principle of the mission's effect. (A) The text states that the Lord GOD has sent the speaker and His Spirit; it does not name the speaker as the Son and does not state a doctrine of mission. (B) Identifying the speaker with the Son is an inference, which Aquinas makes without discussion in this article. (D) The mission/procession distinction is Aquinas's scholastic formulation and must not be presented as the wording of Isaiah 48:16.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article closes q. 43 on the mission of the divine persons, drawing on Augustine's De Trinitate II and IV.

## Researcher notes

Aquinas cites Isaiah 48:16 in the Douay wording ('Now the Lord God hath sent Me and His Spirit'); the ESV renders 'the Lord GOD has sent me, and his Spirit'. The identity of the speaker ('me') is not stated in the verse; Aquinas reads the speaker as the Son.
