## Quotation

> But the Son of God is the Logos of the Father, in idea and in operation; for after the pattern of Him and by Him were all things made, the Father and the Son being one. And, the Son being in the Father and the Father in the Son, in oneness and power of spirit, the understanding and reason ... of the Father is the Son of God.

## Explanation

In Plea 10 Athenagoras argues the Son is the Logos of the Father, in idea and in operation; all things were made after his pattern and by him. He also describes the Holy Spirit as an effluence of God, and concludes that Christians who speak of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, declaring 'their power in union and their distinction in order,' should not be called atheists.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Athenagoras's 'the Father and the Son being one' and 'the Son being in the Father and the Father in the Son' echo the oneness of John 10:30 ('I and the Father are one'), though he does not quote the verse here; the link is a doctrinal connection drawn by the researcher (row B). His language - the Son as 'the first product of the Father, not as having been brought into existence' - is his own philosophical account, not the later Nicene formulation (row D).

## Historical context

A Plea for the Christians, written c. 176-177 (range), is a petition to the Emperors Marcus Aurelius and Commodus defending Christians against charges of atheism, cannibalism, and immorality. Chapter 10 states the Christian doctrine of God against the charge of atheism.
