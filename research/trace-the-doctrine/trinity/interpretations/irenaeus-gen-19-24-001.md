## Quotation

## Explanation

In the same passage as his Psalm 110:1 argument, Irenaeus cites the destruction of Sodom: the Son, who had also been talking with Abraham, received power to judge the Sodomites. The two 'Lords' of the verse are, for Irenaeus, the Father in heaven and the Son who executes judgment.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Irenaeus reads Genesis 19:24 as the Son judging Sodom by power received from the Father. The text itself (row A) says the LORD rained fire from the LORD; the Father/Son identification is Irenaeus's reading, and it is not the later Nicene doctrine (row D).

## Historical context

Against Heresies, written c. 175-185 (range), is Irenaeus's refutation of Valentinian Gnosticism. Book III argues from Scripture that the Creator is the one true God and Father of Jesus Christ; Book IV argues that the one God formed all things by means of the Word and the Holy Spirit.
