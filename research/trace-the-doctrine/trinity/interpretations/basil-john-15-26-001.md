## Quotation
> You are therefore to perceive three, the Lord who gives the order, the Word who creates, and the Spirit who confirms.

- Location: On the Holy Spirit, ch. 16, §38
- Translation: Blomfield Jackson
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 8
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Immediately after identifying the Word of John 1:1 and the Spirit of truth who proceeds from the Father (John 15:26), Basil concludes that in creation one is 'to perceive three, the Lord who gives the order, the Word who creates, and the Spirit who confirms', where confirmation is perfecting according to holiness, and 'there is no sanctification without the Spirit'.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Basil reads John 15:26 ('the Spirit of truth which proceeds from the Father') as naming the Spirit who confirms and perfects creation, distinct from the Lord who orders and the Word who creates. (B) The inference from the Spirit's procession and perfecting work to his inseparability from Father and Son is Basil's, labelled as such. (D) Basil writes before Constantinople (381), whose creed would take up the same procession language ('who proceedeth from the Father'); Basil's text is a source for that formulation, not a statement of it.

## Historical context

On the Holy Spirit, c. 375 (approximate), ch. 16, §38, on the fellowship of the Spirit with Father and Son in creation.
