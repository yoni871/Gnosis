## Quotation

> In like manner also the three days which were before the luminaries, are types of the Trinity, of God, and His Word, and His wisdom. And the fourth is the type of man, who needs light, that so there may be God, the Word, wisdom, man.

## Explanation

In To Autolycus II.15 Theophilus allegorises the fourth day: the sun is a type of God and the moon of man, while the three days before the luminaries prefigure the Trinity - God, his Word, and his Wisdom. His 'Trinity' is God, Word, and Wisdom; he does not name the Holy Spirit here, and Wisdom is elsewhere in his work also a title of the Son.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Theophilus reads the Genesis 1 creation days typologically. The Genesis text (row A) says nothing of a Trinity; the typology is Theophilus's, and his triad of God, Word, and Wisdom is not the later Nicene Trinity of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit (row D) - the difference must be stated, not smoothed over.

## Historical context

To Autolycus, written c. 180 (approximate), is Theophilus's apology addressed to his pagan friend Autolycus. Book II walks through Genesis 1 day by day with allegorising commentary, and explains how the uncontainable Father can be said to walk in Paradise (ch. 22).
