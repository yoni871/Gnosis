## Quotation

> Certainly, in that it is of the plural number, the word our would not be rightly used if man were made in the image of one person, whether of the Father, or of the Son, or of the Holy Spirit; but because he was made in the image of the Trinity, on that account it is said, After our image.

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series I, Vol. 3, ed. Philip Schaff (1887)
- Translation: Arthur West Haddan
- Location: Book XII, chapter 6, section 6
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

Augustine is rejecting the opinion that the image of the Trinity in man is completed in husband, wife, and offspring. He argues from the plural 'our' in Genesis 1:26 that man was made in the image of the whole Trinity, and from the singular 'God created man in the image of God' (Gen. 1:27) that the Trinity is one God, not three Gods.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads the plural 'let us make' / 'our image' in Genesis 1:26 as showing that man was made in the image of the Trinity. (A) The text itself names no persons and does not state the doctrine; Augustine's reading is reception history, not the text's plain meaning. (B) The step from the unexplained plural to a trinitarian referent is Augustine's inference. (D) The formulated doctrine of one essence in three persons dates from later centuries and is not read back into the verse.

## Historical context

De Trinitate was written 399-422 (range; see works.json). Books I-IV defend the unity and equality of the Trinity from Scripture against those who argued, from texts about the sending and subordination of the Son and the Spirit, that the Son is less than the Father. This passage comes from Book XII, where Augustine seeks the image of God in the mind and rejects a marital/offspring image of the Trinity.
