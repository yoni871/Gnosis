## Quotation
> for in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily, and you are made full in Him, Which is the Head of all principality and power; […]

- Location: On the Trinity, Book I, §13
- Translation: E. W. Watson
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 9 (1899)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Continuing his reading of Colossians 2:8-15 in §13, Hilary cites 'in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily' (Colossians 2:9) as the apostle's inspired teaching against 'philosophy and vain deceit'. Steadfast faith, he says, 'rejects the vain subtleties of philosophic enquiry' and refuses to confine God within the limits of common reason, because in Christ there is the fullness of the Godhead, and 'we must confess that Christ Jesus is none other than God in all the fullness of the Deity.' The ellipsis marks the continuation of the Colossians quotation.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Hilary reads Colossians 2:9 as Paul's statement that the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily in Christ, and therefore that Christ Jesus is none other than God in all the fullness of the Deity. (D) The reading defends the post-Nicene faith against the Arian claim that the Son is a creature; it is Hilary's argument, not a quotation of the creed.

## Historical context

On the Trinity, Book I, §13, c. 356-359 (range), written in exile. The section warns against judging Christ 'after the rudiments of the world'.
