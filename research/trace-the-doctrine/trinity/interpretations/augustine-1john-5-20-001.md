## Quotation

> And the same John most expressly affirms this in his epistle: For we know that the Son of God has come, and has given us an understanding, that we may know the true God, and that we may be in His true Son Jesus Christ. This is the true God, and eternal life.

- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

Augustine cites 1 John 5:20 as the most express testimony that the Son is very God: John says the Son of God has come and has given us understanding, that we may be in His true Son Jesus Christ, and then adds 'This is the true God, and eternal life', which Augustine takes to refer to Jesus Christ Himself.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads 1 John 5:20 as expressly affirming that Jesus Christ is the true God and eternal life. (A) The text states that we are in His true Son Jesus Christ and that 'This is the true God, and eternal life'; the referent of 'this' is debated, though Augustine takes it to be Christ. (B) Taking 'This is the true God' to refer to the Son is Augustine's reading of the verse's syntax. (D) The consubstantiality language is a later formulation applied to the text.

## Historical context

De Trinitate was written 399-422 (range; see works.json). Books I-IV defend the unity and equality of the Trinity from Scripture against those who argued, from texts about the sending and subordination of the Son and the Spirit, that the Son is less than the Father. This passage comes from Book I, chapter 6, where Augustine collects scriptural testimonies that the Son is very God, of the same substance with the Father.
