## Quotation

> I would not have thee mistake that place in the epistle of John the apostle where he saith, 'There are three witnesses: the Spirit, and the water, and the blood: and the three are one.' Lest haply thou say that the Spirit and the water and the blood are diverse substances, and yet it is said, 'the three are one:' for this cause I have admonished thee, that thou mistake not the matter. For these are mystical expressions, in which the point always to be considered is, not what the actual things are, but what they denote as signs.

## Explanation

Maximinus had cited 1 John 5:7-8 to argue that the Father and the Son are not one substance, since the Spirit, the water, and the blood are said to be one although they are diverse things. Augustine replies that the verse names three witnesses, the Spirit, the water, and the blood, and that these are mystical expressions: the point is not what the actual things are, but what they denote as signs. He then reads them as signifying the Trinity - the Spirit signifying the Father, the blood the Son, and the water the Holy Spirit - so that 'There are Three Witnesses, and the Three are One' can be said of them too.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads the three witnesses of 1 John 5:7-8 allegorically as signifying the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. (A) The verse names the Spirit, the water, and the blood and says the three are one; it does not name the persons of the Trinity. (B) The allegorical identification of the three witnesses with the three persons is Augustine's reading, not the text's claim. (D) The doctrine of the Trinity is not stated by the verse. TEXTUAL DISPUTE: Augustine's text of 1 John 5:7-8 contains only the earthly witnesses (Spirit, water, blood). The heavenly-witnesses clause (the 'Comma Johanneum' - the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost) does not appear in his text; the same source page's footnote 56 states that the clause 'appears to be wholly unknown to St. Augustin'. The Comma is therefore NOT asserted here as original.

## Historical context

Contra Maximinum was written 427-428 (range), shortly after Augustine's public debate at Hippo with Maximinus, a Homoian Arian bishop. In Book II, chapter 22, Augustine answers Maximinus's use of 1 John 5:7-8 against the unity of substance of Father and Son.
