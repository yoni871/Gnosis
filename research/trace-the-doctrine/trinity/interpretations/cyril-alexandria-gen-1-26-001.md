## Quotation

> But now the writer of the Book, not saying this indeed, but allotting the creation to the plural number and adding Our image, well-nigh with clear and mighty voice proclaims the enumeration of the Holy Trinity to be above One.

- Edition: P. E. Pusey edition/translation (Oxford, 1872), as reproduced at elpenor.org

## Explanation

Cyril is opening his commentary on John 1:1 and answering the question who created the world. He cites Genesis 1:26, where God says 'Let us make man in our image', and argues that the plural 'Let us' and 'Our image' shows that the work of creation is not the Father's alone but is shared with the Son and the Holy Spirit - the Holy Trinity, 'above One'.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Cyril reads the plural 'Let us make' / 'Our image' in Genesis 1:26 as proclaiming the enumeration of the Holy Trinity to be above One. (A) The Genesis text names no persons and does not state the doctrine; it speaks of God in the plural. (B) The step from the unexplained plural to the Trinity is Cyril's inference. (D) The formulated doctrine is later and is not read back into the verse.

## Historical context

The Commentary on the Gospel according to John was written 425-428 (range), before the outbreak of the Nestorian controversy. Cyril argues throughout against Arian and Eunomian teaching about the Son and the Holy Spirit.
