## Explanation

In III.17 Irenaeus argues the apostles taught that the Holy Spirit, not a Gnostic Christ or Saviour, descended on Jesus; the Spirit is then given to the Church, and baptism in the threefold name is the power of regeneration into God, renewing men from their old habits into the newness of Christ.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Irenaeus cites the commission verbatim as evidence of the Spirit's work and of baptismal regeneration. The verse (row A) is the commission itself; Irenaeus's framing is anti-Gnostic, not a later creedal statement (row D).

## Historical context

Against Heresies, written c. 175-185 (range), is Irenaeus's refutation of Valentinian Gnosticism. Book III argues from Scripture that the Creator is the one true God and Father of Jesus Christ; Book IV argues that the one God formed all things by means of the Word and the Holy Spirit.
