## Quotation

> It is said (Matthew 3:16) that, when our Lord was baptized, the Holy Ghost descended upon Him in the shape of a dove.

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 43, a. 7, sed contra

## Explanation

Aquinas asks whether it is fitting for the Holy Ghost to be sent visibly. He answers yes, citing Matthew 3:16: at the Lord's baptism the Holy Ghost descended upon Him in the shape of a dove. The visible mission of the Spirit, for Aquinas, manifests the invisible mission: the Son is sent visibly as the author of sanctification, the Holy Ghost as the sign of sanctification. In his reply to the objections he adds that the Father's voice spoke at the baptism, 'This is My beloved Son' (Matthew 3:17).

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads Matthew 3:16 as the visible mission of the Holy Ghost: the Spirit descends on the Son at His baptism as the sign of sanctification. (A) The text states that the Spirit of God descended like a dove and rested on Jesus, with the Father's voice speaking; it does not use the scholastic vocabulary of 'visible mission' or 'procession'. (D) The visible/invisible mission framework is Aquinas's scholastic formulation, linking the eternal processions to temporal sending, and must not be presented as the wording of Matthew 3:16.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article belongs to q. 43 on the mission of the divine persons, which links the eternal processions to the temporal missions (sending) of Son and Spirit.
