## Quotation

> I and the Father are One. By the word 'One' He signifies the Sameness of their Essence: and by the word 'are' He severs into two that which is understood, and again binds them up into One Godhead.

- Edition: P. E. Pusey edition/translation (Oxford, 1872), as reproduced at elpenor.org
- Translation: P. E. Pusey

## Explanation

Cyril comments on John 10:29-30, where Christ says 'I and the Father are One'. He argues that 'One' signifies the sameness of the Father's and the Son's essence, while 'are' keeps the two distinct - the Word is not the Father - and yet binds them into one Godhead. The verse is used against the Arians to show that the Son is not a creature but is one in essence with the Father.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Cyril reads John 10:30 as teaching the sameness of the Father's and the Son's essence while preserving two persons. (A) The text states 'I and the Father are one'; it does not use the language of essence (ousia) or Godhead. (B) The step from 'are one' to 'sameness of essence' is Cyril's inference, governed by his argument against Arianism. (D) The essence vocabulary is a later formulation applied to the text.

## Historical context

The Commentary on the Gospel according to John was written 425-428 (range), before the outbreak of the Nestorian controversy. Cyril argues throughout against Arian and Eunomian teaching about the Son and the Holy Spirit.
