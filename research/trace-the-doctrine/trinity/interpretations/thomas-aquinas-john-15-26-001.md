## Quotation

> But in the Sacred Scripture we are not told that the Holy Ghost proceeds from the Son; but only that He proceeds from the Father, as appears from John 15:26: "The Spirit of truth, Who proceeds from the Father."

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 36, a. 2, Objection 1
- **Language:** English

## Explanation

In q. 36, a. 2, Aquinas asks whether the Holy Ghost proceeds from the Son. The opening objection states the Eastern case: Scripture says only that the Holy Ghost proceeds from the Father, 'as appears from John 15:26: "The Spirit of truth, Who proceeds from the Father."' Aquinas answers in the respondeo that the Holy Ghost must be from the Son, because otherwise the Son and the Holy Ghost could not be personally distinguished, since the divine persons are distinguished only by opposite relations. The quotation records the Johannine text as Aquinas himself presents it in this article; the reading he defends is the filioque, and the Eastern objection is recorded in alternativeReadings.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas treats John 15:26 as the text stating that the Spirit proceeds from the Father, and argues (in the respondeo of the same article) that the Spirit must also be from the Son if Son and Spirit are to be personally distinct. (A) The text states that the Spirit of truth proceeds from the Father; it does not state that the Spirit proceeds from the Son. (B) The step from 'proceeds from the Father' to 'proceeds from the Father and the Son' is Aquinas's inference from the principle that persons are distinguished only by opposite relations. (D) The filioque formulation is a later Western formulation; the East-West disagreement over it is recorded in alternativeReadings and must not be harmonised away.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article is the central scholastic treatment of the filioque, written in the aftermath of the 1054 schism and the long East-West dispute over the Spirit's procession; Aquinas also cites the Council of Constantinople's creed and Damascene in the objections.

## Alternative readings

- **john-of-damascus**: John of Damascus, as reported by Aquinas, says the Holy Ghost is from the Father and is named the Spirit of the Father, but is not said to be from the Son, although He is named the Spirit of the Son. Aquinas records this Eastern position in the objection and then argues against it in the respondeo, holding that the Holy Ghost is from the Son. The disagreement is therefore recorded here as attested, not harmonised.
