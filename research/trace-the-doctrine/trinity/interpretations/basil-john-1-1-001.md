## Quotation
> The Word then is not a mere significant impression on the air, borne by the organs of speech; nor is the Spirit of His mouth a vapour, emitted by the organs of respiration; but the Word is He who was with God in the beginning and was God, and the Spirit of the mouth of God is the Spirit of truth which proceeds from the Father.

- Location: On the Holy Spirit, ch. 16, §38
- Translation: Blomfield Jackson
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 8
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Discussing creation, Basil distinguishes the Father as original cause, the Son as creative cause, and the Spirit as perfecting cause, and supports this with Psalm 33:6 ('By the word of the Lord were the heavens made, and all the host of them by the breath of His mouth'). The Word, he says, is not a mere sound but 'He who was with God in the beginning and was God' (John 1:1), and the Spirit of God's mouth is the Spirit of truth who proceeds from the Father (John 15:26).

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Basil reads John 1:1 as identifying the Word as a subsistent person, God with God in the beginning, not a mere utterance; and he joins it to John 15:26 for the Spirit. (D) Basil's three-cause scheme (Father original cause, Son creative cause, Spirit perfecting cause) is his own pre-381 formulation; it is not the Nicene Creed's language, and it must not be read as if it were.

## Historical context

On the Holy Spirit, c. 375 (approximate), ch. 16, §38, on the fellowship of the Spirit with Father and Son in creation.

## Alternative readings

- arius (arius-thalia): Arius read the Word as a creature made out of nothing, who 'once was not' and 'was not before His origination'; the Word is God only in name, by participation of grace, and is 'alien and unlike in all things to the Father's essence'. See arius-john-1-1-001.
