## Quotation

> Some things, then, are so put in the Scriptures concerning the Father and the Son, as to intimate the unity and equality of their substance; as, for instance, I and the Father are one.

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series I, Vol. 3, ed. Philip Schaff (1887)
- Translation: Arthur West Haddan
- Location: Book II, chapter 1, section 3
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

Augustine sets out his rule for reading scriptural language about the Son: some texts intimate the unity and equality of substance, some speak of the Son as less according to the form of a servant, and some only intimate that He is of the Father. John 10:30 is his first example of the first class.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads John 10:30 as intimating the unity and equality of the Father's and the Son's substance. (A) The text states 'I and the Father are one'; it does not use the language of substance or essence. (B) The step from 'are one' to 'one substance, equal' is Augustine's inference, governed by his stated rule of reading. (D) The substance vocabulary is a later formulation applied to the text, not the text's own wording.

## Historical context

De Trinitate was written 399-422 (range; see works.json). Books I-IV defend the unity and equality of the Trinity from Scripture against those who argued, from texts about the sending and subordination of the Son and the Spirit, that the Son is less than the Father. This passage comes from Book II, chapter 1, where Augustine states the double rule for understanding scriptural modes of speech concerning the Son.
