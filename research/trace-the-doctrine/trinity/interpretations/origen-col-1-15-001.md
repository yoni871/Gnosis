## Quotation
> He is also styled First-born, as the apostle has declared: “who is the first-born of every creature.” The first-born, however, is not by nature a different person from the Wisdom, but one and the same.

- Location: De Principiis, Book I, ch. 2, §1
- Translation: Frederick Crombie
- Edition: Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 4 (1885)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Origen identifies the Son under his many names: he is Wisdom (Proverbs 8:22), First-born (Colossians 1:15), and the power and wisdom of God (1 Corinthians 1:24). The First-born is not a different person from the Wisdom, but one and the same: the only-begotten Son. In §5 he adds that Paul calls the Son 'the image of the invisible God' and 'the first-born of every creature'.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Origen reads Colossians 1:15 as naming the Son 'first-born of every creature' while insisting he is one and the same person as the Wisdom, not a different being. (B) Origen's inference that 'first-born' does not make the Son a creature is his theological reading; the verse itself calls him first-born of creation, which Arians later used to argue the opposite. (D) Origen's account is pre-Nicene and subordinationist in places: the Son derives from the Father and is second to him, though Origen insists the generation is without beginning.

## Historical context

De Principiis, c. 220-230 (range), Book I, ch. 2, 'On Christ'. Origen is answering how the Son can be called Wisdom, Word, First-born, and image while remaining one person.
