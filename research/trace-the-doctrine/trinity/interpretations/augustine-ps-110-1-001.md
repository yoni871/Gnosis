## Quotation

> But since it is found that God the Father also is called Lord in many places - for instance, The Lord has said to me, You are my Son; this day have I begotten You; and again, The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand; since also the Holy Spirit is found to be called Lord, as where the apostle says, Now the Lord is that Spirit;

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series I, Vol. 3, ed. Philip Schaff (1887)
- Translation: Arthur West Haddan
- Location: Book II, chapter 10, section 19
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

Augustine is arguing that the name 'Lord' does not belong to the Son alone. He cites Psalm 110:1, where the LORD speaks to 'my Lord', as evidence that God the Father is also called Lord; the same sentence goes on to cite 2 Corinthians 3:17 for the same point about the Spirit.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads Psalm 110:1 as a place where the Father is called Lord, so that 'Lord' is not the Son's exclusive title. (A) The psalm itself is a royal oracle to a Davidic lord; it does not identify the second lord or state the doctrine. (B) Identifying the speaking LORD with the Father is Augustine's reading. (D) The doctrine of the Son's session at the Father's right hand is a later formulation and is not read back into the psalm.

## Historical context

De Trinitate was written 399-422 (range; see works.json). Books I-IV defend the unity and equality of the Trinity from Scripture against those who argued, from texts about the sending and subordination of the Son and the Spirit, that the Son is less than the Father. This passage comes from Book II, chapter 10, where Augustine asks whether the Father, the Son, the Holy Spirit, or the Trinity itself appeared to the fathers.
