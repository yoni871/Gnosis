## Explanation

In First Apology 61 Justin explains baptism to the emperor: converts who believe and repent are brought to water and 'regenerated' in the threefold name. He presents this as the practice learned from the apostles, and adds that the name of God the Father, the name of Jesus Christ, and the name of the Holy Ghost are pronounced over the one being washed.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Justin's baptismal practice uses the threefold name of Matthew 28:19. The passage itself (row A) is the risen Jesus' commission to baptize in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit; Justin's report is evidence about second-century baptismal practice and belief, not a doctrinal treatise, and he uses no later Trinitarian terminology (row D).

## Historical context

First Apology, written c. 150-155 (range) and addressed to Emperor Antoninus Pius, defends Christians against charges of atheism and immorality and describes Christian worship, including baptism (ch. 61) and the refutation of atheism (ch. 6).
