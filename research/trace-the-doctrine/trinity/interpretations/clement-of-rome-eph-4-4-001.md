## Explanation

In 1 Clement 46 Clement rebukes the Corinthians' strife: they who share one God, one Christ, and one Spirit of grace, and one calling, should not tear apart the members of Christ. The letter does not quote Ephesians 4:4 verbatim; the parallel is in the threefold 'one God, one Christ, one Spirit' and 'one calling.'

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Clement's threefold unity formula is consistent with, and likely echoes, Ephesians 4:4-6. The claim is a doctrinal connection (row B inference by the researcher), labelled as such: Clement does not cite Ephesians here, and the passage itself (row A) is about the one body, Spirit, hope, and calling. This is not a Trinitarian creed (row D).

## Historical context

First Epistle of Clement to the Corinthians, written c. 90-100 (range), is a letter from the church of Rome to the church of Corinth urging an end to the schism caused by the deposition of presbyters. Chapter 46 appeals to the Corinthians' shared unity as the ground for peace. The letter itself names no author; it is traditionally ascribed to Clement of Rome.
