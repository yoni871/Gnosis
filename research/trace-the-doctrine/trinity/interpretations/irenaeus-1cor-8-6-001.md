## Explanation

In III.6.5 Irenaeus cites Paul against the Gnostic multiplication of gods: Paul separates 'those which are indeed called gods, but which are none' from the one God the Father, and confesses in the most decided manner one Lord Jesus Christ.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is Irenaeus's use of the passage (row C) against Gnostic aeons: one God the Father and one Lord Jesus Christ, with all things from the Father through the Son. The passage itself (row A) states one God and one Lord; it does not state the later doctrine of the Trinity (row D), and Irenaeus does not claim that it does.

## Historical context

Against Heresies, written c. 175-185 (range), is Irenaeus's refutation of Valentinian Gnosticism. Book III argues from Scripture that the Creator is the one true God and Father of Jesus Christ; Book IV argues that the one God formed all things by means of the Word and the Holy Spirit.
