## Quotation
> Nay, it was because He had already His Son close at His side, as a second Person, His own Word, and a third Person also, the Spirit in the Word, that He purposely adopted the plural phrase, “Let us make;” and, “in our image;” and, “become as one of us.”

- Location: Against Praxeas, ch. 12
- Translation: Peter Holmes
- Edition: Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 3 (1885)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Tertullian argues that the plural 'Let us make' in Genesis 1:26 cannot be God speaking to angels (the Jewish reading he rejects) or God speaking to himself as a solitary being; it is God speaking with the Son already at his side as a second Person and the Spirit as a third Person. The plural is therefore, for Tertullian, evidence of a plurality of Persons in the Godhead.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Tertullian reads Genesis 1:26 as the Father speaking to the Son and the Spirit, and so as proof of the plurality of Persons. (A) The text itself does not name Father, Son, and Holy Spirit and does not state the Trinity; Tertullian's reading is reception history, not the text's plain meaning. (D) Tertullian's language is pre-Nicene: he speaks of three Persons of one substance, but he does not use or teach Nicaea's homoousios, and his Son is in some respects subordinate (a portion of the Father's substance, proceeding for the work of creation).

## Historical context

Against Praxeas was written c. 207-213 (range), in Tertullian's Montanist period, against Praxeas, who taught that Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are one and the same Person. The treatise is the earliest Latin work to use trinitas (Trinity) and persona (Person) as theological terms.
