## Quotation

> And we do not speak of the Spirit as from the Son: but yet we call Him the Spirit of the Son. For if any one hath not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of His, saith the divine apostle.

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series II, Vol. 9, ed. Philip Schaff and Henry Wace (1898)
- Translation: S. D. F. Salmond
- Location: Book I, chapter 8
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

John of Damascus states the Eastern doctrine: the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father's essence, not having been generated but simply proceeding, and is not spoken of as from the Son. Nevertheless He is called the Spirit of the Son, because the apostle says that anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to Him (Romans 8:9). For John, 'Spirit of the Son' describes the Spirit's manifestation through the Son, not His eternal procession from the Son.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) John of Damascus reads Romans 8:9 as warrant for calling the Spirit 'the Spirit of the Son' while denying that the Spirit is from the Son. (A) The text states that anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to Him; it does not state a doctrine of the Spirit's procession. (B) The distinction between 'Spirit of the Son' and 'from the Son' is John's theological construction. (D) The East/West difference on procession is a later formulation and is recorded here, not harmonised (see the alternative reading).

## Historical context

An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith was written c. 730-743 (range; exact date not established). Book I, ch. 8 ('Concerning the Holy Trinity') is a compendium of earlier Greek Fathers' teaching on the Trinity, and sets out the Eastern doctrine of the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father alone.

## Alternative readings

- Author: `augustine`; work: `augustine-de-trinitate`.
  Augustine holds that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and from the Son (De Trinitate XV.26.46; II.3.5). Leo I likewise speaks of the Spirit as proceeding 'from both' (Letter 15, ch. II). The East/West difference is recorded, not harmonised.
  Citation: Augustine, On the Holy Trinity, Book XV, ch. 26 (NPNF Series I, Vol. 3); Leo the Great, Letter 15, ch. II (NPNF Series II, Vol. 12).
