## Quotation
> For all these texts have the same force and meaning, a religious one, declarative of the divinity of the Word, even those of them which speak humanly concerning Him, as having become the Son of man.

- Location: Four Discourses Against the Arians, Discourse II, Chapter XIV, section 1 (NPNF2 vol. 4, p. 348; New Advent §1)
- Translation: John Henry Newman and Archibald Robertson
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 4 (1892)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Athanasius lists the texts the Arians misuse to call the Son a creature, including 'He emptied Himself, and took upon Him the form of a servant' (Philippians 2:7), alongside John 1:1, John 1:14, Acts 2:36, Proverbs 8:22, and Hebrews 1:4. He insists all these texts 'have the same force and meaning, a religious one, declarative of the divinity of the Word, even those of them which speak humanly concerning Him, as having become the Son of man': the lowly language belongs to the Word become man, not to the Word as a creature.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Athanasius reads Philippians 2:7 (within the Philippians 2:5-11 hymn) as speaking of the divine Word who became man, so that the self-emptying declares rather than denies his divinity. (D) The reading defends the Nicene settlement: the one who emptied himself is the eternal Word, not a creature exalted for foreseen virtue (the Arian reading).

## Historical context

Four Discourses Against the Arians, Discourse II, Chapter XIV, §1, c. 356-360 (range). The Arians argued from Proverbs 8:22 and Hebrews 3:2 that the Son is a work and a creature; Athanasius answers by re-reading the whole group of texts.
