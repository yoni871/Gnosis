## Quotation

> But when the Comforter has come, whom I will send unto you from the Father, even the Spirit of truth which proceeds from the Father, He shall testify of me? He is said, therefore, not to speak of Himself, in that He proceeds from the Father;

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series I, Vol. 3, ed. Philip Schaff (1887)
- Translation: Arthur West Haddan
- Location: Book II, chapter 3, section 5
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

Augustine is answering the claim that the Spirit must be less because John 16:13 says He shall not speak of Himself. He argues that 'not speaking of Himself' belongs to the Spirit as proceeding from the Father (John 15:26), just as the Son's 'can do nothing of Himself' belongs to Him as being of the Father; in neither case does the language imply inferiority. He also notes that the Spirit 'shall receive of mine' because all things that the Father has are the Son's also.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads John 15:26 as teaching that the Spirit proceeds from the Father, and that His not speaking of Himself marks His procession, not inferiority. (A) The text states that the Spirit of truth proceeds from the Father and will testify of Christ. (B) The inference that procession does not imply inferiority is Augustine's argument. (D) The later Western formulation that the Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son is developed by Augustine elsewhere (De Trinitate XV.26) and must be distinguished from what John 15:26 itself states.

## Historical context

De Trinitate was written 399-422 (range; see works.json). Books I-IV defend the unity and equality of the Trinity from Scripture against those who argued, from texts about the sending and subordination of the Son and the Spirit, that the Son is less than the Father. This passage comes from Book II, chapter 3, where Augustine treats texts about the Holy Spirit that might be taken to imply His inferiority.

## Alternative readings

- Author: `john-of-damascus`; work: `john-of-damascus-exposition-of-the-orthodox-faith`.
  John of Damascus holds that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father alone ('only the Holy Spirit proceedeth from the Father's essence'), not from the Son, while still calling Him the Spirit of the Son. The East/West difference on the Spirit's procession is recorded here, not harmonised.
  Citation: John of Damascus, An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith, Book I, ch. 8 (NPNF Series II, Vol. 9).
