## Quotation
> It did not disperse its faith among a plurality of deities, for it heard that He is God of God; nor did it fall into the error of attributing a difference of nature to this God of God, for it learned that He is full of grace and truth.

- Location: On the Trinity, Book I, §11
- Translation: E. W. Watson
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 9 (1899)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Hilary recounts his own conversion through reading John 1:1-14: his soul learned from the Gospel that the Word was with God and was God, that the Word became flesh, and that his glory was seen as of the Only-begotten from the Father, full of grace and truth. From this he learned that the Creator is 'God of God': faith is not dispersed among many deities, nor is a difference of nature attributed to this God of God. In §10 he quotes the whole of John 1:1-14, and in §13 he argues that the Word's 'was' has no limit of time and no commencement: 'the uncreated Word was in the beginning.'

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Hilary reads John 1:1 as teaching that the Word is God of God, of the same nature as the Father, eternal and uncreated. (D) Hilary writes in exile c. 356-359, after Nicaea, defending the Nicene faith against the Arians, who held the Son to be created; his 'God of God' echoes the Nicene Creed's language without being a quotation of it.

## Historical context

On the Trinity, Book I, §§10-13, c. 356-359 (range), written during Hilary's exile after the Council of Milan (355). Book I is autobiographical, recounting his path from philosophy to faith.

## Alternative readings

- arius (arius-thalia): Arius read the Word as a creature made out of nothing, who 'once was not' and 'was not before His origination'; the Word is God only in name, by participation of grace, and is 'alien and unlike in all things to the Father's essence'. See arius-john-1-1-001.
