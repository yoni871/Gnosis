## Quotation
> The Word is God from God; for ‘the Word was God,’ and again, ‘Of whom are the Fathers, and of whom Christ, who is God over all, blessed forever. Amen’.

- Location: Four Discourses Against the Arians, Discourse IV, §1
- Translation: John Henry Newman and Archibald Robertson
- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, vol. 4 (1892)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Athanasius opens Discourse IV by proving the substantiality of the Word from Scripture: the Word is God from God because John says 'the Word was God', and Paul says Christ is 'God over all' (Romans 9:5). Since Christ is God's Word, Wisdom, Son, and Power, 'but One God is declared in the divine Scriptures': Father and Son are two, yet the Monad of the Godhead is indivisible and inseparable, and the Word is the Father's own Son, 'from Essence Essential and Substantive, and Being from Being'.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Athanasius reads John 1:1 as proof that the Word is God from God, of the Father's own essence, eternal and inseparable from him. (D) This is the Nicene position argued after Nicaea (325): the Word is not a creature and 'once was not' is false. Athanasius's 'from the Father's essence' is the substance of the Nicene homoousios, though the word homoousios itself is not used in this passage.

## Historical context

Four Discourses Against the Arians, Discourse IV, §1, c. 356-360 (range; Discourse IV possibly later). Written against the Arians, who held the Son to be a creature made out of nothing.

## Alternative readings

- arius (arius-thalia): Arius read the Word as a creature made out of nothing, who 'once was not' and 'was not before His origination'; the Word is God only in name, by participation of grace, and is 'alien and unlike in all things to the Father's essence'. See arius-john-1-1-001.
