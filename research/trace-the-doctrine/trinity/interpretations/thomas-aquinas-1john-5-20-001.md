## Quotation

> In this sense neither the Son nor the Holy Ghost would be true God: and this is contrary to what is said of the Son, "That . . . we may be in His true Son. This is true God" (1 John 5:20).

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 27, a. 1, respondeo
- **Language:** English
- **Translation:** Fathers of the English Dominican Province
- **Edition:** English translation by the Fathers of the English Dominican Province (1920), New Advent online edition
- **Reproducibility:** public-domain

## Explanation

Aquinas argues that procession in God must be understood as an inward, intelligible procession, not an outward movement. He rejects the Arian reading, under which the Son proceeds from the Father as His primary creature, because it contradicts 1 John 5:20: the Son is 'true God'. The verse is used as the proof that the Son who proceeds is truly God, not a creature.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads 1 John 5:20 as stating that the Son is 'true God', and uses it to reject the Arian reading of the Son's procession as creaturely. (A) The text states that Jesus Christ 'is the true God and eternal life'; it does not use the language of procession or of the Son's relation to the Father. (D) The scholastic account of procession as an inward intelligible emanation is Aquinas's formulation and must not be presented as the wording of 1 John 5:20.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article opens the Trinitarian treatise by establishing that there is procession in God, against Arius (who made the Son a creature) and Sabellius (who denied real distinction).
