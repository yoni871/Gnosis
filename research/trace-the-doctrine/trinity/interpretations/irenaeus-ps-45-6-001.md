## Quotation

> Your throne, O God, is for ever and ever; the sceptre of Your kingdom is a right sceptre. You have loved righteousness, and hated iniquity: therefore God, Your God, has anointed You. For the Spirit designates both [of them] by the name, of God - both Him who is anointed as Son, and Him who does anoint, that is, the Father.

## Explanation

The psalm's address 'Your throne, O God' is applied by Irenaeus to the Son, while 'God, Your God, has anointed You' shows the Father anointing him; for Irenaeus the Spirit designates both by the name of God - the anointed as Son and the anointer as Father.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Irenaeus reads Psalm 45:6-7 as the Spirit naming both Son and Father God. The psalm itself (row A) is a royal psalm addressed to a king as God and speaking of God anointing him; the Father/Son identification is Irenaeus's reading, not the text's explicit statement, and not the later Nicene formulation (row D).

## Historical context

Against Heresies, written c. 175-185 (range), is Irenaeus's refutation of Valentinian Gnosticism. Book III argues from Scripture that the Creator is the one true God and Father of Jesus Christ; Book IV argues that the one God formed all things by means of the Word and the Holy Spirit.
