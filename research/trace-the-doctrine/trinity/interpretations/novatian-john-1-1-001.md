## Quotation
> Moreover, this Word “was in the beginning with God, and God was the Word.”

- Location: Treatise Concerning the Trinity, ch. 13
- Translation: Robert Ernest Wallis
- Edition: Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 5 (1886)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Novatian cites John 1:1 alongside John 1:3, 1:10-11 and Colossians 1:16 to argue that the Word who became flesh is God: because all things were made by him, he cannot be merely man, who is subsequent to all things, but is God also, since God is prior to all things. The chapter accumulates scriptural proofs that Christ is both man and God.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Novatian reads John 1:1 as stating that the Word is God and was with God in the beginning, and therefore that Christ is God. (D) Novatian's framework is pre-Nicene and subordinationist in structure: the Son derives his being from the Father, who alone is without origin; Novatian does not teach the Nicene homoousios. His reading supports the Son's deity, not the later formulated doctrine as such.

## Historical context

Treatise Concerning the Trinity, c. 250 (approximate), ch. 13, 'That the Same Truth is Proved from the Sacred Writings of the New Covenant'. Written against Sabellian modalism; the oldest extant treatise devoted specifically to the Trinity.

## Alternative readings

- arius (arius-thalia): Arius read the Word as a creature made out of nothing, who 'once was not' and 'was not before His origination'; the Word is God only in name, by participation of grace, and is 'alien and unlike in all things to the Father's essence'. See arius-john-1-1-001.
