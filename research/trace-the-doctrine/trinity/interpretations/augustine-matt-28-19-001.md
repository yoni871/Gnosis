## Quotation

> we ought not to doubt that the same Holy Spirit was given when Jesus breathed upon them, of whom He by and by says, Go, baptize all nations in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, where this Trinity is especially commended to us.

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series I, Vol. 3, ed. Philip Schaff (1887)
- Translation: Arthur West Haddan
- Location: Book XV, chapter 26, section 46
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

Augustine is arguing that the Holy Spirit was given when Jesus breathed on the disciples (John 20:22) and again at Pentecost. He appeals to the baptismal formula of Matthew 28:19, where the three are named under the single name, as the place where 'this Trinity is especially commended to us'.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads Matthew 28:19 as the place where the Trinity is especially commended, the three persons being named under one name. (A) The text commands baptism in the name (singular) of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit; it does not use the word Trinity or state a doctrine of one essence. (B) The conclusion that the single name shows one God in three persons is Augustine's inference. (D) The formulated doctrine is later and is not read back into the commission.

## Historical context

De Trinitate was written 399-422 (range; see works.json). Books I-IV defend the unity and equality of the Trinity from Scripture against those who argued, from texts about the sending and subordination of the Son and the Spirit, that the Son is less than the Father. This passage comes from Book XV, chapter 26, where Augustine treats the giving of the Holy Spirit.
