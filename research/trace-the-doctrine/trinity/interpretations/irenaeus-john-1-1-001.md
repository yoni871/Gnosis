## Explanation

Against Cerinthus and the Nicolaitans, who separated the Creator from the Father of the Lord, Irenaeus argues that John begins his Gospel by teaching one Almighty God who made all things by his Word, and that the Word also bestowed salvation on men included in the creation.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Irenaeus reads John 1:1 as establishing that the Creator and the Father of Jesus Christ are one God, acting by his Word. The verse itself (row A) identifies the Word as God and as the agent of creation; the anti-Gnostic application is Irenaeus's, and the passage is not presented as a later creedal statement (row D).

## Historical context

Against Heresies, written c. 175-185 (range), is Irenaeus's refutation of Valentinian Gnosticism. Book III argues from Scripture that the Creator is the one true God and Father of Jesus Christ; Book IV argues that the one God formed all things by means of the Word and the Holy Spirit.

## Alternatives and disagreements
