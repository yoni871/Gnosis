## Quotation

> There is one Physician who is possessed both of flesh and spirit; both made and not made; God existing in flesh; true life in death; both of Mary and of God; first passible and then impassible - even Jesus Christ our Lord.

## Explanation

In Ephesians 7 Ignatius contrasts teachers who 'carry about the name' in guile with the one Physician: Jesus Christ, at once flesh and spirit, made and not made, God in flesh, passible then impassible. The paradoxical pairs confess the same twofold reality Paul states in Philippians 2: Christ Jesus, in the form of God, emptied himself and was found in human likeness.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Ignatius's confession is consistent with Philippians 2:5-8, though he does not quote the passage here; the link is a doctrinal connection drawn by the researcher (row B). The Philippians text (row A) speaks of Christ's form of God and form of a servant; Ignatius's 'God existing in flesh' is his own confessional phrasing, not later creedal language (row D).

## Historical context

Epistle to the Ephesians, written c. 105-115 (range) while Ignatius travelled under guard to Rome for martyrdom, exhorts the Ephesians to unity and warns against false teachers. Chapters 7 and 9 confess Christ and picture the believers' ascent to God.
