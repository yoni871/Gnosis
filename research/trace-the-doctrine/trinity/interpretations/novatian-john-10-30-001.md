## Quotation
> and if, whereas this word can be true of no man, “I and the Father are one,” Christ alone declared this word out of the consciousness of His divinity; […]

- Location: Treatise Concerning the Trinity, ch. 13
- Translation: Robert Ernest Wallis
- Edition: Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 5 (1886)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Novatian lists 'I and the Father are one' among words that can be true of no mere man: only Christ, conscious of his divinity, could declare it. Together with Thomas's confession and Romans 9:5, it is offered as proof that Christ is God. The ellipsis marks the continuation of Novatian's sentence ('and if, finally, the Apostle Thomas...'), which is quoted in novatian-john-20-28-001.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Novatian reads John 10:30 as Christ's own claim to divinity, true of him alone and of no mere man. (A) The verse itself states oneness without specifying whether the oneness is of essence, will, or power; Novatian's inference to divinity is his reading. (D) Novatian does not formulate the oneness as Nicene homoousios; his Son remains derived from the Father.

## Historical context

Treatise Concerning the Trinity, c. 250 (approximate), ch. 13, arguing against Sabellius that the Son is distinct from the Father yet divine.
