## Quotation

> And Christ was certainly not then anointed with the Holy Spirit, when He, as a dove, descended upon Him at His baptism. For at that time He deigned to prefigure His body, i.e. His Church, in which especially the baptized receive the Holy Spirit.

- Edition: Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series I, Vol. 3, ed. Philip Schaff (1887)
- Translation: Arthur West Haddan
- Location: Book XV, chapter 26, section 46
- Reproducibility: public-domain

## Explanation

Augustine is showing that the Holy Spirit was given when Jesus breathed on the disciples and again at Pentecost. He insists Christ was not then anointed with the Spirit at His baptism, since He was already the Anointed; rather the dove at the baptism prefigured His body, the Church, in which the baptised receive the Holy Spirit.

## Relationship to the doctrine

(C) Augustine reads the dove of Matthew 3:16 as prefiguring the Church receiving the Spirit in baptism. (A) The text states that the Spirit of God descended like a dove and alighted on Jesus; it does not state that the dove prefigures the Church. (B) The prefiguration of the Church is Augustine's reading. (D) The doctrine of the Spirit's gift in baptism is a later theological development, not the Gospel's own claim.

## Historical context

De Trinitate was written 399-422 (range; see works.json). Books I-IV defend the unity and equality of the Trinity from Scripture against those who argued, from texts about the sending and subordination of the Son and the Spirit, that the Son is less than the Father. This passage comes from Book XV, chapter 26, where Augustine treats the giving of the Holy Spirit at Christ's breathing on the disciples and at Pentecost.
