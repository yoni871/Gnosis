## Quotation
> The Lord says, I and the Father are one; and again it is written of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, And these three are one.

- Location: On the Unity of the Church, §6
- Translation: Ernest Wallis
- Edition: Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 5 (1886)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

Arguing for the unity of the Church, Cyprian appeals to the unity of God: as the Lord says 'I and the Father are one', so 'it is written of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, And these three are one.' The sentence is cited in the Wallis translation with the footnote reference '1 John 5:7'. Cyprian uses the threefold unity as the pattern and ground of ecclesial unity.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Cyprian reads the words 'these three are one' as written of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and uses them as evidence of divine unity. (A) The wording Cyprian quotes resembles the later Johannine Comma ('For there are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one'), but Cyprian's sentence names the Father, the Son (not the Word), and the Holy Spirit, and does not quote the full Comma wording. Whether Cyprian knew a form of 1 John 5:7 containing the Comma is disputed; this record does NOT assert that the Comma is original to 1 John, and the Phase 2 finding stands that the Comma is not verified as original. (D) Cyprian predates Nicaea by about 75 years.

## Historical context

On the Unity of the Church, c. 251 (approximate), §6, written on the occasion of the schism of Novatian to recall the Carthaginians to unity. The treatise is about church unity, not a treatise on the Trinity; the trinitarian sentence serves the argument about unity.

## Notes

FLAG (textual history): Cyprian's 'And these three are one' is the earliest Latin witness sometimes cited in the Comma debate, but it is not a verbatim quotation of the Johannine Comma as it appears in the Textus Receptus/KJV (which names 'the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost' and speaks of bearing record in heaven). The Comma's originality is not verified (Phase 2: 1 not-verified record). No dissenting reading of 1 John 5:7 from a named writer in this era was verified in the sources checked.
