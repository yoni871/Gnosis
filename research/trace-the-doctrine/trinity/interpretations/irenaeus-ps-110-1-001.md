## Quotation

## Explanation

Against Heresies III.6 argues that Scripture never calls anyone God or Lord absolutely except the true God - the Father ruling over all and his Son who has received dominion from the Father. Psalm 110:1 is Irenaeus's first proof: the Father speaks to the Son as 'my Lord.' Since both Father and Son are truly Lord, the Holy Spirit has fitly designated them by the title of Lord.

## Relationship to the doctrine

This is a historical reading (row C): Irenaeus reads Psalm 110:1 as the Father addressing the Son. The psalm itself (row A) is a royal psalm in which God speaks to a lord at his right hand; the identification of the speakers as Father and Son is Irenaeus's reading, directed against Gnostic claims of other gods, and it is not the later Nicene formulation (row D).

## Historical context

Against Heresies, written c. 175-185 (range), is Irenaeus's refutation of Valentinian Gnosticism. Book III argues from Scripture that the Creator is the one true God and Father of Jesus Christ; Book IV argues that the one God formed all things by means of the Word and the Holy Spirit.
