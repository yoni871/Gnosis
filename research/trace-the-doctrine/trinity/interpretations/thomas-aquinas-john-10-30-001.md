## Quotation

> Although we may not find it declared in Holy Writ in so many words that the three persons are of one essence, nevertheless we find it so stated as regards the meaning; for instance, "I and the Father are one (John 10:30)," and "I am in the Father, and the Father in Me (John 10:38)"; and there are many other texts of the same import.

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 39, a. 2, reply to Objection 2
- **Language:** English
- **Translation:** Fathers of the English Dominican Province
- **Edition:** English translation by the Fathers of the English Dominican Province (1920), New Advent online edition
- **Reproducibility:** public-domain

## Explanation

Aquinas is defending the formula that the three persons are 'of one essence' against the objection that Holy Writ never says so in so many words. He replies that, although the phrase is not found verbatim, it is found 'as regards the meaning', and he gives John 10:30 ('I and the Father are one') and John 10:38 ('I am in the Father, and the Father in Me') as the scriptural evidence for that meaning. For Aquinas, 'I and the Father are one' states the unity of essence of Father and Son.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads John 10:30 as stating, as regards its meaning, that Father and Son are of one essence. (A) The text states 'I and the Father are one'; it does not use the words 'one essence' or the Nicene term homoousion. (B) The step from 'are one' to 'of one essence' is an inference, which Aquinas himself labels as a matter of meaning rather than of wording. (D) The formula 'of one essence' is the later Nicene/scholastic formulation and must not be presented as the wording of John 10:30.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article treats the persons in relation to the essence, defending the Nicene homoousion ('of one essence') against Arian readings.
