## Quotation

> The Holy Ghost proceeds from the Father (John 15:26); and He is distinct from the Son, according to the words, "I will ask My Father, and He will give you another Paraclete" (John 14:16).

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 27, a. 3, sed contra
- **Language:** English
- **Translation:** Fathers of the English Dominican Province
- **Edition:** English translation by the Fathers of the English Dominican Province (1920), New Advent online edition
- **Reproducibility:** public-domain

## Explanation

Aquinas asks whether there is any other procession in God besides the generation of the Word. He answers yes, citing two Johannine texts together: the Holy Ghost proceeds from the Father (John 15:26) and is distinct from the Son, because Christ promises 'another Paraclete' (John 14:16). The word 'another' is the evidence that the Spirit is a distinct person from the Son, and therefore that there is a second procession, the procession of love.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads John 14:16 as showing that the Holy Ghost is distinct from the Son ('another Paraclete'), which grounds his claim of a second procession in God, the procession of love. (A) The text promises another Helper/Paraclete; it does not state that the Spirit proceeds by way of the will or that there are two processions. (D) The two-processions framework (Word by intellect, Love by will) is Aquinas's scholastic formulation and must not be presented as the wording of John 14:16.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article establishes the two processions (Word by intellect, Love by will) on which the whole scholastic account of the three persons rests.
