## Quotation

> It is said (Psalm 2:7): "This day have I begotten Thee."

- **Location:** Summa Theologiae I, q. 27, a. 2, sed contra

## Explanation

Aquinas asks whether any procession in God can be called generation. Against the objection that generation involves change from non-existence to existence, he cites Psalm 2:7, 'This day have I begotten Thee', as the scriptural authority that the procession of the Word is called generation. In his respondeo he argues that the Word proceeds by way of similitude in the same nature, so that this procession is properly generation and the Word is properly called Son.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Aquinas reads Psalm 2:7 as the scriptural warrant for calling the Word's procession 'generation' and the Word 'Son'. (A) The psalm states, as a royal decree, 'You are my Son; today I have begotten you'; it does not state that the Son is divine or eternal. (B) The step from the psalm's enthronement language to an eternal generation is an inference, which Aquinas supports by his scholastic account of the Word as proceeding by way of similitude in the same nature. (D) 'Eternal generation' is a later doctrinal formulation and must not be presented as the psalm's own statement.

## Historical context

Written as part of the Prima Pars of the Summa Theologiae, composed 1265-1268 (whole work composed 1265-1274; certainty: range). The article opens the Trinitarian treatise (qq. 27-43) on the procession of the divine persons, against Arian and Sabellian readings of procession.
