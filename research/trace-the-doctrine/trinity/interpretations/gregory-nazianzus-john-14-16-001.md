## Explanation

Gregory traces how the Spirit was 'declared by Jesus' by little and little: 'I will ask the Father, He says, and He will send you another Comforter, even the spirit of Truth' (John 14:16). Jesus said this, Gregory explains, 'that He might not seem to be a rival God, or to make His discourses to them by another authority.' The Spirit then came gradually to dwell in the disciples, 'measuring Himself out to them according to their capacity to receive Him'. In §3 Gregory had already called the Spirit 'the Other Comforter' and 'the True Light which lighteneth every man coming into the world', one Light with Father and Son.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Gregory reads John 14:16 as Jesus promising 'another Comforter', the Spirit of Truth, sent by the Father at Jesus' asking; the 'another' marks the Spirit as a distinct person who is nevertheless one God with Father and Son. (D) Gregory is writing in 380, on the eve of Constantinople (381); his oration is a direct source for the council's confession of the Spirit, not yet the creed itself.

## Historical context

Fifth Theological Oration (Oration 31), c. 380 (approximate), §26, delivered at Constantinople during the struggle against the Arians and the Pneumatomachians, who denied the Spirit's deity.
