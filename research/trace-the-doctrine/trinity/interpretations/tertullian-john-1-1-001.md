## Quotation
> Now if He too is God, according to John, (who says,) “The Word was God,” then you have two Beings—One that commands that the thing be made, and the Other that executes the order and creates.

- Location: Against Praxeas, ch. 12
- Translation: Peter Holmes
- Edition: Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 3 (1885)
- Language: English
- Reproducibility: public-domain


## Explanation

In the same chapter, Tertullian turns from Genesis 1:26 to John 1:1-3: because John says 'the Word was God' and 'through whom all things were made', there are two Beings, one who commands creation and one who executes it. He immediately qualifies this as a distinction of Personality, not of Substance, 'in the way of distinction, not of division'.

## Relationship to doctrine

(C) Tertullian reads John 1:1 as establishing that the Word is God and is distinct from the Father who commands, while sharing one substance. (D) This is pre-Nicene: 'two Beings' distinguished by command and execution reflects Tertullian's economic subordination, not the Nicene confession of one essence (homoousios). The reading supports the Son's deity and distinction, but must not be presented as the Nicene doctrine.

## Historical context

Against Praxeas, c. 207-213 (range), written against the modalist Praxeas. Chapter 12 adduces scriptural quotations 'in proof of the plurality of Persons in the Godhead'.

## Alternative readings

- arius (arius-thalia): Arius read the Word as a creature made out of nothing, who 'once was not' and 'was not before His origination'; the Word is God only in name, by participation of grace, and is 'alien and unlike in all things to the Father's essence'. See arius-john-1-1-001.
