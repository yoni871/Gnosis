## Overview

YHWH tells Noah to enter the ark with his household: clean animals by sevens, the rest by pairs, seven days before rain. Noah is six hundred when the waters come. On the seventeenth day of the second month the fountains of the great deep break open and windows of heaven open; Noah and his household enter, and YHWH shuts them in. Rain falls forty days while the waters prevail a hundred and fifty, rising fifteen cubits above the mountains, and all flesh on dry land dies; only Noah is left. God remembers Noah: a wind passes over the earth, the waters recede, and the ark rests on the mountains of Ararat. The raven flies to and fro; the dove, sent three times, at last does not return. In year 601 the earth is dry, and God commands the exit. The passage gives no independently established date.[^source-bible-hub-interlinear][^source-bdb-lexicon]

## Ancient World

### Dates

The text dates everything in Noah's years: the Flood begins 2/17 of year 600; the ark rests 7/17; the mountain tops are seen 10/1; dry ground 2/27 of year 601 — 370 days on thirty-day months. The forty days of rain fall within the 150; whether 8:3 repeats the same 150 days is disputed. The Greek text reads the 27th day and the 11th month; the original numbers are disputed.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-nets-septuagint-genesis]

### Creation echoed

The deep of 7:11 is the same Hebrew word as the deep of Genesis 1:2, and the wind of 8:1 echoes the Spirit of Genesis 1:2, but 8:1 has no "of God" — Spirit or ordinary wind is disputed. The openings close as they opened — creation undone and remade. Wenham reads the story as a 31-step chiasm turning on "God remembered Noah" (8:1); Emerton disputes it.[^source-bdb-lexicon][^source-wenham-coherence-flood]

### Other flood stories

Three Mesopotamian stories share the pattern. In Atrahasis the gods act because human noise disturbs Enlil; the storm lasts seven days and seven nights, with the landing lost in a gap. In Gilgamesh XI the boat is a 120-cubit cube, the storm six days and seven nights, and the birds survive — dove, swallow, raven; the Sumerian story names no birds and no mountain. Genesis alone has one God, direct speech, and a covenant. The relation — borrowing, shared tradition, or independent use — is disputed and unknown.[^source-dalley-myths-mesopotamia][^source-george-epic-gilgamesh-penguin][^source-etcsl-flood-story]

### Extent and evidence

The extent of the waters is disputed. For a universal flood: the repeated "all", "under the entire heaven", fifteen cubits above the mountains. For a regional one: the same words are used relatively elsewhere. Neither reading is established. The Ur, Kish, and Shuruppak flood layers are real but asynchronous; the Shuruppak date is not a date for the Genesis Flood. No ark remains are authenticated, and Durupınar is a natural syncline. "The mountains of Ararat" is a plural region, Assyrian Urartu; the oldest traditions point to Qardu, and Mount Ararat is a medieval identification.[^source-walton-lost-world-flood][^source-whitcomb-morris-genesis-flood][^source-roux-ancient-iraq][^source-collins-fasold-durupinar]

### Later readings

Later readers expanded the story. Jubilees gives seven flood-gates and names the resting place Lubar; 1 Enoch has the angel Uriel warn Noah; Josephus retells it with a four-storey ark and the Place of Descent. The New Testament first names the Flood a figure of baptism (1 Peter 3:20–21); the fathers: one ark, one Church. Whether it is two sources (J and P) or one composition is disputed.[^source-charles-jubilees][^source-charles-enoch][^source-josephus-antiquities][^source-philo-questions-genesis][^source-augustine-city-of-god]

## Why This Context Matters

The Flood is judgement and remembrance together: the waters undo creation, and God's remembering — not human effort — brings the wind, the receding, and the dry ground. It assigns no date, proves no extent, and names no landing peak; it leaves Noah's family to begin again under a God who acts, remembers, and keeps the story open to the covenant that follows.
