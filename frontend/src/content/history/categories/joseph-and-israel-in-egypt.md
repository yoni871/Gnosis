## Overview

Genesis 37–50 is the closing movement of Genesis, and it is held together by garments and by one ruling idea.[^source-bible-odyssey-joseph-clothing][^source-skinner-genesis] Joseph is given a garment, stripped of it, leaves one behind as false evidence, and is finally clothed in linen at his investiture — the garment motif runs through the whole story, and with it runs the sentence Joseph speaks at the end: "Ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to save much people alive" (Gen 50:20).[^source-bible-odyssey-joseph-clothing][^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-skinner-genesis] The story opens in Canaan with Joseph, seventeen years old, "the son of his old age", loved by Jacob "more than all his children" and given the long-sleeved tunic that marks him out from his brothers.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-bdb-lexicon] He dreams twice — sheaves bowing in the field, then sun, moon, and eleven stars bowing to him — and tells the dreams, and his brothers hate him and "could not speak peaceably unto him".[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-bdb-lexicon]

The narrative moves in three movements.[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] In Canaan (Gen 37–39) Joseph is stripped, thrown into a pit, and sold for twenty pieces of silver to a caravan bound for Egypt, while the intervening chapter of Judah and Tamar (Gen 38) prepares the Judah who will later offer himself in Benjamin's place.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis] In Egypt (Gen 40–41) Joseph rises from prison to palace: he interprets the cupbearer's and the baker's dreams, then Pharaoh's doubled dreams of fat and lean kine and full and thin ears, and at thirty he is set over the land, given Pharaoh's signet ring, fine linen, and a gold chain, and named Zaphnath-paaneah.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis] In the reconciliation and settlement (Gen 42–50) the famine drives his brothers to Egypt twice, Judah offers himself as a slave in Benjamin's stead, Joseph reveals himself with the theology of providence — "God did send me before you to preserve life" (Gen 45:5) — and Jacob's whole household descends to Goshen, where Jacob blesses his twelve sons and dies, and Joseph himself dies at a hundred and ten, his bones awaiting the exodus.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph]

No date is known for any of this, and no pharaoh is named in Genesis 37–50.[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] The narrative's own chronology is relative — Joseph seventeen at the sale, thirty at his rise, a hundred and ten at his death; Jacob a hundred and thirty at his arrival and a hundred and forty-seven at his death — not an absolute date, and the question of when Joseph lived remains open.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] What the story does establish is the shape of Genesis's ending: the family that began with Abraham's call is carried into Egypt by famine and by providence, the younger is set before the elder once more, and the book closes looking forward — to a new king who "knew not Joseph" (Exod 1:8) and to the bones that will be carried up out of Egypt when Israel leaves.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph]

## Ancient World

### Dreams and the Sale (Gen 37–38)

Joseph is seventeen when the story takes him up, "the son of his old age", and Jacob loves him "more than all his children" (Gen 37:2–3).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] The mark of that love is the *ketonet passim*, a long-sleeved tunic reaching to the wrists and ankles.[^source-bdb-lexicon][^source-skinner-genesis][^source-jfb-commentary] The King James Version renders it "coat of many colours", following the Septuagint's *chitōn poikilos*; "variegated tunic" is closer to the Hebrew.[^source-nets-septuagint-genesis][^source-skinner-genesis][^source-bible-hub-interlinear] Joseph brings an evil report of his brothers, and the tunic, the report, and the dreams together drive the hatred: the brothers "could not speak peaceably unto him" (Gen 37:4).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-bdb-lexicon]

Joseph dreams twice: his sheaf stands upright while his brothers' sheaves bow down to it, and then the sun, the moon, and eleven stars bow down to him (Gen 37:5–11).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] Rachel was already dead when the second dream was told, so the "moon" cannot literally be Rachel; interpreters differ, while Josephus treats Rachel as alive.[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph][^source-josephus-antiquities]

Sent to find his brothers at Shechem, Joseph follows them to Dothan, where they strip him of the tunic, throw him into a pit, and sell him for twenty pieces of silver to a caravan bound for Egypt (Gen 37:12–28).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-skinner-genesis][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] The tunic is dipped in a goat's blood and carried back to Jacob, who mourns his son as dead (Gen 37:31–33).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-bible-odyssey-joseph-clothing] Skinner presents the sale as a double recension — Ishmaelites on Judah's advice in one strand, Midianites in the other — a critical reconstruction, not the text's own presentation.[^source-skinner-genesis]

Genesis 38 interrupts the story with Judah and Tamar; Keil and Delitzsch hold it integral — Judah's Canaanite alliance endangered the call — while Skinner treats it as a legend independent of the Joseph cycle.[^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis][^source-skinner-genesis] Tamar's motive was children, not lust, and Judah confesses, "She is more righteous than I" (Gen 38:26).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis]

### Potiphar, Prison, and Pharaoh's Dreams (Gen 39–41)

In Egypt Joseph is bought by Potiphar — Skinner's Pedephre, "He whom the sun-god gives", chief of the cooks, whom the Septuagint calls "Pharao's gelding, a chief butcher" (Gen 37:36).[^source-skinner-genesis][^source-nets-septuagint-genesis] Through Gen 39 runs the refrain "the LORD was with Joseph" (39:2–3, 21, 23).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph]

Potiphar's wife propositions Joseph day after day, and he refuses on grounds of trust in his master and sin against God: "how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?" (Gen 39:9).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] She seizes the garment he leaves in her hand and accuses him falsely, and Joseph is imprisoned (Gen 39:12–20).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] The Tale of Two Brothers parallels Gen 39 only in the seduction and false-accusation episode; the rest turns fantastic, with gods, transformations, and self-mutilation, and dependence is not established.[^source-moldenke-tale-two-brothers][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph][^source-bible-odyssey-joseph-clothing]

In prison Joseph interprets the cupbearer's and baker's dreams — "Do not interpretations belong to God?" (Gen 40:8) — the cupbearer restored in three days, the baker hanged, and the cupbearer forgets Joseph for two full years (Gen 40:23; 41:1).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] Pharaoh then dreams twice in one night — seven fat kine devoured by seven lean, seven full ears by seven thin (Gen 41:1–7) — and Joseph is summoned, shaves, and is clothed before Pharaoh; the doubling means the thing is established by God: seven years of plenty, then seven of famine (Gen 41:25–32).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis]

Joseph advises storing a fifth part, and Pharaoh appoints him — "Can we find such a man as this is, in whom the Spirit of God is?" (Gen 41:38) — with the signet ring, fine linen, and a gold chain (Gen 41:42–45).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis] Zaphnath-paaneah's meaning is debated — "salvation of the world" (Keil and Delitzsch), "revealer of secrets" (Josephus), "the god speaks and he lives" (BiblicalTraining).[^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis][^source-josephus-antiquities][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] Joseph is thirty at his rise (Gen 41:46); Manasseh is born — "God made me forget all my toil" — and Ephraim — "God made me fruitful in the land of my affliction" (Gen 41:51–52).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv]

On an early-exodus reckoning (1 Kgs 6:1; Exod 12:40) Joseph falls under the 12th Dynasty, in the Middle Kingdom; on a late-exodus reckoning he falls in the Second Intermediate Period under the Hyksos, circa 1650–1550 BC, whose capital Avaris lay near Goshen.[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] A Semitic official would have risen most easily under such rulers, although Egyptian records show that Asiatics could hold high office at other periods as well. The question remains open.[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] No dynasty is assigned here, and no Egyptian record names Joseph; the absence is stated alongside the dating question, not as a refutation.[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph][^source-skinner-genesis] Skinner judges the Yanhamu identification "too slender"; the Arisu/Irsu parallel "vanishes on closer inspection".[^source-skinner-genesis]

### The Brothers' Journeys and Reconciliation (Gen 42–45)

The famine drives the brothers to Egypt twice, in two journeys with four interviews, as Skinner's source analysis presents it.[^source-skinner-genesis] Joseph tests them — the spy accusation, three days in prison, Simeon bound as hostage, the silver returned in their sacks (Gen 42) — and the brothers confess divine punishment, recalling Joseph's pleading: "we saw the anguish of his soul, when he besought us, and we would not hear" (Gen 42:21).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] Judah's surety — "let me bear the blame for ever" (Gen 43:9) — is accepted.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv]

Benjamin's portion is five times the others' in the Masoretic text (Gen 43:34); Josephus says double and Jubilees seven times, both as their traditions, not as the biblical text.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-josephus-antiquities][^source-charles-jubilees] The silver cup is hidden in Benjamin's sack and Joseph claims divination by it (Gen 44:5, 15); Josephus has a golden cup, and Jubilees reframes the divination as delight in the cup.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-josephus-antiquities][^source-charles-jubilees] Judah's speech (Gen 44:18–34) is the pivot: the man who once proposed selling a brother now offers himself as a slave in Benjamin's stead.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] Joseph reveals himself — "God did send me before you to preserve life" (Gen 45:5–8) — and the wrong is named, not excused; Philo reads the forgiveness as unconditional and unchanging, which is Philo's reading.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph][^source-skinner-genesis][^source-philo-de-josepho]

### Jacob's Descent, Goshen, and the Blessings (Gen 46–49)

Jacob descends only after the theophany at Beersheba (Gen 46:2–4) — the only theophany in Genesis 37–50, and none is given to Joseph himself.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis] Seventy souls descend in the Masoretic reckoning (Gen 46:27); the Septuagint gives seventy-five, the figure Acts 7:14 follows.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-nets-septuagint-genesis][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] The family settles in Goshen, in the eastern Nile Delta; "the land of Rameses" (Gen 47:11) is taken by many to be an updating to a later place name — an inference, not a statement of the text.[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph][^source-bible-gateway-kjv]

Gen 47:13–26 records the famine administration descriptively, in the text's sequence: money spent, then livestock surrendered, then land, then persons, while the priests' land is exempt and the fifth part becomes a permanent statute.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-nets-septuagint-genesis] Gen 47:21 is a textual variant — the Masoretic text has Joseph moving the people into the cities, while the Samaritan Pentateuch and the Septuagint have him reducing them to servitude — and how far the narrator means to commend the policy is ambiguous: commentators differ over it.[^source-nets-septuagint-genesis][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph]

In Gen 48 Jacob adopts Ephraim and Manasseh (48:5) and crosses his hands, laying his right hand on Ephraim the younger (48:19), and Reuben forfeits the firstborn rank: leadership passes to Judah and the double portion to Joseph (Gen 49:3–4; 1 Chr 5:1–2).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-bible-odyssey-sibling-rivalries][^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis]

Skinner reads Gen 49 as "one of the oldest pieces of Hebrew poetry", oracles on the twelve tribes as unfolded in Judges and the early monarchy — *vaticinia ex eventu*, the horizon closing at David and Solomon.[^source-skinner-genesis] Keil and Delitzsch reject that reading: attacks on genuineness proceed from an a priori denial of supernatural prophecy, and *ʾaḥărît hayyāmîm* means "the end of the days", the Messianic age of consummation.[^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis] Gen 49:10 is read three ways: as the Messiah's personal name — "the man, or bearer, of rest" (Keil and Delitzsch), "He who is to be sent" (Calvin); as the place Shiloh, rejected as unhistorical by Skinner, Keil and Delitzsch, and Calvin; and as the Septuagint's "things stored up for him".[^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis][^source-calvin-genesis][^source-skinner-genesis][^source-nets-septuagint-genesis] What all three share is established: "the gathering of the peoples" means the willing obedience of the nations, and the Septuagint agrees in sense, "he is the expectation of nations".[^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis][^source-calvin-genesis][^source-nets-septuagint-genesis]

### Death and the Bones (Gen 50)

Jacob is buried at Machpelah (Gen 50:1–13).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] After the funeral the brothers fear revenge, and Joseph answers: "Fear not… ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to save much people alive" (Gen 50:19–20).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] Joseph dies at a hundred and ten, embalmed and put in a coffin in Egypt, having taken an oath that his brothers carry his bones up with them (Gen 50:22–25).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] The oath is kept (Exod 13:19; Josh 24:32).[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph][^source-bible-gateway-kjv]

The New Testament never explicitly calls Joseph a type of Christ.[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] The early church drew correspondences — beloved son, rejected, sold for silver, lowly then exalted, source of life for wrongdoers — and Acts 7 is cited for a wider reading.[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] Keil and Delitzsch restrict it: Joseph is not a medium of salvation like Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and the reading is not represented by prophets, Christ, or apostles as the Exodus guidance is.[^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis] Neither position is presented as the dossier's conclusion.[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph][^source-keil-delitzsch-genesis]

## Why This Context Matters

The Joseph story is Genesis's closing movement, and its ruling idea is providence without excuse: "Ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to save much people alive" (Gen 50:20).[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-skinner-genesis] God intends good from human evil, and the wrong is not excused — the brothers' guilt is named throughout, from their confession in Gen 42 to Joseph's final word in Gen 50, and forgiveness follows the naming rather than erasing it.[^source-biblicaltraining-joseph][^source-bible-gateway-kjv] That is the thread that holds the three movements together: a tunic stripped and a brother sold, a prison and a palace, a cup hidden in a sack and a speech offering a life for a life — and at every turn the same immanent presence, "the LORD was with Joseph", working through human scheming rather than around it.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-skinner-genesis]

The younger-son motif closes here as it began: Ephraim is set before Manasseh, and the blessing passes not by birth order but by a crossed pair of hands, as it had passed to Jacob over Esau and to Joseph over his brothers.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-bible-odyssey-sibling-rivalries] The descent to Egypt is also the mechanism by which an older promise begins to be fulfilled: the four-hundred-year affliction foretold in Gen 15:13–16 starts when Jacob's household settles in Goshen, and the family that entered Egypt as seventy souls will leave it as a nation.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph] Genesis ends not with a settlement but with a coffin and an oath — Joseph's bones waiting for the exodus — and the next book opens with a new king who knew not Joseph.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-biblicaltraining-joseph]
