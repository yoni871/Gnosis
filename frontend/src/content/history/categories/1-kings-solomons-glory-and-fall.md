## Overview


Solomon has finished the temple and his own palace, and the LORD appears to him a second time, as he had at Gibeon [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The word he hears is in two halves. First the promise: the house is hallowed, the LORD's name will be there for ever, and his eyes and heart shall be there perpetually — and if Solomon walks as David walked, keeping the statutes and judgments, his throne will be established upon Israel for ever [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Then the curse, stated in full before anything has gone wrong: if they turn to other gods, Israel will be cut off from the land, the house cast out of the LORD's sight, and Israel a proverb and a byword among all people [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Between the warning and its fulfilment comes the glory. The queen of Sheba travels some 1,500 miles from southern Arabia to test Solomon with hard questions, and finds the half was not told her: his wisdom, his palace, the food on his table, and the worship at the temple leave her overwhelmed, and she blesses the LORD who set him on the throne to do judgment and justice [^source-biblicaltraining-queen-of-sheba]. The wealth that follows is counted like a ledger: 666 talents of gold in one year, targets and shields of beaten gold, an ivory throne with twelve lions on its six steps, a fleet that returns once in three years with gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks, and 1,400 chariots with horses brought out of Egypt [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text][^source-biblicaltraining-talent]. Then the turn. In his old age Solomon loves many strange women — 700 wives and 300 concubines — and his heart is turned after other gods: Ashtoreth of the Zidonians, Milcom of the Ammonites, Chemosh of Moab, and Molech, with high places built on the hill before Jerusalem [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text][^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. The sentence follows at once: the kingdom will surely be rent from him and given to his servant, though not in his days, and one tribe will be kept for David's sake and for Jerusalem's sake [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Adversaries rise on every side — Hadad of Edom, Rezon in Damascus, and Jeroboam, to whom the prophet Ahijah tears his new garment into twelve pieces and gives ten [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. Solomon reigns forty years, is buried in the city of David, and Rehoboam his son reigns in his stead [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text].

## Ancient World


### The warning and the promise

The second appearance answers the prayer Solomon prayed at the dedication. He had asked the LORD to keep the word spoken to David — that his children would keep to the way — and the answer repeats the condition back to him: walk as David walked, and the throne will stand for ever [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text][^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. The promise to David has been read two ways ever since. In the ancient Near East, a royal grant — land and dynasty given by a king to a loyal servant — was normally unconditional once given, while a treaty bound the vassal to loyalty on pain of curses [^source-bible-odyssey-covenant]. Some scholars read the promise to David as such a grant [^source-weinfeld-covenant-grant]; Kings keeps attaching conditions to it. Chapter 9 states it conditionally and the curse unconditionally: the house is hallowed for ever, but the throne stands only if the king walks in the statutes — and if they turn to other gods, the house itself will be cast out, and passers-by will be answered, "Because they forsook the LORD their God" [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The curse is not hindsight: the dedicatory prayer had already foreseen captivity and return. That is why the chapter is the hinge of the reign — the glory and the apostasy alike are read against a warning already given.

### Tyre, timber, and the twenty cities

Solomon did not build alone. Hiram of Tyre had been on friendly terms with David, and the two kings made a league: Hiram's workers cut cedar and juniper in Lebanon, floated the logs down the coast in rafts, and delivered them for Israelite hauling, while Solomon sent 20,000 cors of wheat and twenty cors of pressed oil each year [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. The Phoenicians of Tyre, Sidon, and Byblos were the great sailors of the ancient world, and Solomon used their seamen for his ships at Ezion-geber, on the Gulf of Aqaba [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. At the end of twenty years, Solomon paid Hiram in land: twenty cities in Galilee. Hiram came out from Tyre to see them, and they pleased him not. "What cities are these which thou hast given me, my brother?" he asked, and called them the land of Cabul [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The name is given "unto this day," and the text never explains it. Some read the towns as security for a loan; others as separate transactions; many see the strain on the royal treasury [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. The gift, the inspection, the displeasure, the name — and then Hiram's 120 talents of gold, about four tons, sent in return [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon][^source-biblicaltraining-talent]. The alliance survives, and Hiram's sailors still man Solomon's fleet to Ophir. But the episode is the first crack in the picture of effortless plenty, and it comes before the glory is displayed.

### The queen from the incense road

Sheba was the land of the Sabeans in southwestern Arabia, in what is now Yemen, rich from the caravan trade in incense and spices [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. By caravan the journey from Marib to Jerusalem covered roughly 1,500 miles [^source-biblicaltraining-queen-of-sheba]. The Sabeans grew wealthy on frankincense and myrrh, in heavy demand throughout the ancient world, and camel caravans carried them north toward Gaza and Damascus [^source-biblicaltraining-queen-of-sheba]. The queen heard of Solomon's fame "concerning the name of the LORD" and came to prove him with hard questions — in Hebrew, riddles [^source-biblicaltraining-queen-of-sheba]. She arrived with camels carrying spices, gold, and precious stones, put to Solomon everything she had in mind, and found nothing too hard for him to explain [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text][^source-biblicaltraining-queen-of-sheba]. Then she looked — the wisdom, the house, the food on his table, his officials, the cupbearers, and the ascent to the house of the LORD — and "there was no more spirit in her" [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Whether the Hebrew word means a stairway or a burnt offering, the overwhelm is the same [^source-biblicaltraining-queen-of-sheba]. Her speech is the centre of the story: the half was not told her, and blessed be the LORD who set him on the throne to do judgment and justice [^source-biblicaltraining-queen-of-sheba]. She gave 120 talents of gold and spices never matched afterward; Solomon gave her all her desire, and she returned home [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Where Sheba lay is still debated — Arabia or the African coast — and no inscription names the queen [^source-biblicaltraining-queen-of-sheba][^source-bible-odyssey-queen-of-sheba]. The Bible never gives her a personal name: the Hebrew simply means "queen of Sheba" [^source-biblicaltraining-queen-of-sheba]. Later traditions named her Bilqis, or Makeda, mother of Menelik — stories Scripture says nothing of [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon].

### Gold, ivory, and horses

The wealth catalogue of chapter 10 is the display between the warning and the fall. The talent was the largest unit of weight in the Bible: 60 minas, or 3,000 shekels, roughly 75 pounds, used above all for weighing precious metals [^source-biblicaltraining-talent]. The fleet brought 420 talents of gold from Ophir — about fourteen tons — and the queen gave 120; the weight of gold that came to Solomon in one year was 666 talents, roughly twenty-two tons, beside the merchants' revenue [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text][^source-biblicaltraining-talent]. Where Ophir was is disputed: southern Arabia, East Africa, and India have all been proposed [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. Two hundred targets and three hundred shields of beaten gold went into the house of the forest of Lebanon; the great ivory throne, overlaid with gold, had six steps and twelve lions standing on them, "there was not the like made in any kingdom" [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. All the drinking vessels were gold, and silver was nothing accounted of in the days of Solomon [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Once in three years the navy of Tarshish — large seagoing merchant vessels — came bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. And then the horses: 1,400 chariots and 12,000 horsemen, silver in Jerusalem as stones, and horses from Egypt at 600 shekels a chariot and 150 a horse, sold on to the kings of the Hittites and of Syria [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text][^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. The law of the king had forbidden the king to multiply horses, wives, silver, and gold — and the catalogue itemises, one by one, the very things the law forbade [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. The narrative passes no comment. The judgment comes from the law, and the fall shows it coming true.

### The gods on the hill

The marriages were probably treaty marriages, sealing treaties with neighbouring states, as was common among ancient kings [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. But the law had forbidden exactly this — and the narrative says the warning came true in Solomon's old age [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. Ashtoreth was the Hebrew form of Astarte, a leading goddess of the Canaanites and Phoenicians, linked with love, fertility, war, and the morning and evening star [^source-biblicaltraining-ashtoreth]. She became a principal goddess of the Phoenician cities; the Bible calls her the goddess "of the Zidonians" [^source-biblicaltraining-ashtoreth]. Chemosh was the god of Moab: on the Moabite Stone, King Mesha says he devoted captured Israelites to "Ashtar-Chemosh," joining the goddess with Chemosh [^source-biblicaltraining-ashtoreth][^source-mesha-stele]. Milcom — Molech — was the god of the Ammonites, Israel's neighbours east of the Jordan [^source-biblicaltraining-ammonites]. Solomon built a high place for Chemosh and for Molech on the hill before Jerusalem, and did likewise for all his strange wives, who burnt incense and sacrificed to their gods [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. His heart, the text says, was not perfect with the LORD his God, as was the heart of David his father [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The shrines stood until Josiah destroyed them [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon].

### Three adversaries and a torn cloak

The LORD stirred up an adversary unto Solomon, Hadad the Edomite, of the king's seed in Edom [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. As a child Hadad had fled to Egypt when Joab spent six months in Edom cutting off every male; Pharaoh gave him a house, land, and the sister of Queen Tahpenes to wife, and let him go home when David and Joab were dead [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text][^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. God stirred up another adversary, Rezon son of Eliadah, who had fled from Hadadezer of Zobah, gathered a band, and took Damascus, reigning there as king of Aram all the days of Solomon [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text][^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. The third was Jeroboam son of Nebat, an Ephraimite from Zeredah, whom Solomon had put in charge of the labour force of the house of Joseph after he built Millo [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. One day the prophet Ahijah of Shiloh met him alone in the field and rent his new garment in twelve pieces, giving Jeroboam ten: the kingdom would be rent from Solomon, and one tribe would stay for David's sake and for Jerusalem's sake [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text][^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. And to Jeroboam, Ahijah offered exactly what David had been offered: "if thou wilt hearken… and wilt walk in my ways… as David my servant did; that I will be with thee, and build thee a sure house, as I built for David" [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The condition Solomon failed is offered afresh to his rival [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Solomon sought to kill Jeroboam, who fled to Shishak king of Egypt — Sheshonq I — until Solomon's death [^source-biblicaltraining-solomon]. The rest of Solomon's acts and wisdom are in the book of the acts of Solomon; he reigns forty years, is buried in the city of David, and Rehoboam his son reigns in his stead [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text].

## Why This Context Matters


These three chapters are about how a kingdom is given, displayed, and torn — and the order is the lesson. The warning comes first: the house is hallowed for ever, but the throne stands only if the king walks as David walked, and the curse of exile is stated before anything has gone wrong. Then comes the glory at its height — the queen from the ends of the earth, the gold counted by the ton, the throne no other kingdom could match — so that the reader sees what is at stake when the fall comes. Knowing the world of Solomon's reign keeps the fall from looking like bad luck. The trade routes, the treaty marriages, the forced labour, and the gods on the hill were all ordinary tools of ancient kingship, and the law of the king had named each of them as a danger. The lamp kept for David's sake is the one mercy in the sentence: the kingdom is torn, but not wholly, and not for ever.
