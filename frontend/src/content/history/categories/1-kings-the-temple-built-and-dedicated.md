## Overview


Solomon builds the house his father could not. David had wanted to build a house for the name of the LORD, but the wars about him stood in the way; now the LORD has given Solomon rest on every side, and the son builds what the father desired [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The work begins with a treaty. Hiram king of Tyre, who had been a friend of David, sends servants to the new king, and Solomon asks him for cedar out of Lebanon, because no one in Israel can hew timber like the Sidonians [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Hiram agrees: his servants will cut the timber and float it down the coast in rafts, and Solomon will pay in wheat and pure oil, year by year [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text][^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. The two kings make a league together, and Solomon raises the labour — thirty thousand men working in Lebanon in monthly shifts, seventy thousand carriers, eighty thousand stonecutters, and three thousand three hundred officers [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. In the four hundred and eightieth year after the Exodus, in the fourth year of Solomon's reign, in the month Zif, the building begins [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The house is sixty cubits long, twenty broad, and thirty high, with a porch, side chambers three storeys high, and an inner sanctuary twenty cubits square, overlaid with pure gold [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The stone is dressed at the quarry, so that no hammer or axe or any tool of iron is heard in the house while it is building [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Halfway through the work the word of the LORD comes to Solomon: if he walks in the statutes and keeps the commandments, the LORD will perform his word to David, dwell among Israel, and not forsake his people [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Seven years later the house is finished, in the eleventh year, in the month Bul [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Solomon's own house takes thirteen years, and beside it stands the porch of judgment where the king sits to judge [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Then comes the furnishing. Hiram the brass-worker, a widow's son of Naphtali with a Tyrian father, casts the two great pillars, Jachin and Boaz, the molten sea standing on twelve oxen, and the ten wheeled bases with their lavers, all cast in the Jordan plain and left unweighed [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Solomon adds the gold vessels — the altar, the table, the ten lampstands — and brings in the silver and gold his father had dedicated [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Last comes the dedication. At the feast in the seventh month the priests and Levites bring the ark up out of the city of David and set it in the oracle under the wings of the cherubim, and the cloud fills the house so that the priests cannot stand to minister, "for the glory of the LORD had filled the house of the LORD" [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Solomon blesses the people, rehearses the promise to David, and prays the longest prayer in the book — seven petitions, from the oath at the altar to the stranger from a far country, and at last to captivity and return, prayed before any exile has happened [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The sacrifices are beyond counting, the feast runs fourteen days, and the people go home joyful [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text].

## Ancient World


### A treaty with Tyre

No king builds a temple alone, and Solomon does not try. He turns at once to Hiram of Tyre, whose Phoenician subjects were renowned craftsmen [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. The Phoenicians lived along the coastal areas in what is today Lebanon and Syria, and the Bible states that Solomon contracted craftsmen from Tyre and imported cedar and cypress from the same region [^source-bible-odyssey-first-temple]. Hiram had been a friend of David and had supplied timber and workmen for his palace; the friendship passes to the son, and with it the supply line [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. The bargain is practical on both sides. Hiram's men cut the cedar and juniper in Lebanon, bind the logs into rafts, and float them down the coast — the Chronicler names Joppa as the port where the timber was received for the overland haul up to Jerusalem [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. Solomon pays in food: twenty thousand measures of wheat and twenty measures of pure oil every year [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The two kings make a league together [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Solomon's own labour works beside Hiram's men: thirty thousand Israelites go to Lebanon in shifts of ten thousand, a month away and two months at home, under Adoniram, with seventy thousand carriers and eighty thousand stonecutters in the hills [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The same levy will return a generation later as the grievance that splits the kingdom, and a later history says bluntly that Solomon oppressed his subjects through hard labour to build the temple and the royal complex [^source-bible-odyssey-solomon]. In chapters 5 to 8 the text itself states the numbers and offers no judgment on them.

### The house and its measure

The house Solomon builds is modest by the standards of empires. The ancient cubit was about eighteen inches, so the main building was about ninety feet long, thirty feet wide, and forty-five feet high [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. It was not meant to hold a congregation; the people gathered in the courts outside, while only priests entered the building itself [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. The temple faced east and was laid out in three parts in a straight line: an entrance porch, a main hall, and an inner sanctuary [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. That shape was familiar in its world. Temples throughout the ancient Near East followed a similar three-part plan, and Iron Age temples at Tell Tayinat, Ain Dara, and Aleppo share the same building plans and features [^source-bible-odyssey-first-temple]. What made the Jerusalem temple different was not its shape but its contents: the inner room held no image of its God, only the ark beneath the cherubim [^source-bible-odyssey-first-temple]. Inside, the walls are lined with cedar from floor to ceiling so that no stone can be seen, the cedar carved with gourds and open flowers, the walls throughout bearing carvings of cherubim, palm trees, and open flowers, all overlaid with gold [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. Against the outer walls stand side rooms three storeys high, stepped back at each level so that nothing has to be inserted into the temple walls themselves [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. The inner sanctuary is a perfect cube, twenty cubits in each direction, overlaid with pure gold and closed by doors of olive wood [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. An inner court walled with dressed stone and cedar beams surrounds it, and beyond lies a larger outer court [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. No trace of the building has been unearthed in Jerusalem, because it would have stood on the Temple Mount, where excavations are not allowed since the site is holy to both Muslims and Jews [^source-bible-odyssey-first-temple]. 

### Pillars, sea, and bases

The bronze work is the most fully described furnishing in the Bible, and it is the work of one man. Solomon fetches Hiram out of Tyre, a widow's son of Naphtali whose father was a Tyrian, a worker in brass filled with wisdom and understanding and cunning to work all works in brass [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. In front of the porch he casts two freestanding pillars, each eighteen cubits high and twelve cubits around, topped with capitals five cubits high and decorated with chainwork, lily shapes, and rows of pomegranates [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. The right pillar is called Jachin, the left Boaz — names usually understood to mean "he establishes" and "in him is strength" [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. South of the temple stands the Sea, a huge round basin of cast bronze ten cubits across and five deep, resting on twelve bronze oxen arranged in four groups of three, facing north, west, south, and east, its rim shaped like the blossom of a lily [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. Kings gives its capacity as two thousand baths; the Chronicler gives three thousand [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. Then come the ten bases of brass, each on four wheels made like chariot wheels, graved with lions, oxen, and cherubim, each carrying a laver of forty baths, five on the right side of the house and five on the left [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The large bronze pieces are cast in clay moulds in the Jordan plain between Sukkoth and Zarethan, where suitable clay is available [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. Solomon leaves all the vessels unweighed, because they are exceeding many [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Inside, the gold vessels answer the bronze of the court [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. 

### The ark and the cloud

At the heart of the building stands the ark, and at the heart of the dedication the ark comes home. Inside the inner sanctuary stand two enormous cherubim carved from olive wood and overlaid with gold, each ten cubits high with a wingspan of ten cubits, their outer wings touching the side walls and their inner wings meeting in the middle of the room [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. Beneath their wings the ark is placed [^source-biblicaltraining-solomons-temple]. The Bible Odyssey article describes the cherubs as winged feline creatures with human faces, flanking the ark and forming a throne where God's glory was said to be present [^source-bible-odyssey-first-temple]. At the feast in the seventh month the elders assemble, the priests and Levites bring the ark up out of the city of David with the tabernacle and the holy vessels, and set it in the oracle under the cherubim's wings [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The text says what is inside: nothing save the two tables of stone which Moses put there at Horeb [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. A later letter describes the ark as holding more — the golden pot of manna and Aaron's rod — and the two accounts are not reconciled here. When the priests come out of the holy place, the cloud fills the house, and the priests cannot stand to minister, for the glory of the LORD has filled the house [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Solomon's own house, thirteen years against the temple's seven, stands beside it — the house of the forest of Lebanon, the porch of judgment, the house for Pharaoh's daughter — all of the same costly hewn stone [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The First Temple would stand alongside the royal palace in Jerusalem for nearly four hundred years, until the Babylonians burned it in 586 BC [^source-bible-odyssey-first-temple].

### The prayer and the exile it foresees

Solomon's prayer is the longest in the book, built as a series of cases, each ending in the same plea: hear in heaven, and forgive. It opens with the God who keeps covenant and mercy, and with the word kept to David, asked now to be kept further — if his children take heed to their way [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Then the question that frames everything: will God indeed dwell on the earth, when the heaven and heaven of heavens cannot contain him [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The answer the prayer works out is that God's name is in the house, his eyes are open toward it night and day, and he hears in heaven his dwelling place [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The seven petitions cover the whole life of the people: the oath sworn at the altar, defeat in battle and return, drought and the rain that follows repentance, famine and plague, the stranger from a far country, battle abroad, and at last captivity [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The stranger's petition is the widest of them. A foreigner who is not of Israel comes for the LORD's great name, his strong hand, and his stretched-out arm, and is heard so that all people of the earth may know his name [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The last petition is the longest and the most forward-looking: sin, anger, and carrying away captives far or near — then bethinking in the land of captivity, repentance, and prayer toward the land, the city, and the house, with forgiveness and compassion before the captors [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. It is prayed before any exile has happened, grounded in the Exodus, "from the midst of the furnace of iron" [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. Solomon closes by blessing the people: there has not failed one word of all the LORD's good promise [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text]. The feast runs fourteen days, and on the eighth day the people go home joyful [^source-mechon-mamre-1-kings-masoretic-text].

## Why This Context Matters


The temple is the place where the whole story of Israel gathers into one building: the promise to David, the rest after war, the treaty with Tyre, the timber of Lebanon, the labour of thousands, the gold of the inner room, and the ark coming at last to rest. Knowing that world keeps the dedication from shrinking into a ceremony. The house is small — ninety feet by thirty — and it holds no image, only the two tables of stone beneath the wings of the cherubim; yet the prayer spoken in it reaches further than the building, to the stranger from a far country and to captives praying toward a land they cannot see. The conditional word spoken halfway through the building is the warning the rest of the book will carry out: the glory fills the house, and the staying of the glory depends on walking in the statutes. That is why the prayer already speaks of exile before any exile has happened, and why the people go home joyful all the same — the house stands, the promise has not failed one word, and the God who cannot be contained has chosen to be sought there.
