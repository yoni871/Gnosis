## Overview

In the third month after leaving Egypt, Israel camps before Sinai. Moses goes up to God, who tells him to say to Israel: you have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I carried you on eagles' wings. If you obey my voice and keep my covenant, you will be my treasured possession, a kingdom of priests and a holy nation. The people answer together that all the LORD has spoken they will do. They are told to wash, sanctify themselves, and keep back from the mountain, for whoever touches it will die. On the third day thunder, lightning, thick cloud, and a long trumpet blast shake the camp, the mountain smokes and quakes, and Moses brings the people out to meet God.

Then God speaks the Ten Words: I am the LORD your God who brought you out of Egypt. You shall have no other gods before me, make no graven image, and take my name in vain. Remember the Sabbath day, six days you shall labour and the seventh is a Sabbath to the LORD. Honour your father and mother, that your days may be long. You shall not kill, commit adultery, steal, bear false witness, or covet anything your neighbour's. The people see the thunder, the trumpet, and the smoking mountain, tremble, and stand far off, asking Moses to speak for them lest they die. Moses tells them not to fear: God has come to test them, that his fear may keep them from sin. The LORD then gives laws for altars: earth, or unhewn stone, with no steps.

Then come the judgments for life together. A Hebrew servant serves six years and goes free in the seventh; the rules cover wives, children, and the servant who chooses to stay, his ear bored at the doorpost. There are laws for killing and manslaughter, for striking or cursing a parent, for kidnapping, for injury between men, for a master who strikes a servant, for a goring ox, and for theft, with restitution set by measure. More laws govern goods held in trust, borrowing, seduction, sorcery, sacrifice to other gods, the stranger, the widow and orphan, lending without interest, and the firstborn.

Then the laws of justice and worship: no false report, no following a crowd to do evil, no perverting judgment, no bribe. A straying ox is returned, a burdened ass helped. The land rests in the seventh year and the seventh day is a Sabbath. Three feasts are kept yearly: unleavened bread, harvest, and ingathering. An angel will go before them to the promised place and drive out six nations; Israel must make no covenant with them or their gods. Their borders run from the Red Sea to the Philistines' sea, and from the desert to the river.

The covenant is ratified. Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders are called up, but Moses alone comes near. Moses tells the people all the LORD's words and judgments, and they answer with one voice that they will do them all. Moses writes the words, builds an altar with twelve pillars, and has young men offer burnt and peace offerings. Half the blood goes on the altar; the book is read aloud, the people answer again, and Moses sprinkles the blood on them: behold the blood of the covenant. Then the elders go up, see the God of Israel on a pavement of sapphire, and eat and drink. Moses enters the cloud alone and stays forty days and forty nights.

## Ancient World

### Arrival at Sinai

In the third month after leaving Egypt, Israel camps before Sinai. Moses goes up to God, who calls from the mountain: you have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I carried you on eagles' wings. If you obey my voice and keep my covenant, you shall be my treasured possession, a kingdom of priests and a holy nation.

Moses lays these words before the elders, and the people answer together that they will do all the LORD has spoken. The LORD says he will come in a thick cloud, so the people may hear and believe. Moses must sanctify the people and have them wash, ready by the third day, when the LORD comes down on Sinai. Bounds are set: no one may go up or touch the mountain, on pain of death, until the trumpet sounds long.

On the third morning thunder, lightning, and cloud cover the mountain, and a trumpet sounds, and the camp trembles. Moses brings the people out to meet God at its foot. Sinai is all smoke, for the LORD descends in fire, and it quakes. The trumpet grows louder, Moses speaks, and God answers. The LORD calls Moses to the top and warns him to charge the people not to break through to gaze, lest many perish; the priests must sanctify themselves. Moses says bounds are set, and the LORD sends him to bring Aaron up.

### The Ten Words

Then God speaks: I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of Egypt. You shall have no other gods before me. You shall not make a graven image or any likeness of heaven, earth, or the waters below, nor bow down to them or serve them, for I am a jealous God, visiting iniquity to the third and fourth generation, showing mercy to thousands who love me. You shall not take my name in vain.

Remember the Sabbath day to keep it holy. Six days you shall labour, but the seventh is the Sabbath of the LORD your God, and no work is done in it, by you, your household, your servants, cattle, or stranger. For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth and rested. Honour your father and mother, that your days may be long. You shall not kill, commit adultery, steal, or bear false witness. You shall not covet anything your neighbour's.

The people see the thunder, the trumpet, and the smoking mountain, and tremble and stand afar. They ask Moses to speak instead, lest they die if God speaks. Moses tells them not to fear: God has come to test them, so his fear may keep them from sin. The people stand far off, and Moses draws near the thick darkness. Then come laws for altars: earth, or unhewn stone, with no steps.

### Judgments: servants, injury, and property

These are the judgments. A Hebrew servant serves six years and goes free in the seventh: if alone he leaves alone, if married his wife leaves with him, but a wife given by his master stays, with her children. If the servant loves master, wife, and children and will not go free, his ear is bored at the doorpost and he serves for ever. A maidservant sold by her father does not go out as men do: if she displeases her master she may be redeemed but not sold abroad; if betrothed to his son she is treated as a daughter, or goes free.

Whoever strikes a man dead is put to death, except one who did not lie in wait, for whom a refuge is appointed. Striking or cursing father or mother is put to death, as is stealing a man. If men fight and one is hurt but recovers, the striker pays for time and healing. A master striking his servant dead at once is punished; if the servant lives a day or two, he is not. If a fight hurts a woman with child, the offender pays as the judges determine; if harm follows, the measure is life for life, eye for eye, and tooth for tooth. A master knocking out a servant's eye or tooth must free him. If an ox gores someone to death, the ox is stoned and not eaten, and the owner is quit, unless the ox was known to gore and was not kept in, when the owner dies too, or pays a ransom. The same covers son or daughter; a servant costs thirty shekels. The pit's owner pays for a fallen beast. One ox killing another's is settled by selling the live ox and sharing the price, unless it was known to gore.

A thief who steals and kills or sells an ox restores five oxen, four sheep for a sheep; if found alive, double. A thief breaking in at night may be struck without bloodguilt, not after sunrise. A beast grazing another's field, or fire spreading to grain, is repaid from the best of the offender's own. Goods in trust and things borrowed are covered: the keeper swears if the loss was unseen, pays double if he took it, and makes good what was borrowed and harmed. A man seducing an unbetrothed maid must endow her as wife, or pay the dowry. A sorceress shall not live; lying with a beast is death; sacrificing to any god but the LORD is destruction. The stranger must not be oppressed, for Israel was a stranger in Egypt. No widow or orphan may be afflicted, for the LORD hears their cry. No interest is taken from the poor, and a pledge is returned by sunset. God is not to be blasphemed, nor a ruler cursed. The firstborn belongs to the LORD; Israel must be holy, not eating torn flesh.

### Justice, Sabbaths, and feasts

No false report is raised, and no witness joins the wicked. A crowd is not followed to do evil, and judgment is not twisted for or against the poor. An enemy's straying ox is returned, his burdened ass helped. No bribe is taken, for a gift blinds the wise. The stranger is not oppressed, for Israel knows a stranger's heart.

The land rests in the seventh year: six years it is sown, and in the seventh it lies still for the poor to eat. The seventh day is a Sabbath, so ox, ass, servant's son, and stranger may rest. The names of other gods are not even spoken. Three feasts are kept yearly: unleavened bread in Abib, harvest of firstfruits, and ingathering at year's end. Three times a year all males appear before the LORD, and none comes empty.

An angel goes before them to guard the way. If they obey, he will be an enemy to their enemies, and the angel will cut off the Amorites, Hittites, Perizzites, Canaanites, Hivites, and Jebusites. They must not bow to their gods, but overthrow and break their images. The LORD will bless their bread and water, take away sickness, and let none be barren. He will send fear and hornets before them, but the land will not be emptied in one year; little by little they will grow and inherit it. Their borders run from the Red Sea to the Philistines' sea, and from the desert to the river. They must make no covenant with the inhabitants or their gods, for they would be a snare.

### The covenant ratified

The LORD calls Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders to come up and worship afar, but Moses alone shall come near. Moses tells the people all the LORD's words and judgments, and they answer with one voice that they will do them. Moses writes the words, rises early, and builds an altar at the mountain's foot with twelve pillars for the tribes. Young men offer burnt and peace offerings of oxen. Half the blood Moses puts in basins; half he sprinkles on the altar.

Then he reads the book of the covenant aloud, and they answer again: all the LORD has said we will do, and be obedient. Moses sprinkles the blood on the people and says, behold the blood of the covenant the LORD has made with you. Then Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and the seventy elders go up and see the God of Israel, with sapphire pavement beneath his feet, clear as the sky. On the nobles he lays no hand: they see God, and eat and drink.

The LORD tells Moses to come up and stay, and he will give him tablets of stone, with the law and commandments he has written, for Moses to teach. Moses rises with Joshua and goes up, leaving the elders to wait with Aaron and Hur. A cloud covers the mountain six days; on the seventh he calls Moses from it. The glory looks like devouring fire on the mountaintop below. Moses enters the cloud and stays on the mountain forty days and forty nights.

## Why This Context Matters

These chapters are the covenant itself, and everything in the passage gathers toward the mountain. The journey out of Egypt reaches Sinai in the third month, and the LORD sets out the terms in plain words: if Israel obeys his voice and keeps his covenant, they will be his treasured possession, a kingdom of priests and a holy nation. The people answer together, twice, that all the LORD has said they will do.

The Ten Words are spoken by God himself to the whole people, and the people, trembling at the thunder and the smoking mountain, ask Moses to speak to them instead. Then come the judgments, law after law for servants and injury and theft, for strangers and widows and the poor, for the Sabbath and the feasts, for the angel who goes before them and the nations they will meet. The passage ends with the covenant ratified in blood: half sprinkled on the altar, half on the people, the book read aloud, and the elders going up to see the God of Israel and eat and drink before him, while Moses alone enters the cloud for forty days and forty nights.
