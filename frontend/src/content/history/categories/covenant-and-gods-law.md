## Overview

In the third month after leaving Egypt, Israel comes into the wilderness of Sinai and camps in the desert before the mount, having departed from Rephidim.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] Moses goes up to God, and the LORD calls to him out of the mountain with an offer in conditional form: you have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I carried you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself; if you will obey my voice and keep my covenant, you shall be a peculiar treasure to me above all people, a kingdom of priests and a holy nation.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-bdb-lexicon] Moses lays the words before the elders, and all the people answer together that all the LORD has spoken they will do.

The people are sanctified for two days, washing their clothes to be ready against the third day, and bounds are set about the mount, with death for whoever touches it.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] On the third day there are thunders and lightnings, a thick cloud upon the mount, and the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud; the mount smokes as the smoke of a furnace and quakes greatly, and Moses speaks while God answers him by a voice.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-josephus-antiquities] The LORD comes down upon Sinai, calls Moses up, and warns the people and the priests not to break through to gaze.

Then God speaks the Ten Words to the whole people, beginning with the preamble that grounds the law in the exodus: I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] The words forbid other gods, any graven image or likeness with bowing down or serving, and the taking of the LORD's name in vain; they command remembering the Sabbath day, grounded in the six days of creation and God's rest; honouring father and mother, and they forbid killing, adultery, stealing, false witness, and coveting anything that is a neighbour's.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] The passage gives the words as the text gives them: three numberings are in use, and Scripture gives no distinct statement settling between them.[^source-keil-delitzsch-exodus]

The people, seeing the thunderings, the lightnings, the trumpet, and the smoking mountain, stand afar off and ask Moses to speak for them; Moses draws near to the thick darkness, and receives the altar law — an altar of earth, or of unhewn stone, with no steps.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] Then come the judgments of the Book of the Covenant: the Hebrew servant's six years and freedom in the seventh, the capital offences, the brawl and its compensation, the goring ox, theft and restitution, deposits and trust, and the protection of the stranger, the widow, and the fatherless; then the justice laws, the Sabbath year, the three feasts, and the Angel who goes before them.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv]

The covenant is ratified in blood. Moses writes all the words, builds an altar with twelve pillars for the tribes, and reads the book of the covenant aloud; the people answer again that all the LORD has said they will do and be obedient, and Moses sprinkles the blood on the altar and on the people: behold the blood of the covenant.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] Then Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and the seventy elders go up and see the God of Israel, with a paved work of sapphire stone under his feet, and eat and drink; Moses alone enters the cloud and stays in the mount forty days and forty nights.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] No year is given for the arrival at Sinai, and the date of the Exodus itself is debated; the category carries no independently established date.

## Ancient World

### The covenant offer and the theophany

The offer of Exodus 19 has a three-part shape: the recital of what God has done, the condition, and the promise.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] The recital looks back to Egypt; the promise, to a people set apart — a peculiar treasure, valued property chosen and taken by the LORD, first occurring here and always of Israel.[^source-bdb-lexicon] The "kingdom of priests" is glossed as priests and kings at once toward the nations, and the priests named at Sinai — before Aaron's ordination — are classed with chieftains exercising priestly functions, as Jethro did.[^source-bdb-lexicon][^source-bible-gateway-kjv]

The theophany is announced a day in advance, so the people may hear God speak with Moses and believe him for ever, and the mountain is fenced with death for touching it.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] The phenomena — thunder, lightning, thick cloud, trumpet, smoke, earthquake — are accompanied by a voice speaking words and a summoned mediator; the twentieth-century volcanic reading is therefore recorded, not adopted.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] Josephus retells the scene: feasting, abstinence, cloud and thunder, the people hearing the voice directly.[^source-josephus-antiquities] One commentary calls the trumpet miraculous, not human breath — a commentary reading.[^source-jfb-commentary]

### The Ten Words

The Decalogue opens with the preamble, a general preface, one commentator says, preparing the people's minds for obedience: the lawgiver names himself by his redemptive act.[^source-calvin-harmony-of-the-law][^source-bible-gateway-kjv] The first words forbid other gods "before me" — over against, allowing no companions — and any graven image, with iniquity visited to the third and fourth generation and mercy to thousands.[^source-keil-delitzsch-exodus][^source-calvin-harmony-of-the-law] The Sabbath command, "Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy", is grounded in the six days of creation and God's rest; "Remember" implies it was already known before Sinai.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-clarke-commentary][^source-bdb-lexicon]

The Decalogue exists in two versions, Exodus 20 and Deuteronomy 5, agreeing in substance, differing in wording — above all the Sabbath rationale, creation in Exodus and deliverance in Deuteronomy.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-keil-delitzsch-exodus] The older commentators judge the Exodus text the original, the Deuteronomic variants rhetorical and hortatory.[^source-keil-delitzsch-exodus] "Thou shalt not kill" uses a Hebrew verb for unlawful killing, never used for execution, holy war, or sacrifice.[^source-bdb-lexicon][^source-bible-hub-interlinear] The Septuagint orders the second table adultery, steal, murder, against the Hebrew order.[^source-nets-septuagint-exodus] Three numberings are in use: the Jewish, taking the preamble as the first word; the Philonian and Reformed, five and five; and the Augustinian, three and seven.[^source-keil-delitzsch-exodus][^source-josephus-antiquities][^source-philo-decalogue] The older commentators judge the question less important than often claimed; this page presents the ten words as the text gives them, without a numbered table privileging one tradition.

### The Book of the Covenant

"Now these are the judgments which thou shalt set before them." The Book of the Covenant is a series of *mišpāṭîm* (judicial decisions), mostly casuistic — "if... then..." — with apodictic "thou shalt not" insertions.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-bdb-lexicon] It runs from persons to property to protection: servants, homicide, injury, the goring ox, theft, deposits, the stranger, widow, and fatherless, usury, the pledge, the Sabbath year and feasts.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] The Hebrew servant serves six years and goes out free in the seventh; the servant who stays is brought before *ʾĕlōhîm*, his ear bored with an awl, and serves for ever. Whether *ʾĕlōhîm* means "judges" is unsettled; the Septuagint has "tribunal of God".[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-nets-septuagint-exodus]

The beaten-servant law is stated as written: a master beating his servant to death "shall be surely punished", but if the servant survives a day or two, "he shall not be punished: for he is his money".[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] The page neither softens the text nor endorses the rationale: the "he is his money" clause is the ancient rationale, not the page's endorsement, and whether the law limits or licenses a master's power is disputed. The pregnant-woman case gives the *lex talionis* formula, read by one commentary as a civil rule for magistrates, not private revenge; the Talmudic monetary reading is traditional, not directly verified, and the literal-versus-compensatory question stands as a debate.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-jfb-commentary] The goring-ox law has the ox stoned and the owner die or pay a ransom, with thirty shekels, the price of the slave, where a slave is killed; the Septuagint has "thirty didrachmas".[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-nets-septuagint-exodus]

### The ratification

The ratification of Exodus 24 is written, read aloud, accepted twice, sealed with blood.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] Moses tells the people all the words, they answer with one voice, and Moses writes, builds an altar with twelve pillars for the tribes, and has young men offer burnt and peace offerings; half the blood goes on the altar, half in basins, and after the reading he sprinkles the blood on the people.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-jfb-commentary] Then the elders ascend, see the God of Israel above a sapphire pavement, and eat and drink — a vision one commentary reads Christologically, as a faint form of Christ's humanity.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-jfb-commentary] The Septuagint has the pavement like lapis lazuli brick, and notes that not one of the chosen perished.[^source-nets-septuagint-exodus] Moses is then called to the mount for the tables of stone and stays forty days and forty nights, eating no bread and drinking no water; the six days of waiting are not counted within the forty.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-josephus-antiquities]

### Where and when: the mountain and the date

The mountain's modern identification is not established; this page presents both positions rather than choosing. The traditional site is Jebel Musa, identified since the fourth century, with neighbouring Es Suksafeh as the alternative; one commentary notes it is not certain on which they stood.[^source-jfb-commentary] The alternative places Sinai in north-west Arabia, on the mountain of God in Midian and "Mount Sinai in Arabia"; the exact location remains uncertain.[^source-biblicaltraining-mount-sinai]

No year is given for the arrival at Sinai. The Exodus date is disputed: the early date, about 1446 BCE from the 480 years of 1 Kings 6:1, against the late date, about 1270–1250 BCE from the store-city Raamses of Exodus 1:11; most scholars favour the late date, and neither is established to the required standard.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv] The one firm anchor is the Merneptah Stele of about 1208 BCE — "Israel is laid waste, its seed is not" — a terminus ante quem for Israel, not a date for Sinai.[^source-merneptah-stele] No direct archaeological evidence ties any inscription, sanctuary, or artefact from second-millennium Sinai to these chapters, and no Israelite settlement, camp, or burial ground has been found there. The setting is the Egyptian presence — the mines of Serabit el-Khadim and the Proto-Sinaitic inscriptions, the earliest alphabetic writing — context, not evidence.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv]

Two traditional datings are labelled as traditions, not as the biblical date. Jubilees 1, a Second Temple retelling, opens "in the third month, on the sixteenth day of the month" — a day the Exodus text does not give — with the angel of the presence dictating from the heavenly tablets.[^source-charles-jubilees] The law is also traditionally dated to the fiftieth day after the Passover; Augustine draws the parallel with the Spirit descending on the fiftieth day, the Finger of God.[^source-jfb-commentary][^source-augustine-city-of-god]

### The treaty form and Hammurabi's Code

Modern scholarship compares the covenant with the Hittite suzerainty treaties of the fourteenth and thirteenth centuries BCE — as a comparison, not as evidence of a date. The six-element form — preamble, historical prologue, stipulations, document clause, witnesses, blessings and curses — partially maps onto the passage: the form is clearest in Exodus 20, where the witnesses element has no counterpart.[^source-lucas-covenant-treaty-prophecy][^source-bible-odyssey-covenant] The four-part version comes from the directly read source.[^source-bible-odyssey-covenant] Two qualifications: later scholars found closer parallels for Deuteronomy in first-millennium Neo-Assyrian oaths, so the form is not a unique second-millennium fingerprint; and the historical prologue, typical of second-millennium treaties but rare in first-millennium ones, is the strongest argument for an early setting — about the form, not a proof of the date.

The same care applies to the Code of Hammurabi, the closest ancient Near Eastern parallel to the Book of the Covenant. The parallels are real: *lex talionis*, the pregnant woman, the goring ox, debt servitude, the brawl's compensation, multiple restitution.[^source-hammurabi-code] So are the differences: no grading by class, no vicarious punishment, freedom rather than money for the injured slave, and protections — stranger, widow, orphan, pledge returned at sunset — with no Hammurabi parallel.[^source-hammurabi-code][^source-bible-gateway-kjv] One commentary's summary stands: most of these laws were primitive usages, founded on natural equity, incorporated with improvements in the Mosaic code.[^source-jfb-commentary] The comparison is not a derivation, and it proves no date.

### Later readings

The Sinai covenant is conditional — "if ye will obey... then" — unlike the unconditional covenants; the distinction is a classification, not a claim about the wording.[^source-bible-odyssey-covenant][^source-bdb-lexicon] Philo, the earliest philosophical interpreter of the law, gives four reasons the law was given in the desert: impious cities; the soul cleansed apart from the multitude; laws prepared before settlement; miraculous provision proving the laws God's, not men's.[^source-philo-decalogue] The same author presents Moses as king, lawgiver, high priest, and prophet in one man.[^source-philo-life-of-moses] Josephus gives the fullest ancient retelling after Exodus itself: five commandments upon each table, and forty days of fasting.[^source-josephus-antiquities] The identity of the Angel of Exodus 23:20–23 — "my name is in him" — is unsettled; it is not identified here with Michael or with the Angel of the LORD.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv]

## Why This Context Matters

These chapters are the covenant itself, and everything in the passage gathers toward the mountain. Redemption precedes command: the people are brought to God first, and only then given the law — the same order the treaty form encodes, with the historical prologue of deliverance before the stipulations.[^source-bible-gateway-kjv][^source-bible-odyssey-covenant] The Ten Words are spoken by God himself to the whole people, and the people, trembling at the thunder and the smoking mountain, ask Moses to speak to them instead; the law is then mediated, written, read aloud, and sealed in blood, with the elders going up to see the God of Israel and eat and drink before him.

The Book of the Covenant shows what the covenant meant in daily life: servants freed in the seventh year, injury answered by measure, theft by restitution, and the stranger, the widow, and the fatherless protected by laws that have no parallel in the neighbouring codes. The New Testament takes up the same words — the kingdom of priests, the blood of the covenant, the mount that burned with fire set against the mountain that may be approached — reading Sinai as the covenant fulfilled and surpassed. What the mountain leaves unresolved — its place, its year — is left unresolved here too, so that the reader meets the text as it stands: a covenant offered, accepted, written, and ratified.
