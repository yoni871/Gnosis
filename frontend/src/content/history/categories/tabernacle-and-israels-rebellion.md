## Overview

This category covers Exodus 25–34, the long middle stretch of the Sinai story. It opens on the mountain, where Moses receives detailed instructions for a dwelling place, and it closes with the people below the mountain breaking faith and the relationship being put back together. The first half is given over entirely to plans: chapter 25 asks the people to bring offerings of gold, silver, bronze, blue, purple and scarlet yarn, fine linen, goat hair, tanned ram skins, acacia wood, oil, spices, and precious stones, and then describes the ark, the table for the bread, and the lampstand. Chapter 26 describes the tent itself, its curtains, frames, and coverings; chapter 27 adds the bronze altar, the courtyard, and the oil for the lamp. Chapters 28 and 29 turn to the priests: the garments Aaron is to wear, and the week-long ceremony by which Aaron and his sons are set apart for service. Chapter 30 adds the altar of incense, the census payment, the bronze basin, the anointing oil, and the incense. Chapter 31 names the craftsmen who will do the work, Bezalel and Oholiab, and closes the mountain instructions with the Sabbath.

Then the story breaks. While Moses is still on the mountain, the people below grow impatient, gather their gold, and make a calf. Chapter 32 tells how Aaron fashions it, how the people worship it, how Moses comes down with the two tablets and breaks them, how the Levites act, and how Moses pleads for the people. Chapter 33 follows with the tent of meeting set up outside the camp, the pillar of cloud, and Moses asking to see God's glory. Chapter 34 closes the category: new tablets are cut, the covenant is renewed, and Moses comes down from the mountain with his face shining, so that he wears a veil when he speaks to the people. The category thus moves from pattern to breach to renewal: the dwelling is described, the covenant is broken, and the covenant is made again.

## Ancient World

### The offerings and the pattern (chapter 25)

The chapter opens with a call for a freewill offering from everyone whose heart prompts them to give. The list is gold, silver, and bronze; yarn and linen; goat hair; skins; wood; oil; spices; and gems. A sanctuary is to be made from these, so that God may dwell among them, according to the pattern shown to Moses on the mountain. First comes the ark, a gold-overlaid chest with rings and poles, topped by the atonement cover with two cherubim, wings spread, faces turned toward each other. The tablets go inside, and God says he will meet Moses there. Next the table, overlaid with gold, with vessels of pure gold and the bread of the Presence set on it always; then the lampstand, one piece of hammered gold, with a shaft, six branches, and seven lamps.

### The tent itself (chapter 26)

Chapter 26 describes the tabernacle as a tent of curtains. The inner layer is ten curtains of fine linen and coloured yarn, worked with cherubim, joined in two sets of five and fastened with fifty gold clasps. Over this goes a layer of eleven goat-hair curtains, joined with bronze clasps, with coverings of skins and leather above them. The framework is upright boards of acacia wood overlaid with gold, standing in silver bases, with bars running across them. A veil of coloured yarn and linen, worked with cherubim, hangs on four pillars; behind it goes the ark, and the veil separates the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place. The table stands on the north side outside the veil, the lampstand on the south side, and a worked screen hangs at the entrance on five pillars.

### The altar, the courtyard, and the lamp (chapter 27)

The chapter moves outside the tent to the bronze altar for burnt offerings: a square altar of acacia wood overlaid with bronze, with horns at its corners, a bronze grating, rings, and poles, and utensils all of bronze. Around the tent is a courtyard, enclosed by curtains of fine linen hung on pillars with silver hooks and bands and bronze bases. Its entrance, on the east side, is a screen of coloured yarn and fine linen, and all the pegs are bronze. The chapter ends with the oil for the light: the Israelites bring clear oil of pressed olives, so that the lamps burn continually from evening until morning, tended by Aaron and his sons.

### The priestly garments (chapter 28)

Chapter 28 describes the garments for Aaron and his sons. The ephod is made of gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine linen. On the shoulders sit two onyx stones, engraved with the names of the sons of Israel, set in gold filigree, as stones of remembrance. The breastpiece is a square pouch, folded double, with four rows of three stones, each engraved with one tribe's name. It is fastened to the ephod with gold rings and blue cords, so Aaron bears the names over his heart, and the Urim and Thummim go inside it. The robe of the ephod is all of blue, with pomegranates and bells of gold alternating around its hem. A plate of pure gold on the turban is engraved "Holy to the LORD"; there are tunics, sashes, and caps for Aaron's sons.

### The consecration of Aaron and his sons (chapter 29)

Chapter 29 lays out the ceremony by which Aaron and his sons are consecrated. They are brought to the entrance, washed, and dressed: Aaron in the tunic, robe, ephod, breastpiece, and turban, and his sons in their tunics, sashes, and caps. A young bull is offered as a sin offering, and two rams are brought: one wholly burned as a burnt offering, and the other, the ram of ordination, whose blood is put on the ear, thumb, and toe of Aaron and his sons, with portions waved as a wave offering before being burned. The offerings are repeated for seven days, and the breast and thigh belong to Aaron and his sons. Daily offerings are fixed: two lambs every day, morning and twilight. The chapter closes with the promise that God will dwell among them.

### The altar of incense, the census, and the basin (chapter 30)

Chapter 30 begins with a second altar, the altar of incense, of acacia wood overlaid with pure gold. It stands before the veil, and Aaron burns fragrant incense on it morning and evening, with atonement made on its horns once a year. The chapter then turns to the census: each person counted gives a half shekel to the LORD, the rich no more and the poor no less, as a ransom for their lives. Next comes the bronze basin, placed between the tent and the altar and filled with water, so that Aaron and his sons wash their hands and feet before they serve. The chapter gives the recipes for the anointing oil and for the incense; both are forbidden for ordinary use.

### The craftsmen and the Sabbath (chapter 31)

Chapter 31 names the men who will make all that has been described. Bezalel son of Uri, of Judah, is called by name and filled with the Spirit of God. Oholiab son of Ahisamak, of Dan, is appointed with him, and skill is given to all the workers. The chapter then returns to the Sabbath: the Israelites are to keep the Sabbaths as a sign between God and them, a sign that he makes them holy. Work is done for six days, and the seventh day is a Sabbath of rest, holy to the LORD, a lasting covenant, because in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested. The chapter closes with Moses receiving the two tablets of the covenant law.

### The golden calf (chapter 32)

Chapter 32 opens with the people seeing that Moses is long in coming down from the mountain. They gather around Aaron and ask him to make gods to go before them. Aaron collects their gold earrings and fashions a molten calf, and the people say, these are your gods, Israel, who brought you up out of Egypt. Aaron builds an altar and announces a festival, and the people offer sacrifices and revel. On the mountain, God tells Moses to go down, because the people have become corrupt. Moses pleads, asking God to remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, and the text says God relented. Moses comes down with the two tablets, sees the calf and the dancing, and breaks the tablets. He burns the calf. The Levites go through the camp with their swords, and about three thousand fall. The next day Moses goes up again to plead for the people. The chapter ends with the LORD striking the people with a plague because of the calf.

### The tent of meeting and the journey (chapter 33)

Chapter 33 opens with the command to leave: go up to the land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. But God says he will not go with them, and the people mourn and put off their ornaments. Moses pitches a tent outside the camp and calls it the tent of meeting; when Moses enters, the pillar of cloud comes down and stands at the entrance while the LORD speaks with him. The LORD speaks to Moses face to face. Moses asks to know God's ways, and God answers that his Presence will go with them; Moses answers that if it does not go, they should not be sent up. Moses asks, show me your glory, and God answers that he will cause all his goodness to pass before Moses, but no one may see his face and live, so he puts Moses in a cleft in the rock and lets him see his back.

### The new tablets and the covenant (chapter 34)

Chapter 34 opens with the command to cut two stone tablets like the first ones, and God says he will write on them the words of the first tablets. Moses goes up Mount Sinai alone in the morning. The LORD comes down in the cloud and proclaims his name, the compassionate and gracious God, slow to anger, abounding in love and faithfulness. Moses bows down and asks the Lord to go with them and forgive them. God makes a covenant: he will do wonders never before done, and they are not to covenant with the people of the land or worship their gods. The chapter sets out the appointed festivals and the consecration of the firstborn. Moses stays on the mountain forty days and forty nights, and writes the words of the covenant, the Ten Commandments, on the tablets. When he comes down, his face is radiant, and the people are afraid to come near. He gives them the commands, then puts a veil over his face.
## Why This Context Matters

This category holds together the two halves of the Sinai story: the dwelling place and the breach. The first half gives the people a place where God dwells among them, a tent with an ark, a table, a lampstand, altars, a basin, and priests in garments made for glory and beauty. The instructions are exact, repeated, and tied to a pattern shown on the mountain, and the people are asked to give freely of what they have toward it. The second half shows the same people, while Moses is still on the mountain, turning to a calf of gold and saying that it brought them out of Egypt.

The calf is not the end of the story. Moses pleads, the tablets are broken and then remade, the covenant is spoken again, and the journey is set to continue with God's Presence going along. The tent of meeting outside the camp, the pillar of cloud at its entrance, and Moses speaking with God face to face carry the relationship through the breach, and the shining face with which Moses comes down the mountain closes the category on a visible sign of that renewed meeting. What follows in the next category is the building of the very tent that the first half of this one describes, carried out by the people whose giving and whose failure are both told here.
